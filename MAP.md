# Where IER Lives

This guide maps the public sources, publications, and access routes for
Informational Experiential Realism (IER), by Michael Lehotay. It is navigation
and publication framing, with no independent theoretical authority.

The seed book is *Informational Experiential Realism: The Identity and Structure
of Experience*. Its [PhilPapers record](https://philpapers.org/rec/LEHIER)
provides the free-download route also reached through PhilPeople. The Amazon
and library routes above concern that published book, rather than every later
revision of its sources.

## Inside the Public Repository

The three directories have different purposes:

- [IER/](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/tree/main/IER) contains the theory
  source articles and their orientation material. The
  [Specification](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-specification.md)
  fixes normative commitments; explicitly scoped canonical definition owners
  develop their respective relations under those commitments. Check each
  document's declared status and scope: drafts, comparisons, and summaries do
  not gain defining authority simply by appearing in this directory.
- [apps/](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/tree/main/apps) contains exploratory
  applications, studies, models, and reading projects. Their results and
  interpretations are bounded to their stated assumptions and evidence. They
  do not redefine universal IER commitments or establish experience in a system
  merely by applying IER vocabulary.
- [examples/](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/tree/main/examples) contains
  non-canonical illustrations, analogies, and cases. They help readers explore
  distinctions; they supply no independent definition or proof of the theory.

The public repository is a selected export of the maintained source project.
Its contents are not a complete inventory of every development document or of
the theory's core working scope. Public selection, canonical authority, review
status, and publication readiness are separate questions.

## Books, Papers, and the Compendium

The seed book records the compact first published edition. The working
papers develop particular arguments for scholarly discussion:

- [The Identification Problem for Physicalist Theories of Experience](https://philpapers.org/rec/LEHTIP-2).
- [Informational Experiential Realism: Experience as a Continuing Physical Operation](https://philpapers.org/rec/LEHIER-2).
- [Constraint, Closure, and the Missing Subject](https://philpapers.org/rec/LEHCCA-4).

Record titles can differ across revisions. A working-paper listing is not a
claim of peer review or journal publication.

The compendium is a selected collection of canonical articles, summaries,
and publication framing. It gives readers a book-shaped route through its
declared sources. Selection and chapter order grant no additional theoretical
authority. Its current Working Canon Edition remains separate from readiness
for standalone public distribution.

The compendium is the development basis for a planned second edition of the
seed book. Publication details will be announced when the edition is ready.
The second edition is not yet available; the current working selection is not
its final contents.

## Choosing a Starting Point

- For an overview: begin with the
  [Model Overview](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-model-overview.md),
  then the Specification and
  [Theory](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-theory.md).
- For a guided learning route: use the PhilPeople teaching materials or the
  [guided annotated reading list](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-bibliography.md).
- For the scholarly argument: use the working-paper records above.
- For a stable book edition: download the seed book, buy it, or consult the
  library record. Cite the edition actually read.
- For a source passage: record the file and a Git commit or tag, rather than
  relying only on a moving `main` link. The repository's
  [citation file](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/CITATION.cff) supplies
  citation metadata.

When versions differ, distinguish what an earlier edition said from what a
current source says. For current theory, the Specification and relevant
canonical definition owners control; a later upload, publisher description,
example, or paper does not override them by its date or venue.

## Other Discovery Routes and Coverage

PhilPapers supplies scholarly records linked from PhilPeople; related
[PhilArchive discovery](https://philarchive.org/s/Informational%20Experiential%20Realism)
can also lead to IER material. These routes should not be counted as separate
versions of the theory without checking the particular record and file.
For similarly named but unrelated theories, consult the
[naming and disambiguation guide](NAMING.md).

The publisher also links the author's
[ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0007-3624-5148) and
[personal site](https://mlehotay.github.io/). These identify the author and help
readers find the project; they are not additional canonical partitions.

This inventory was assembled on 18 October 2026 from the repository's publication
records, the linked public pages, and the author's supplied access routes. The
Amazon and WorldCat links are author-supplied; their live listings and holdings
were not independently inspected for this guide. Hosted PDFs were not compared
byte for byte with local builds. Other mirrors, retailers, and library records
may exist. Update this shared guide when a new edition or distribution route
is announced.
