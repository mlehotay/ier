# Office Translation

## When a Visible Capability Becomes an Expected Contribution

## Status

This is a constructed, non-canonical case. It illustrates one possible change
in workplace expectations; it makes no claim about a particular worker's
experience, duties or employment rights. It supplies no criterion for
experiential identity or constitutive membership.

## The situation

Imagine a worker who speaks a second language but has not mentioned that skill
at work. Translation requests go to an outside service or another department.
The worker's ordinary tasks do not include translation.

One afternoon, the worker helps a visitor who speaks that language. Colleagues
notice. The next request comes directly to the worker: “Could you translate
this? It will only take a minute.”

Nothing about the worker's fluency changed that afternoon. What changed was
other people's knowledge of it, and the route by which work reached them.

## From a request to an expectation

Suppose the worker helps several times. Colleagues begin to send documents
without first asking whether translation fits the worker's schedule. A request
that once needed an outside arrangement now has an apparently easy destination.

The change can be described at several levels:

| Before the skill was visible | After repeated requests |
| --- | --- |
| Colleagues seek translation elsewhere. | They approach the worker first. |
| Translation is absent from the worker's usual workload. | It competes with existing tasks. |
| Declining requires little explanation. | Colleagues may treat refusal as unexpected. |
| Availability is not assumed. | Past help can become a reason to expect more help. |

This is one possible development, not an inevitable result of revealing a skill.
A manager might instead arrange paid translation time, preserve the outside
service, or make clear that occasional help creates no continuing expectation.

## What has changed?

The worker was already capable of translating. Revealing the skill made that
capability available to other people's planning. Repeated use can then stabilize
a routing habit: when translation is needed, ask this person.

For the worker, each request may now require translating, negotiating time,
explaining a refusal or redirecting the task. The visible skill can therefore
change the practical costs of continuing existing work even without a formal
job change. Increased cost does not mean that refusal has become physically
impossible or that an irreversible foreclosure has occurred.

This distinguishes having a capability, being approached to use it, and
being expected to remain available. None entails the others by itself.
Nor does the convenience of assigning work establish an obligation to accept it.

## Reading the case through IER

The case makes several ordinary relations easier to distinguish:

- Interpretation: colleagues now recognize the worker as someone who can help.
- Affordances: approaching this worker becomes an available practical route.
- Bias: repeated success can favor that route over alternatives.
- Participation: the worker may become involved in additional activity.
- External coupling: requests and reactions from others can affect the
  worker's planning and subsequent responses.

These are descriptive uses within a hypothetical situation. The account does
not establish technical binding, owned resolution, intrinsic closure or a shared
workplace subject. Assignment of a task is not transfer of experiential ownership.
Whether the worker feels willing, burdened, valued or exploited remains a
separate question; the routing pattern alone cannot decide it.

## Variations that matter

Compare a single favor with a standing expectation. Compare volunteered help
with requests that displace already assigned work. Compare an explicit agreement
about availability with a pattern that develops without discussion.

The same language skill can remain unchanged across all these cases while its
role in the situation differs. That is the useful contrast: capability visibility
can change how activity is organized, without itself changing capability or
establishing what anyone owes.
