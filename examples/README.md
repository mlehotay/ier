# Examples

Illustrating Informational Experiential Realism

## Purpose

The `examples/` directory contains short, self-contained examples that illustrate concepts from Informational Experiential Realism (IER).

Unlike the canonical documents in `IER/`, examples are intended to build intuition rather than introduce theory.

Unlike the larger projects in `apps/`, examples focus on a single concept, phenomenon, or worked case.

Examples introduce no new theoretical commitments and possess no normative authority. Their purpose is explanatory only.


## Relationship to the Repository

The repository is organized into complementary layers.

* `IER/` contains the canonical theory.
* `examples/` contains concise illustrations of that theory.
* `apps/` contains larger exploratory applications of the theory.

Examples help bridge the gap between abstract theoretical concepts and familiar experiences.


## Current Examples

### `analogies/`

Ordinary Experiences Viewed Through IER

Analogies demonstrate how familiar situations and physical systems can illuminate structural concepts developed within IER.

Rather than presenting abstract definitions, each example begins with an ordinary experience and examines the underlying organizational principles involved.

Current analogies include:

* black-snake - rapid interpretation, expectation, and experiential organization under uncertainty.
* drum-and-string - physical interaction and emergent organizational constraint.
* garden-hose - continuity, propagation, and constraint through an intuitive physical system.
* lonely-hearts-runners - coordination, participation, and shared experiential organization.

These examples are intended as intuition pumps rather than formal arguments.
Larger exploratory applications live under `apps/`.


## Design Philosophy

Good examples should:

* illustrate one or a small number of IER concepts,
* remain accessible without specialized background,
* avoid introducing new theoretical claims,
* help readers develop intuition for the underlying framework.

Examples are deliberately brief and focused.

Readers seeking the full theoretical account should consult the canonical documents in `IER/`.


## Guiding Principle

Examples explain how to think with Informational Experiential Realism.

They are illustrations of the theory, not extensions of it.
