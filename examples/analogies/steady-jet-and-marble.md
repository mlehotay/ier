# The Steady Jet and the Marble

## A Physical Analogy for Regime-Level Load-Bearing Through Material Turnover

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.5)**\
**Date:** 22 September 2026\
*Tier-2 · Explanatory · Illustrative · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This article develops one physical analogy: a vertically directed water jet can remain a continuous load-bearing formation even though the water composing it is continuously replaced.

It introduces no new IER primitive, criterion, experiential mode, dynamical operator, threshold, measurement, or implementation. A jet is not claimed to be experiential. Mechanical load-bearing is not intrinsic constraint. Material turnover does not establish UEF persistence, and causal dependence does not by itself establish participation, ownership, closure, or subject boundary.

The example is intended to clarify the difference among:

- changing material throughput;
- the motion of that material;
- a persisting organized regime;
- a load borne at the regime level; and
- the larger apparatus that enables the regime to continue.

Framework definitions belong to the [IER précis](../../IER/IER-precis.md). The [Garden Hose](garden-hose.md) illustrates changing boundary conditions and flow patterns. The [Billowed Sail](billowed-sail.md) illustrates continuing load-bearing through a connected deformable substrate. The present example isolates a different point: *the continuing bearer can persist while its immediate material participants pass through it.*

## The Central Image

Imagine a fountain nozzle directing a narrow jet of water vertically upward. Place a glass marble above it. If the jet is sufficiently strong and appropriately shaped, its upward action can oppose the marble's weight and keep the marble suspended.

The image is idealized. The required flow rate, stability, and restoring behavior depend on the marble, the nozzle, and the actual fluid dynamics. None of those engineering details changes the structural point.

Water continuously enters the bottom of the jet, travels upward, encounters or passes around the marble, and leaves. No parcel remains beneath the marble for long. Nevertheless, the jet continues.

The marble is not supported by the metal nozzle touching it. It is not supported by a permanent column made of the same water. It is supported by the dynamically maintained jet.

> The water changes. The jet persists. The jet bears the load.

## Water, Flow, Jet, and Profile

The vocabulary matters because several different physical facts are easy to collapse into the word *water*.

| Term | Meaning in the example |
| --- | --- |
| **Water** | the material passing through the formation |
| **Flow** | the motion and transport of that material |
| **Flow field** | the spatial organization of velocity, pressure, and related quantities |
| **Jet** | the persisting, bounded formation produced by the organized flow |
| **Free-surface profile** | the visible outline of the jet where water meets the surrounding air |
| **Steady jet** | a jet whose organized form remains sufficiently stable through time despite material throughput |

The jet resembles a standing wave only in one limited respect: a spatial form persists while local physical activity continues. It is not literally a standing wave. The useful term is *steady jet*, or, when emphasizing its organization, *steady flow regime*.

Calling the jet steady does not mean that every molecule is still or that every instantaneous detail is invariant. It means that the organized formation remains stable enough to count as the same continuing jet while water traverses it.

## The Jet Is the Bearer

At the scale of individual interactions, parcels of water exchange momentum with the marble. Those local exchanges physically realize the upward force. But no particular parcel bears the marble's continuing load for the duration of the suspension.

The persisting bearer is the jet.

This does not add a second entity above the water. The jet is entirely physically instantiated by moving water under the boundary conditions established by the fountain apparatus. Yet the load-bearing fact belongs to the organized formation, not to an enduring inventory of constituent parcels.

The distinction is the same one used whenever a temporally extended organization performs a continuing physical role through changing local participants. Constituent events realize the operation. The organized regime is the continuing bearer of that operation.

> Bearer continuity does not require the same material to remain locally present.

This claim is bounded. It does not mean that any amount of constituent replacement preserves any regime. The relevant organization, coupling, and temporal continuity must actually persist. A disrupted, dispersed, or redirected flow may no longer instantiate the same jet at all.

## Driver, Throughput, Bearer, Load, and Support

Five roles should remain distinct:

| Role | Fountain system | Structural significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Driver** | pressure head or pump-generated pressure | supplies the continuing energetic conditions for upward flow |
| **Throughput** | successive parcels of water | materially instantiate the jet while entering and leaving it |
| **Bearer** | the steady jet as an organized formation | continuously supports the marble through distributed momentum transfer |
| **Load** | the marble's weight and its reaction on the jet | must be continuously borne for suspension to persist |
| **Support** | nozzle, plumbing, reservoir, pump, mounting, and power source | establishes and maintains the conditions under which the jet exists |

The driver is not the bearer. The pump supplies pressure, but the marble is locally suspended by the jet.

The throughput is not identical to the continuing bearer. Water parcels instantiate the jet, but they enter and leave while the jet persists.

The load is not the bearer. The marble's weight is what the jet must oppose, not what the jet is.

The support apparatus is not automatically part of the load-bearing formation. The nozzle shapes the jet and the pump enables it, but neither occupies the marble's local support relation in the same way as the jet.

These distinctions are mechanical, not experiential. They do not establish the boundary of a UEF.

## Load Makes Regime-Level Reality Visible

Without the marble, the jet may look like nothing more than water moving upward. The marble makes another fact visible: the persisting formation has a continuing capacity that no enduring parcel possesses.

The load changes the jet. Water slows, spreads, redirects, or separates around the marble. Pressure and momentum relations differ from those of the unloaded jet. The jet is therefore not merely an interchangeable background carrying an unaffected object. It is a load-bearing organization whose present dynamics include the marble.

The marble also changes what the jet can sustain. A heavier marble, weaker flow, altered nozzle, or large disturbance may eliminate the suspended configuration. Load-bearing is therefore relational and capacity-limited. It exists only within a range of coupled conditions.

This is not a claim that the jet represents the marble or computes its weight. The load is present as a physical demand on the jet's continuation. The jet either continues to sustain the coupled configuration or it does not.

## Persistence Through Participation Turnover

Successive parcels of water participate in the jet for limited intervals. Each parcel enters an already organized flow, contributes to the jet's current operation, and exits.

The participants therefore turn over while the regime continues.

This offers a narrow intuition for distinguishing:

- the identity of a regime;
- the identity of its present material participants; and
- the history of participation in that regime.

The three are not interchangeable. The jet need not cease merely because a parcel leaves. Nor does a newly arriving parcel create a new jet merely by entering. What matters for the example is continuity of the organized load-bearing formation.

IER's constitutive-membership relation is stronger. A candidate process must
make a constitutive contribution, bear the same global restriction, be jointly
resolved rather than independently completed across an interface, undergo
diachronic uptake, and share the operation's typing across restriction,
variables, grain, scale, regime, intervention family, and interval. Passing
through a causally organized flow is not enough. The water-parcel analogy
therefore illustrates turnover within a continuing organization without
supplying a test for experiential participation.

## A Regime, Not a Static Shape

The jet is not identical to one frozen outline. Its diameter may fluctuate, its surface may ripple, and the marble may bob or rotate. The load-bearing operation can persist across these changes.

The analogy's relevant continuity is dynamical:

- upward flow continues;
- momentum continues to be transferred;
- the marble remains within the supported region;
- deviations remain bounded or are corrected; and
- the overall formation remains connected through time.

The regime is therefore not a static geometrical object. It is a stable pattern of lawful evolution.

If the marble briefly rises and settles, that may be a fluctuation within the continuing operation. If the pressure changes while suspension remains intact, the same regime may alter quantitatively. If the jet breaks apart or the marble falls, the load-bearing regime has ended even if water continues to leave the nozzle.

The analogy does not determine whether such changes correspond to IER excursions, transitions, collapse, or cessation. It only shows why persistence cannot be reduced to visual immobility or fixed constituent membership.

It also does not establish IER's stage-indexed diachronic relation
`Adj_dia(a,b)`, which additionally requires continuous closure, operational
successor production, typed consequence inheritance, operation-mediated
change, and unique two-sided continuation. The jet supplies an intuition for
turnover, not a persistence verdict.

## Boundary and Enabling Conditions

The jet cannot continue without enabling conditions. It depends on a nozzle, pressure source, water supply, and gravitational and environmental relations. Turning off the pump causes the jet to disappear and the marble to fall.

But counterfactual necessity is not sufficient for constitutive membership.

The power source may be necessary for the pump. The pump may be necessary for the pressure. The nozzle may be necessary for the jet. The jet may be necessary for the suspension. It does not follow that every causally necessary item belongs to one undifferentiated system boundary or performs the same role.

This is particularly important for IER. Functional indispensability, energy
supply, causal influence, and visible load-bearing do not by themselves
establish participation in a UEF. Constitutive membership requires all five
conditions stated above within an accepted ownership scope; contribution and
bearing alone are not enough.

The fountain therefore helps separate two questions:

1. What conditions must exist for the phenomenon to continue?
2. Which processes constitute the continuing bearer at the scale under examination?

The first question identifies enabling dependence. The second concerns regime organization. They may yield different boundaries.

## Why the Jet Is Not a UEF Model

The steady jet is an open, externally maintained dissipative formation. Water enters and leaves. Energy and disturbance can be exported. The pump and reservoir maintain the operating conditions. Local incompatibilities can often be absorbed, redirected, or carried away by the outgoing flow.

For those reasons, the example does not establish:

- intrinsic closure;
- globally non-factorizable admissible futures;
- non-externalizable constraint;
- owned collapse;
- sedimentation of resolved constraint;
- experiential continuity; or
- subjecthood.

Indeed, its openness is useful. The jet demonstrates that regime persistence, integrated operation, material turnover, and load-bearing can all occur without satisfying IER's identity conditions. Those features cannot therefore serve as independent diagnostics of experience.

The analogy clarifies how a regime can be physically real as a bearer. It does not show that every regime-level bearer is experiential.

## Correspondence and Limits

| Jet relation | Bounded IER intuition | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water parcels enter and leave | Present participants need not exhaust regime identity | Does not establish experiential participation or arbitrary replaceability |
| The steady jet persists | A regime can remain continuous through material throughput | Does not establish UEF continuity |
| The jet bears the marble | A continuing organization can be the relevant bearer of a load | Mechanical load is not intrinsic constraint |
| The marble deforms the jet | What is borne can reorganize the bearer's present dynamics | Not ownership, representation, or cognition |
| Pump and nozzle enable the jet | Enabling conditions can be causally indispensable | Indispensability does not establish constitutive membership |
| The open flow carries water and disturbance away | A stable regime may possess extensive external resolution pathways | Stability and integration do not imply intrinsic closure |
| Loss of pressure ends suspension | Regime existence depends on continuing physical organization | Not a model of collapse or experiential cessation |

The correspondence is deliberately narrow. The fountain is not a brain model, the jet is not a subject, the marble is not an inner object, and the pump is not an unconscious controller. No component-to-component mapping of that kind is intended.

## Relation to the Garden Hose and Billowed Sail

The three analogies isolate different facts.

The Garden Hose shows how changing boundary conditions produce different stable flow patterns, transitions, excursions, and within-pattern variation.

The Billowed Sail shows how a connected material surface bears changing load while deforming reversibly under continuing drive.

The Steady Jet and the Marble combines neither example wholesale. Its specific contribution is narrower:

> A persisting regime can bear a continuous load even though the material immediately realizing that bearing is continuously replaced.

The jet is therefore useful wherever discussion is slipping among material, motion, form, bearer, and support as though those were interchangeable.

## Compression

A vertical water jet can support a marble without any parcel of water remaining beneath it. Successive parcels enter, transfer momentum, and leave, while the organized jet continues to bear the marble's load. Water is the material throughput; flow is its motion; the jet is the persisting formation; the marble supplies the load; and the fountain apparatus maintains the enabling conditions.

The jet is not an entity separate from moving water, but neither is it reducible to one enduring collection of water parcels. It is a physically instantiated regime whose continuity consists in organized operation through time.

The example does not establish intrinsic closure, experiential participation, ownership, or UEF identity. It establishes one bounded point:

> The bearer can persist because the material changes in an organized way, rather than because the same material remains in place.
