# Duration Example Collection

## Status

Status: reference  
Authority: non-canonical

This collection is an exploratory application of Informational Experiential
Realism (IER) to a 2026 neuroscience study of visual-duration categorization.
It is organized as a worked identification-gap analysis: the documents ask
what the experiment measured, what its models added, what its authors inferred,
and what would still be required to relate those findings to experienced
duration.

The collection does not present the study as evidence for a cortical IER
architecture. It introduces no new IER ontology, criterion of experience,
formal operator, mechanism, or diagnostic.

## Central Distinction

The source study contains four objects that must not be collapsed merely
because each can be described using the word *duration*.

| Object | Role in the study | Evidential status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical stimulus duration | Display time of the visual stimulus | Experimentally controlled |
| Modeled preferred duration | Peak of a fitted Gaussian response function at a cortical vertex | Model-dependent parameter estimated from condition-level BOLD responses |
| Point of subjective equality (PSE) | Physical duration at the 50% boundary between shorter and longer reports | Behavioral criterion estimated from a psychometric function |
| Experienced duration | Phenomenal temporal organization that IER seeks to explain | Present in the task but not independently characterized beyond the reports used to estimate the PSE |

Centanino, Fortunato, and Bueti established relations among the first three.
The study did not independently characterize the structure of the fourth. This
is the collection's governing evidential boundary.

## Files and Division of Labor

### [Neuroscience Primer](neuroscience-primer.md)

A source-facing methodological companion. It explains:

- the experimental task and delayed response cue;
- the offset-locked fMRI analysis;
- the fitted duration-response model;
- the regional findings, PSE analysis, and correlation clusters;
- the difference between observed, estimated, fitted, and interpreted claims;
- and the restricted meanings of terms such as *tuning*, *categorical*,
  *subjective*, *boundary population*, and *hierarchy*.

The primer does not map cortical regions onto IER operations. Read it first if
the source paper's methods or neuroscience vocabulary are unfamiliar.

### [Four Durations, Not One](IER-four-durations-not-one.md)

The primary interpretive article. It:

- separates physical duration, modeled cortical preference, behavioral PSE,
  and experienced duration;
- reconstructs the task as a structured situation without turning task
  components into IER anatomical equivalents;
- distinguishes neural timing and duration-discrimination mechanisms from the
  constitution of temporal phenomenology;
- explains why the existing study remains on one side of the identification
  bridge;
- withdraws the earlier cortex-to-IER mappings;
- and proposes experimental separations, bridge hypotheses, and failure
  conditions for future work.

The article is coordinated with the non-canonical paper *The Identification
Problem for Physicalist Theories of Experience*. That paper uses the duration
case as a worked demonstration of empirical underdetermination, not as a
positive psychophysical identity candidate.

## What the Source Study Contributes

Within its task and fitted model, the study supports a rich account of neural
and behavioral duration processing. In particular:

- BOLD responses varied systematically across physical-duration conditions;
- fitted preferred-duration distributions differed across cortical regions;
- duration-related preferences exhibited spatial organization;
- selected anterior-insular and rostral-SMA preferences covaried with
  participant-specific PSEs in exploratory, uncorrected analyses;
- and regional preferences covaried across participants in patterns summarized
  by three statistical clusters.

These findings constrain accounts of how organisms discriminate and categorize
subsecond visual durations. They do not independently identify the organization
of temporal experience.

## Interpretation Boundary

Nothing in this collection should be read as claiming:

- that neuroscience proves or directly tests IER;
- that the nervous system cannot contain timing, estimation, accumulation,
  comparison, or temporally organized control mechanisms;
- that fitted preferred durations are directly recorded neuronal clocks;
- that visual activity demonstrates within-trial accumulation;
- that a PSE is an experiential event boundary;
- that medium-centered cortical preferences specifically track the PSE rather
  than the learned reference, range statistics, or decision structure;
- that correlation-derived clusters establish causal stages or information
  flow;
- that cortical regions instantiate IER operations such as collapse, welding,
  admissible-future organization, event articulation, or integration
  envelopes;
- that the experiment distinguishes IER from clock, state-dependent, attention,
  memory, event-segmentation, or decision-based accounts;
- or that stimulus-brain-behavior covariation establishes psychophysical
  identity.

Two design couplings are especially important. The learned reference, tested
range midpoint, and expected category boundary were all 0.5 seconds. In
addition, the noise patch changed on every display frame, so longer trials
contained both more elapsed time and more successive sensory input. The study
cannot separate the possible contributions of these variables.

## Two Senses of *Psychophysical*

| Use | Question | Role in this collection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experimental psychophysics | How do controlled stimuli relate to behavioral responses? | Directly investigated by the source study |
| Psychophysical identity | Do independently characterized experiential and physical descriptions refer to one occurrence? | Not established by the source study |

Experimental psychophysics can constrain a proposed psychophysical identity,
but it does not supply that identity automatically. Even perfect covariation
among stimulus duration, BOLD response, fitted preference, and PSE would leave
open whether experienced duration is identical with, caused by, realized by,
constituted by, or insufficiently characterized relative to the relevant
physical organization.

## Recommended Reading Order

1. Read the [Neuroscience Primer](neuroscience-primer.md) for the task,
   analysis pipeline, terminology, results, and evidential ledger.
2. Read [Four Durations, Not One](IER-four-durations-not-one.md) for the IER
   reinterpretation and experimental program.
3. Read *The Identification Problem for Physicalist Theories of Experience*
   for the broader argument about what would warrant co-reference between an
   experiential occurrence and a physical occurrence.

Readers already familiar with the source paper may begin with *Four Durations,
Not One* and consult the primer for methodological details.
