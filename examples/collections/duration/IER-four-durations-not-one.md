# Four Durations, Not One

## An IER Reinterpretation of Centanino, Fortunato, and Bueti's Cortical Duration Study

## Status and Scope

Status: exploratory application  
Authority: non-canonical

This article reinterprets Centanino, Fortunato, and Bueti's 2026 study of visual duration categorization through Informational Experiential Realism (IER). It is also a worked empirical companion to *The Identification Problem for Physicalist Theories of Experience*.

It does not claim that the study tests IER, identifies a Unified Experiential Field, or localizes IER structures in the cortex. It introduces no new IER ontology, criterion, mechanism, or diagnostic. Its purpose is to determine what the experiment establishes, what it leaves unidentified, and what a future empirical bridge would require once four easily conflated objects are kept separate:

1. the physical duration of a displayed stimulus;
2. a fitted cortical response parameter called preferred duration;
3. a behavioral decision criterion called the point of subjective equality;
4. experienced duration as treated by IER.

The experiment established relations among the first three. Beyond the shorter/longer reports from which the PSE was estimated, it did not independently characterize the fourth.

That distinction changes the reinterpretation completely.

## Abstract

Centanino, Fortunato, and Bueti used 7-tesla fMRI to study thirteen participants who categorized visual stimuli as shorter or longer than a learned 0.5-second reference. By fitting a Gaussian duration-response model to condition-level BOLD estimates, the authors found different distributions of modeled duration preferences across cortical regions. Visual regions tended toward the long end of the tested range; several parietal, premotor, and caudal supplementary motor regions contained preferences spanning the range; rostral supplementary motor, anterior insular, and inferior frontal regions tended toward its center. Preferred durations also correlated with participants' behavioral points of subjective equality in selected anterior-insular and rostral-SMA regions, in exploratory tests uncorrected for multiple comparisons.

The study's authors interpret these patterns as supporting a cortical hierarchy of duration encoding, readout, categorization, and subjective representation. An earlier IER treatment replaced those proposed functions with a cortical hierarchy of admissible continuation, frontier geometry, and event articulation. That replacement was not justified. The experiment measured neither admissible futures nor event segmentation, and its regional covariance analyses did not establish a causal sequence.

The more defensible IER interpretation begins elsewhere. The task organizes one physically timed event into several relational forms: persistent sensory stimulation, a learned comparison structure, task-relative neural differentiation, and a categorical action. The fitted cortical parameter preserves distinctions among experimenter-defined durations, while the PSE summarizes a participant's report criterion. Neither is identical to experienced duration. IER can therefore accept that neural systems discriminate, preserve, and transform duration-related relations for behavior without treating an internal clock, a duration map, or a decision boundary as the constitution of temporal phenomenology.

On IER's canonical duration account, experienced duration concerns the density and organization of cognitively registered welded deformation. Centanino et al. did not independently manipulate or measure that structure. Their experiment is consequently not evidence for "duration without clocks." Its present philosophical value is diagnostic: it shows how a sophisticated stimulus-brain-behavior study can remain on one side of the identification bridge. It is also a valuable platform for separating physical persistence, duration-sensitive representation, learned reference, category structure, motor implementation, and phenomenological duration in future work.

## The Reinterpretive Error to Avoid

The tempting argument is simple:

1. the study found distributed cortical response patterns related to a duration-categorization task;
2. different regions showed different fitted preference distributions;
3. selected anterior-insular and rostral-SMA preferences covaried with a behavioral criterion;
4. therefore the cortex organizes IER frontier dynamics rather than elapsed time.

The first three statements summarize results at progressively more interpretive levels. The fourth does not follow from them.

The study did not test for one central timing center, and its distributed results do not oppose clock theories. Contemporary timing accounts may be distributed, state-dependent, population-based, modality-specific, or coupled to separate memory and decision processes. A sensory response that grows with stimulus duration, a population code distributed across intervals, and a category-sensitive response can all coexist within ordinary timing models.

More importantly, changing vocabularies does not produce an explanation. Calling a duration map "admissible-continuation topology" does not show that the map concerns admissible futures. Calling a PSE an "articulation threshold" does not turn a report criterion into an event boundary. Calling correlated regional preferences a "frontier stack" does not establish a processing sequence.

IER's coordinate-system discipline is directly relevant here. A neural model, a behavioral measure, and a temporal-phenomenological account may describe related organization from different explanatory viewpoints. Translation among them must preserve demonstrated relations. It may not invent equivalences.

The proper starting question is therefore not:

> Which brain region performs collapse, welding, or event articulation?

It is:

> What relation does each quantity in the experiment preserve, and what additional claim is required to move from that relation to experienced duration?

## What the Experiment Actually Did

Thirteen healthy adults learned a 0.5-second visual reference before scanning. During scanning, each trial presented a blurred, dynamically changing visual patch for one of six durations: $0.2,\ 0.3,\ 0.4,\ 0.6,\ 0.7,\ 0.8\ \text{seconds}$.

The participant later reported whether the comparison stimulus had lasted longer or shorter than the learned reference. A response cue appeared 0.9 to 1.2 seconds after stimulus offset, so the task also required maintenance of the comparison, preparation of a categorical response, and delayed motor execution.

The fMRI analysis did not record subsecond neural activity unfolding frame by frame. Whole-brain volumes were acquired every 1.32 seconds, and the BOLD signal itself is hemodynamically slow. The researchers first estimated BOLD responses for the duration-by-position conditions using a general linear model time-locked to stimulus offset. They then fitted a population-receptive-field-style model at each cortical surface vertex.

The fitted model assumed:

- a Gaussian, single-peaked response over physical stimulus duration;
- invariance to the stimulus's screen position;
- and two response parameters, of which the article analyzed the estimated peak, $\mu_d$.

The paper calls $\mu_d$ the vertex's preferred duration. This is a model parameter, not a directly observed neural clock reading. At the edges of the tested range, a fitted peak can also be compatible with a monotonic response rather than a fully observed unimodal tuning curve. The authors accordingly interpret the concentration of long-end preferences in visual cortex partly by reference to prior evidence for monotonic sensory responses.

The experiment therefore provides a layered evidential structure:

| Evidential level | What exists at this level | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical manipulation | A visual stimulus persists for one of six externally timed durations | How long the event feels |
| Behavioral measurement | A shorter/longer response on each trial and a fitted psychometric curve | An experiential event boundary |
| fMRI measurement | Condition-dependent BOLD estimates at stimulus offset | Millisecond-by-millisecond neural accumulation |
| Neural-response model | A fitted peak $\mu_d$ for each included cortical vertex | A literal representation stored in seconds |
| Functional interpretation | Encoding, readout, categorization, and subjective criterion | A demonstrated causal hierarchy |
| IER interpretation | A proposed relation to temporal experience or task organization | Evidence that an IER structure was measured |

Keeping these levels separate is not a gesture of caution added after the interpretation. It determines what the interpretation can be.

## Four Quantities That Must Not Be Collapsed

### Physical duration

Let $d$ denote how long the visual patch was displayed according to the experiment's external timing apparatus.

This is an objective property of the experimental event. It is not made unreal by the claim that experience contains no internal timeline. A stimulus displayed for 0.8 seconds physically affects the participant longer than one displayed for 0.2 seconds. The sensory system is therefore exposed to a different physical process.

IER need not deny physical time, physical persistence, or an organism's capacity to discriminate temporally extended stimulation. Its claim about subjective time is not that nothing lasts. It is that temporal phenomenology is not constituted by reading a primitive internal elapsed-time variable.

### Modeled preferred duration

Let $\mu_d(v)$ denote the physical duration at which the fitted model predicts the largest BOLD response for vertex $v$.

The units of $\mu_d$ come from the experimenter's condition labels. A value near 0.6 seconds means that a model indexed by six externally timed conditions fitted a response peak near that condition. It does not show that the vertex contains, reads, or experiences the number 0.6.

Still, the parameter is not meaningless. It shows that cortical responses preserve distinctions systematically related to physical stimulus duration. In the bounded IER sense of representation as structural preservation across transformation, it is legitimate to investigate these responses as duration-related representations. What must be resisted is the stronger inference that the preserved relation is an internal temporal substance or that its neural form explains temporal experience by itself.

### Point of subjective equality

Let $PSE_i$ denote the comparison duration at which participant $i$ is equally likely to answer "shorter" or "longer."

The PSE is estimated from a distribution of actions. It describes the location of a behavioral category transition in physical-duration coordinates. It is called subjective because different participants may use different effective criteria and exhibit different biases. Reports are legitimate evidence about experience, but this statistic is not by itself an independent characterization of phenomenology, and it is not the point at which an experienced event becomes segmented.

This study contains a crucial three-way coincidence:

- the learned reference was 0.5 seconds;
- the midpoint of the tested range was 0.5 seconds;
- and the expected shorter/longer category transition was near 0.5 seconds.

Medium-centered cortical preferences could therefore reflect the learned reference, the statistics of the stimulus range, categorical decision structure, or some combination. The design cannot separate them.

### Experienced duration under IER

IER's canonical duration article proposes:

> Experienced duration tracks the density and structural organization of cognitively registered welded deformation within an interval.

This is not another name for $d$, $\mu_d$, or $PSE_i$.

It refers to how structural change becomes salient, segmented, propagated, integrated, registered, and in some cases reconstructed. The account was introduced to explain why physically similar intervals may feel different, why online and retrospective duration may dissociate, and why novelty, routine, crisis, boredom, flow, narrative, and music reorganize duration without requiring a stored temporal scalar.

Centanino et al. did not manipulate registered deformation density. They did not compare richly and sparsely articulated intervals of equal physical length. They did not independently measure event segmentation, integration-envelope organization, or online versus retrospective duration. Their behavioral task required temporal discrimination, but it did not isolate the structural variable proposed by IER.

The visual patch also changed randomly from frame to frame. A longer presentation therefore contained both more elapsed physical time and more successive sensory input. Even if the added input contributed to registered deformation, the design coupled it perfectly to duration. The experiment cannot determine whether any response difference concerns elapsed extent, sustained stimulation, accumulated sensory change, or the organization of that change.

This does not imply that no temporal experience occurred during the trials. Stimulus onset, continuing stimulation, offset, comparison, cue, and response all participated in the experienced situation. It means only that the experiment supplied no independent measure by which to identify any of them with IER's proposed basis of experienced duration.

The study may therefore concern a neural and behavioral capacity used in duration judgment without testing IER's explanation of experienced duration.

## From Experimental Psychophysics to Psychophysical Identity

The word *psychophysical* has two roles that must be separated.

In experimental psychophysics, a researcher controls a stimulus and measures a response. Centanino et al. did this: physical display duration was related to shorter/longer judgments, and both were related to fitted cortical response parameters. In the philosophy of mind, a psychophysical identity claim proposes that an experiential occurrence and a physical occurrence are one event. The first kind of relation can constrain the second, but it does not establish it.

The study's evidential relations can be written schematically as follows:

| Relation | Empirical status in the study |
| --- | --- |
| Physical duration $d$ and shorter/longer response | Directly manipulated and behaviorally measured |
| Physical duration $d$ and condition-dependent BOLD response | Estimated through the fMRI design and GLM |
| Physical duration $d$ and fitted cortical preference $\mu_d$ | Built into and estimated by the response model |
| Fitted preference $\mu_d$ and participant PSE | Correlated in selected regions; $n=13$ and uncorrected regional tests |
| Experienced-duration structure and any of the above | Not independently characterized |
| Experiential event and qualifying physical regime | Not identified or tested |

This is not a complaint that the experiment failed to solve a philosophical problem it did not set out to solve. It identifies the additional work required before its results can be used as evidence for psychophysical identity. Even perfect stimulus-neural-behavioral covariation would leave open whether the experiential description co-refers with one modeled physical process, is caused or realized by it, is constituted by a wider process, or has not yet been adequately described.

The distinction between function and phenomenology also resolves an apparent conflict within IER.

*IER-duration* is an explanatory proposal about experienced duration, not a negative inventory of possible neural functions. "Without clocks" should therefore mean that a clock is not the constituent or sufficient explanation of temporal phenomenology. It should not be expanded into the empirical claim that nervous systems contain no mechanisms capable of timing, interval estimation, or temporally organized control.

A nervous system may contain mechanisms that:

- respond differently to longer stimulation;
- preserve information about temporal extent;
- compare a current event with a learned reference;
- adapt to a distribution of durations;
- or generate a reliable shorter/longer action.

IER does not have to redescribe every such mechanism as frontier geometry. These are legitimate biological functions. They may involve ramping activity, changing population states, oscillatory relations, recurrent dynamics, or other timing-sensitive processes.

What IER denies is a further explanatory identification:

> A mechanism that estimates or discriminates duration for behavior is thereby what makes duration experienced.

That conclusion does not follow. A timing mechanism can contribute to action without serving as an ontological clock read by an inner subject. Conversely, experienced duration may vary when discrimination performance or physical duration remains stable.

This yields a cleaner relation between IER and timing neuroscience:

| Question | Appropriate explanatory target |
| --- | --- |
| How does the organism discriminate 0.4 from 0.6 seconds? | Sensory, neural, memory, comparison, and decision mechanisms |
| How is a learned duration relation preserved for a task? | Representation and current task organization |
| Why does the comparison become a shorter/longer action? | Categorization and motor implementation |
| Why does an interval feel long, short, thick, empty, expanded, or compressed? | Temporal phenomenology, including IER's registered-deformation proposal |

The questions interact, but they are not interchangeable. The Centanino case therefore functions here as an identification-gap analysis, not as a positive identity candidate.

## The Task as a Structured Situation

The experiment did not present an isolated duration to a passive brain. It created a highly organized situation.

Before a scanning trial began, training had already changed the participant. The 0.5-second reference was behaviorally available because prior exposure and practice constrained how the new stimulus could be taken up. In conventional terms, the participant retained a reference in memory. In IER's historical vocabulary, prior training had left deformation that could organize present re-entry. The current experiment does not distinguish those accounts, so either description must remain at its proper explanatory level.

During a trial, several relations were jointly active:

- a stimulus appeared in a controlled visual position;
- its ongoing presence altered sensory activity;
- its offset made its full physical extent available for comparison;
- the learned reference organized the comparison;
- the task instructions stabilized only two reportable categories;
- a later cue made one of two button actions appropriate;
- and a fixed hand-to-button mapping organized motor preparation.

The stimulus duration mattered because it participated in this whole task organization. A 0.4-second display was not merely "0.4 seconds inside the brain." It was a current event standing in a learned shorter/longer relation, embedded in a delayed-response routine with specific action consequences.

IER's situation concept is useful at this level. A situation is the integrated, pre-action organization through which behavior becomes locally coherent. The duration-categorization situation made physical persistence behaviorally actionable. This does not make physical duration unreal, and it does not imply that situation structure replaces neural timing. It explains why the same temporal sensitivity may be recruited differently when task, reference, response, or relevance changes.

## Reinterpreting the Regional Findings

The regional results should be interpreted as different response organizations within one task, not as anatomical names for IER operations.

### Visual cortex: sensitivity to sustained input

Ventral and lateral visual regions showed fitted preferences concentrated toward the long end of the tested range. The authors interpret this pattern as compatible with modest monotonic responses that increase with stimulus duration and may encode temporal information through sensory accumulation.

The current experiment did not directly observe a within-trial ramp. The GLM estimated offset-related BOLD differences, and the Gaussian model assigned peaks using the six duration conditions. The safest conclusion is therefore:

> Visual cortical responses differed systematically with how long the visual process persisted.

An IER interpretation need add very little. The continuing stimulus continues to perturb and organize visual processing. Longer exposure can produce a larger or more sustained aggregate response without requiring that visual cortex contain a scalar clock. But "continued sensory organization" and "sensory accumulation" are presently alternative descriptions, not experimentally separated mechanisms.

The old claim that visual cortex sustains unresolved frontier participation should be withdrawn. The study offers no measure of frontier admissibility or unresolved participation.

### Parietal, premotor, and caudal SMA: distributed duration-related differentiation

In several parietal, lateral premotor, and caudal supplementary motor regions, fitted preferences covered the tested duration range and were spatially clustered. The authors propose that these populations read out temporal information for perceptual integration, task performance, and action.

The finding supports a bounded statement:

> Duration-related distinctions are preserved in distributed and spatially organized cortical response patterns beyond early visual areas.

It does not determine whether those patterns are best described as dedicated duration codes, evolving network states, magnitude representations, learned task coordinates, action-oriented mappings, or mixtures of these.

IER can describe the patterns as representations if representation means preservation of task-relevant organization across transformations. It should not describe them as maps of $A_{\mathrm{UEF}}(s)$. Canonical admissible futures concern physically reachable continuation under a UEF's intrinsic constraint. The experiment did not estimate that domain, and a topographic cortical map of model parameters is not evidence of a reachability topology merely because both can be called geometries.

### Rostral SMA, anterior insula, and inferior frontal cortex: center-sensitive task organization

Rostral SMA, anterior insula, and inferior frontal regions showed many fitted preferences near the middle of the tested range. The authors interpret this as an abstract categorical representation centered on the boundary used to solve the task.

That interpretation is plausible but underdetermined. The same 0.5-second value served as range midpoint, learned reference, and likely category criterion. A center-sensitive response is therefore evidence of task-related organization near a privileged duration, but it does not identify why that duration is privileged.

The PSE analysis narrows the claim only modestly. Positive correlations emphasized by the authors occurred in selected anterior-insular regions - left FOP5 and bilateral AVI - and in one right rostral SMA region, a32pr. At the broad stream level, bilateral anterior insula and left inferior parietal cortex also showed positive associations. Inferior frontal cortex did not show the emphasized positive PSE relationship.

These correlations involved thirteen participants, many regional tests, and uncorrected $p$-values. They are valuable candidate effects, especially in light of related monkey electrophysiology, but they require replication and an experimental design that moves the reference and range statistics independently.

Most importantly, a report criterion is not event segmentation. Event segmentation in IER concerns the articulation of structurally consequential change through collapse, welding, propagation, and cognitive registration. The PSE instead marks where a psychometric response distribution crosses 50 percent. No event boundary was independently manipulated or measured.

The old claim that rostral SMA and insula perform event-scale articulation should therefore be withdrawn.

### Motor and somatosensory cortex: task implementation is visible in the map

Left motor and somatosensory regions tended toward short-duration preferences. Because participants responded with the right hand, and because motor preparation could begin from stimulus onset, the authors treat this pattern as likely related to motor preparation rather than duration tuning proper.

This is instructive. A fitted "duration preference" can arise from where a process sits in the temporal organization of the task rather than from a representation of duration magnitude. The parameter inherits meaning from both the model and the experimental sequence.

The motor result is therefore not an inconvenient exception. It is a warning against reading $\mu_d$ as one homogeneous neural property across every region.

### Correlation clusters: coordinated variation, not information flow

Correlations among participants' regional median preferences produced three clusters:

1. ventral and lateral visual cortex;
2. intraparietal cortex, anterior insula, and inferior frontal cortex;
3. inferior parietal, supplementary motor, premotor, and motor-somatosensory cortex.

The authors use these clusters to motivate a functional hierarchy with sensory, cognitive, and motor-oriented organization. The clustering result itself shows only that modeled preferences co-varied in similar ways across participants. It does not provide directionality, temporal precedence, effective connectivity, or causal dependency.

IER may regard the clusters as evidence that the task is implemented through several coupled organizations. It may not treat their dendrogram as the frontier stack.

## What "Subjective" Means in This Study

The title of the source article refers to discrete, categorical, and subjective representations of visual durations. These terms have legitimate experimental meanings:

- **discrete** refers to populations fitted as preferring different durations;
- **categorical** refers to response preferences concentrated near the task's middle or boundary region;
- **subjective** refers primarily to covariance between modeled preferences and individual behavioral PSEs.

The word *subjective* does not mean that fMRI measured the intrinsic organization of experience. It means that some modeled response differences covaried with an individual behavioral parameter rather than only with group-level physical duration.

This is still scientifically important. A neural response associated with an individual's criterion is not reducible to the external stimulus value alone. But the move from "individualized behavioral relation" to an independently characterized feature of subjective experience requires another bridge.

IER makes that bridge especially demanding. UEF organization is not externally identified by a report, cortical region, information-processing role, or neural similarity. The study can describe mechanisms and behavioral relations in human participants without thereby observing the IER identity condition or any particular phenomenal content.

## The Proper IER Reinterpretation

The most defensible reinterpretation can now be stated.

Centanino et al. show how a physical persistence relation is preserved and transformed across a duration-categorization task:

1. **Physical persistence** - the visual input lasts for a controlled interval.
2. **Sensory differentiation** - visual responses vary with the continued presence of the input.
3. **Distributed task representation** - fitted cortical response preferences preserve duration-related distinctions in several spatially organized populations.
4. **Relational categorization** - the comparison is organized relative to prior training, the presented range, and the two response categories.
5. **Behavioral settlement** - the trial culminates in a shorter/longer action summarized across trials by a PSE.

This sequence is an explanatory decomposition of the task. It is not a claim that the five terms form a demonstrated causal pathway, and it is not the IER frontier pipeline.

The IER contribution is twofold.

First, it clarifies representation. Duration-related neural organization need not be a miniature timeline or an inner quantity presented to a witness. A response pattern can preserve relations relevant to physical persistence, comparison, and action without copying time into the brain.

Second, it preserves the autonomy of the phenomenological explanandum. Successful discrimination, a cortical map, and a behavioral criterion do not by themselves explain why an interval has the experienced character of length, compression, expansion, thickness, emptiness, or flow. IER assigns that problem to the organization and registration of irreversible deformation, not to a clock reading.

This reinterpretation neither replaces neuroscience with ontology nor shelters IER from neuroscience. It identifies where an empirical bridge would actually have to be built.

## What the Study Contributes to IER

The study does not confirm IER, but it contributes three useful constraints and one worked demonstration of the identification problem.

### Physical, neural, behavioral, and phenomenal duration must be separated

Any IER account of duration discrimination must explain how an organism remains sensitive to objective temporal extent. Saying that subjective duration is registered deformation does not explain why participants reliably distinguish 0.2 from 0.8 seconds. That behavioral capacity requires a neural account.

Conversely, a neural account of discrimination does not automatically explain experienced duration. The two explanations must connect without substitution. Until experienced variation is independently characterized, relations among the other three quantities cannot show that an experiential and a physical description share a referent.

### Temporal representation is task-relative at least in part

The observed diversity across visual, parietal, frontal, insular, and motor regions is difficult to describe as one homogeneous duration variable. At least some response profiles are plausibly shaped by sensory persistence, task statistics, category structure, and action preparation.

This favors an analysis in which temporal information participates in a situation rather than existing as one context-free cortical quantity. It does not rule out dedicated or reusable timing mechanisms within that situation.

### IER's duration bridge needs operational content

"Registered welded deformation" cannot function as a retrospective relabeling of any neural activity that accompanies duration judgment. To become an empirical bridge hypothesis, its components need measures that are defined independently of the outcome they are meant to explain.

At minimum, a study would need to distinguish:

- physical duration;
- amount and organization of within-interval change;
- perceptual or cognitive event boundaries;
- attention and arousal;
- predictability and novelty;
- task difficulty;
- decision criterion;
- motor preparation;
- immediate duration judgment;
- and retrospective duration reconstruction.

Without that separation, vocabulary specific to IER has no greater empirical content than the vocabulary it replaces.

## A Better Experimental Program

No single experiment will isolate every distinction. A staged program can.

### Study 1: Separate reference, range center, and decision criterion

Vary the learned reference and the statistical center of the presented range independently. Use enough participants to estimate individual PSE relations with correction for the planned regional tests.

This design asks whether center-sensitive responses follow:

- the remembered reference;
- the current range distribution;
- the participant's fitted PSE;
- or a stable physical duration.

Possible results would have different meanings:

| Result | Strongest immediate interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Response preferences shift with the reference | Reference- or criterion-relative organization |
| Preferences shift with the range center | Adaptation to environmental statistics |
| Preferences track PSE after both are separated | Candidate representation of an individual decision criterion |
| Preferences remain fixed in physical units | More stable duration-selective organization |

This study would refine the source article's boundary interpretation. It would not yet test IER's account of experienced duration.

### Study 2: Separate duration relevance from physical stimulation

Present identical duration distributions under several task conditions:

- explicit duration categorization;
- categorization of a non-temporal visual feature;
- passive viewing;
- and an action task in which duration predicts a response without being reported.

This would reveal which response patterns remain stimulus-bound and which depend on task situation, attention, comparison, or action.

If a regional tuning pattern remains stable across tasks, a strong task-relative reinterpretation would be weakened. If it reorganizes with relevance and action structure, a context-free reading of the map would be weakened. Neither result alone would decide between IER and other cognitive models.

### Study 3: Separate physical duration from experienced-duration structure

Hold physical duration constant while manipulating the internal organization of the interval. Candidate manipulations include:

- one coherent event versus several perceptual boundaries;
- predictable repetition versus novel but matched stimulation;
- resolved versus deliberately unfinished action structure;
- sparse versus richly articulated sequences with matched low-level energy;
- or coherent versus disrupted grouping.

Collect both immediate and delayed duration judgments. Also measure event segmentation, attention, arousal, predictability, and memory performance independently.

This study approaches IER's duration proposal because it varies plausible contributors to registered deformation while holding elapsed time fixed. But event-segmentation theory, attention models, predictive accounts, and memory-density accounts may make overlapping predictions. IER would need a preregistered structural model explaining what its variable adds beyond those alternatives.

### Study 4: Dissociate decision boundary from event boundary

Manipulate the shorter/longer category criterion independently of perceptual segmentation within the stimulus.

For example, the same interval can contain or lack a salient internal event boundary while the learned comparison reference changes across blocks. If frontal or insular responses follow the report criterion but not segmentation, they should not be described as event articulation. If distinct responses follow each manipulation, criterion formation and event segmentation can be separated neurally.

This is the direct test that the earlier reinterpretation failed to require.

### Study 5: Establish causality and temporal order

The source paper's correlation clusters should be followed by methods capable of addressing direction and necessity. Depending on the specific hypothesis, this might involve temporally resolved electrophysiology, perturbation, effective-connectivity models, or convergent lesion and stimulation evidence.

Only then would it become reasonable to speak of information passing through stages rather than of regional response profiles and covariance.

## Bridge Hypotheses and Their Failure Conditions

The empirical program should test explicit bridge hypotheses rather than the IER identity claim wholesale.

| Bridge hypothesis | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duration-related neural patterns preserve task relations rather than one context-free scalar | Systematic remapping with reference, range, task, or action while physical duration is held constant | Stable coding in physical units across those manipulations |
| Experienced duration depends on organized registered change beyond interval discrimination | Equal-duration intervals differ in immediate or retrospective duration as a preregistered structural measure changes, after major alternatives are controlled | No added explanatory value beyond attention, arousal, predictability, event count, memory, and decision variables |
| PSE-linked responses concern decision criterion rather than event segmentation | Responses move with the criterion when segmentation is fixed | Responses move primarily with independently measured event boundaries |
| Online and retrospective duration depend on partly different organization | Within-subject dissociations follow preregistered measures of current organization versus later reconstruction | Both judgments reduce to the same timing and decision variables across suitable manipulations |

Failure of one of these bridge hypotheses would not by itself falsify IER's identity claim. It would falsify or displace this particular explanatory projection. That distinction is not immunity: an empirical reinterpretation that predicts nothing, survives every result, or merely renames established variables has failed as an explanation.

## Claims Withdrawn from the Earlier Reinterpretation

The following claims should not be retained:

- Occipital activity is evidence of sustained frontier participation.
- Parietal duration maps are maps of admissible-continuation topology.
- Caudal SMA stabilizes coherence-preserving futures.
- Rostral SMA and anterior insula implement event-scale articulation.
- The PSE is a participant-specific segmentation or collapse threshold.
- Inferior frontal cortex tracks each participant's PSE.
- The regional dendrogram reveals the IER frontier-resolution stack.
- The results are difficult for clock theories.
- The existing predictions experimentally distinguish IER from ordinary timing, attention, event-segmentation, or decision accounts.

These are not merely claims that require softer wording. They are mappings the experiment did not establish.

## What May Be Retained

Several more modest claims remain productive:

- The brain's response to a duration-categorization task is distributed and functionally heterogeneous.
- Physical persistence, task reference, category structure, and action implementation are all plausible contributors to the observed response organization.
- A fitted duration preference is a relation between a response model and externally timed conditions, not a literal internal clock reading.
- Some cortical response profiles may preserve duration-related organization in task-dependent forms.
- Selected anterior-insular and rostral SMA preferences may covary with individual decision criteria, pending replication and better experimental separation.
- Temporal discrimination and experienced duration are related but distinct explananda.
- The study supplies a strong paradigm for constructing future tests of those distinctions.

## Conclusion

Centanino, Fortunato, and Bueti did not discover a cortical version of the IER frontier. They found a rich set of modeled response differences while people transformed a physically timed visual event into a learned categorical action.

That result is more interesting when its levels are not collapsed.

The physical stimulus has a duration. Cortical activity varies with that duration. A model summarizes those variations as preferred durations. A participant's responses define a PSE. Each quantity is real within its explanatory coordinate system, and relations among them matter. None is automatically identical to experienced duration.

IER's contribution is not to replace every timing term with admissible futures. It is to insist that behavioral timing, neural representation, decision criterion, and temporal phenomenology answer different questions. A neural system may estimate duration without containing an inner timeline. A population may preserve duration-related structure without constituting the experience of duration. A PSE may organize report without segmenting experience. And a distributed cortical map may support temporal behavior without being a map of the subject's admissible future.

The source study therefore does not show duration without clocks. It shows why the word *duration* names several relations at once, and why relations among stimulus, brain, and behavior do not by themselves identify the occurrence of temporal experience.

The next step is not anatomical relabeling. It is experimental separation.
