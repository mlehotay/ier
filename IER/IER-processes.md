# Processes
## Constraint Processes and Constitutive Participation

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.5)**\
*T2 · Explanatory Elaboration · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This canonical Tier-2 elaboration develops the physical-process vocabulary
connecting the IER dynamical schema with bilateral constitutive participation.

Its relation vocabulary follows this dependency order: candidate physical
operation, synchronic closure, ownership scope, bilateral constitutive
membership, and only then diachronic continuity. The article does not supply a
general empirical oracle for any of those relations.

It:

* introduces no new ontological primitive
* introduces no criterion, threshold, measurement, or diagnostic for
  experience
* does not identify processhood, persistence, feedback, or reciprocal coupling
  with a Unified Experiential Field (UEF)
* does not treat mathematical variables or trajectories as additional physical
  objects
* does not treat constraint as an agent, force, controller, or selector
* does not claim that the phosphorylation or steady-jet examples are
  experiential
* does not replace the canonical definitions of constraint, intrinsic closure,
  ownership, participation, or identity.

The article clarifies how physically occurring processes can instantiate and
alter constraint structure, how several processes can form one candidate
operation, and why constitutive membership requires more than causal influence
in either or both directions.


## Abstract

IER is expressed through states, transitions, admissible futures, regimes, and
operations. These terms require a corresponding account of physical process.
A concentration, temperature, pressure, or membrane potential is a variable or
configuration. The consumption, replenishment, transport, exchange, and
transformation through which that variable changes are processes. A process is
therefore not a static value or an abstract path through state space. It is the
physically occurring, temporally extended transition-bearing organization
described by those values and paths.

A *constraint process* is a physical process whose evolving organization
instantiates, maintains, or changes restrictions on physically available
continuation. Constraint itself does not act. The physical process acts or
changes according to physical dynamics; the term *constraint* describes how
that organization limits admissible transitions.

Processes can affect one another without forming one operation. They can also
be mutually coupled, indispensable, or jointly stable while their relevant
continuations remain independently resolvable. Bilateral constitutive
participation names the stronger relation required for membership in one owned
operation: a process must help constitute that operation's continuation and
must have its own continuation constrained, jointly resolved, and inherited
through that same operation over the same interval and at the same physical
typing.


## Why IER Needs a Process Vocabulary

IER identifies experience with the operation of a physical system as a UEF.
That formulation can become unclear when *system*, *state*, *process*,
*regime*, and *operation* are used interchangeably.

The distinctions matter because a physical description may contain all of the
following:

* material that enters and leaves
* variables whose values change
* local transitions among configurations
* processes that produce those transitions
* an organized regime sustained through those processes
* an operation performed by that regime while it exists
* enabling conditions that remain outside the operation.

None can be substituted automatically for another. A material inventory does
not identify a process. A process does not become one regime merely by being
causally connected to other processes. A persisting regime does not thereby
become intrinsically closed. A qualifying UEF operation is not an additional
object placed above its physical processes.

The vocabulary has six ordered levels:

| Level | Meaning | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical variable or configuration | A physically instantiated respect or arrangement at a stated grain | A process or persisting bearer |
| Physical process | Temporally extended lawful change through configurations | One unified or owned operation |
| Constraint process | A process that instantiates or changes restrictions on admissible continuation | Agency, selection, or experience |
| Candidate operation | A jointly described, occurrent transition-bearing organization over a non-zero interval | Closure, ownership, membership, or persistence |
| Constitutive participant | A process satisfying contribution, bearing, joint resolution, uptake, and common typing within an ownership scope | A smaller experience or independent subject |
| Enabling or external process | A process that supplies conditions or effects while retaining independent resolution | Constitutive membership merely through necessity or coupling |

The order blocks an inference from physical influence directly to subject
membership. Each later classification presupposes the earlier physical
description without being reducible to it.


## Configurations, Variables, and Processes

A physical variable describes a physically instantiated respect in which a
system can differ. Examples include:

* concentration
* temperature
* pressure
* velocity
* conformation
* membrane potential
* spatial distribution.

A configuration gives values or relations among such variables at a stated
grain. It describes how the relevant physical system is organized at that
stage.

A process is not one of those values. It is the physically occurring sequence
or field of lawful changes through which configurations succeed one another.
Consumption is a process. Replenishment is a process. Diffusion, transport,
binding, release, phosphorylation, heat transfer, deformation, and fluid flow
are processes.

The distinction can be compressed as follows:

| Description | Example | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variable | available ATP concentration | A physically measurable respect of configuration |
| Configuration | the present distribution of ATP, ADP, substrate, products, and enzymes | A stage of the represented physical organization |
| Transition | one lawful change from the present configuration to a successor | A relation between configurations |
| Process | consumption, replenishment, transport, binding, and reaction occurring through time | The physical occurrence through which transitions are realized |
| Trajectory | a representation of the ordered configurations | A description of process history, not another physical bearer |

This vocabulary does not require that every physical process have a unique or
observer-independent decomposition. A process description must state its
variables, scale, grain, interval, and physical relations. Different
descriptions may overlap or place boundaries differently. The relevant demand
is that the proposed process be physically projectible and not assembled only
to obtain a desired UEF or subject verdict.


## Processes Persist Through Change

A process can persist without retaining the same material participants or the
same instantaneous configuration.

*The Steady Jet and the Marble* isolates this point. A water jet can remain a
continuing load-bearing formation while successive
parcels of water enter and leave it. The water is material throughput. Flow is
the motion of that material. The jet is the organized flow regime. The nozzle,
pump, reservoir, and power source supply enabling conditions. The marble's
load is borne through the jet even though no parcel bears it for the duration
of the episode.

This does not turn the jet into an entity separate from moving water. It shows
that process identity is not identical to the continued presence of one
material inventory.

Nor does turnover guarantee persistence. A jet ends if the organized flow
ceases even when water remains present. A different flow can begin using the
same nozzle and reservoir. What persists is the physically connected
transition-bearing organization, not the matter considered independently of
that organization.


## Steady States Are Still Processes

A process can remain active while a measured variable stays approximately
constant.

If ATP is regenerated at approximately the rate at which phosphorylation
consumes it, the measured available ATP concentration may remain stable. That
stability does not show that nothing is occurring. It is dynamically sustained
through continuing consumption, ADP production, ATP regeneration, transport,
and reaction.

The same distinction applies to steady fluid flow, physiological regulation,
thermal balance, maintained pressure, and other non-equilibrium organizations.
A static value can be the visible result of ongoing opposed or interlocking
processes.

IER must therefore distinguish:

```text
unchanging represented value

from

continuing physical process that maintains that value
```

Only the second supplies an occurrent operation through time.


## What a Constraint Process Is

Constraint in IER is a structural restriction on admissible continuation. It
does not push, choose, control, or select.

A *constraint process* is therefore not a process performed by an entity named
constraint. It is:

> a physically occurring process whose evolving organization instantiates,
> maintains, or changes restrictions on physically available continuation.

Examples can include:

* consumption changing which reaction pathways remain available
* transport changing which resources can reach a reaction site
* binding changing which molecular transformations remain possible
* membrane activity changing which ionic continuations remain viable
* deformation changing which mechanical trajectories remain reachable
* heat exchange changing which reaction rates or conformations can continue.

The physical interactions do the causal work. Constraint language describes
their modal consequence: some transitions remain available while others do
not.

The label is role-relative. Heat transfer may constrain one reaction while
remaining part of a wider independently continuing thermal process. Resource
replenishment may be constitutive of one reaction regime and externally
supplied in another. No physical kind is intrinsically internal, external, or
experiential.


## From Processes to a Candidate Operation

A candidate operation is an occurrent organization of physical processes
described jointly over a non-zero interval. It is not created by listing
everything that influences an outcome.

The candidate must have:

* physically interpreted variables and relations
* lawful transitions through which earlier configurations give rise to later
  configurations
* a stated scale, grain, interval, and intervention context
* a projectible joint description that survives permissible redescription and
  participant turnover
* counterfactual consequences beyond the one observed trajectory.

This excludes arbitrary aggregates, copied patterns, static snapshots, and
sequences assembled by an observer. It does not yet establish that the
candidate is unified, intrinsically closed, correctly bounded, numerically
continuous, experiential, or a subject.

A regime is the organized manner in which such processes continue. An
operation is the regime as physically occurring and doing its regime-level
work. These terms mark different explanatory views of the same physical
organization rather than additional ontological layers.

The formal synchronic condition belongs to *Intrinsic Closure*. Candidate
processhood does not anticipate its verdict. *Closure Under Consequence*
explains how production and inheritance belong to one continuing operation
without turning this process vocabulary into another closure test.


## Interlocking Is Not Yet Constitutive Membership

Two processes are interlocking in a minimal causal sense when changes in each
affect the continuation of the other. This is stronger than one-way influence
but weaker than common ownership.

Reciprocal coupling can occur across a boundary. A reaction changes the
temperature of its environment while environmental temperature changes the
reaction. An organism changes a tool while the tool changes the organism. A
jet pushes a marble while the marble deforms the jet. These relations run in
both directions without establishing one ownership scope.

The following features remain insufficient separately or together:

* causal influence
* causal reciprocity
* feedback
* correlation or synchronization
* indispensability
* common location
* shared material
* persistence of an effect
* stable equilibrium or steady state
* contribution to one observable outcome.

They show dependence. They do not show that the processes resolve their
relevant continuations through one non-externalizable operation.


## Phosphorylation in a Beaker

Consider an ongoing phosphorylation process in a beaker. Its continuation can
depend on an available phosphoryl donor, substrate concentration, enzyme
conformation, products of earlier reactions, and temperature.

The available ATP concentration is a present variable. The processes of ATP
consumption, ADP production, ATP regeneration, transport, binding, and release
produce its successor values. Phosphorylation depends on present ATP
availability and, by consuming ATP, helps produce the next nucleotide-resource
configuration. Free inorganic phosphate is not the direct phosphoryl donor in
the kinase-mediated reaction considered here.

In an endogenous resource loop:

```text
present ATP availability
    -> constrains present phosphorylation
    -> phosphorylation changes the ATP/ADP configuration
    -> regeneration changes later ATP availability
    -> later availability constrains subsequent phosphorylation
```

The resource process and phosphorylation process can therefore be examined as
candidate parts of one successor-producing organization. Present availability
is a consequence of prior operation, a condition of current operation, and a
component of the operation's next configuration. The example does not establish
that the candidate passes synchronic closure, ownership, or the full
constitutive-membership relation.

Temperature also constrains phosphorylation, and phosphorylation may release
or absorb heat. Yet the surrounding thermal process can remain independently
resolved by the room, water bath, hot plate, cooling apparatus, or other
environmental organization. The beaker then receives temperature as a
boundary condition even though heat exchange is reciprocal and indispensable.

The comparison is not ATP inside and heat outside by physical kind. An
external ATP-regeneration or supply apparatus can externalize the relevant
resource continuation. An insulated or actively thermoregulating operation
can incorporate relevant thermal processes. The boundary follows the process
role within the stated regime.

The beaker example does not establish a UEF. It isolates the physical
difference between endogenous interlock and external coupling.


## Why Constitutive Participation Must Be Bilateral

The argument for bilateral constitutive participation begins with the boundary
problem rather than with a definition.

First, a process cannot be constitutive of an operation if physically possible
differences in that process make no difference to the operation's admissible
continuation. Constitutive membership therefore requires contribution.

Second, contribution alone is insufficient. An external perturbation, fuel
source, controller, heat bath, or supplier can change every continuation of an
operation while resolving its own relevant continuation elsewhere. If its
completed effects cross an interface while its own successor problem remains
independent, it is a cause or support rather than a constitutive member.
Membership therefore also requires bearing.

Third, the presence of causal arrows in both directions is insufficient. The
contribution and bearing must concern the same restriction within the same candidate
operation. Otherwise evidence from different interactions, scales, or
intervals could be combined opportunistically to manufacture membership.

Fourth, momentary mutual constraint is insufficient for interval-level
membership. The processes must participate in one successor-producing
organization whose consequences are taken up into its later continuation.
Membership therefore requires joint resolution and diachronic uptake.

The resulting relation is bilateral because the process stands on both sides
of one owned restriction:

* it helps constitute what the operation can do next
* what the operation can do next constrains the process's own continuation.

These are not two unrelated causal arrows. They are two aspects of membership
in the same globally organized successor problem.


## The Full Membership Relation

For a physical process to count as a constitutive participant over a stated
interval, the following conditions must hold together:

1. *Constitutive contribution.* Differences in the process help determine the
   admissible continuation of the candidate operation rather than merely
   supplying an already completed input.
2. *Global bearing.* The operation's globally binding restriction constrains
   the process's own physically possible continuation.
3. *Joint resolution.* The relevant alternatives of the process and the rest
   of the operation are settled through the same non-externalizable successor
   organization rather than independently settled and joined at an interface.
4. *Diachronic uptake.* Consequences of the process's contribution and bearing
   enter production of the operation's later organization.
5. *Common typing.* Contribution, bearing, resolution, and uptake concern the
   same operation, restriction, variables, grain, scale, regime, intervention
   family, and interval.

No clause is sufficient alone. Together they distinguish constitutive
membership from external influence, realization support, and momentary
frontier relevance.


## Replacement and Externalization

The contrast can be tested conceptually by asking whether a proposed process
can be replaced by an interface supplying its completed effects.

If replacement preserves the same successor-producing organization while the
replaced process continues independently elsewhere, the process was external
for that typed restriction. Its effects mattered, but its own continuation was
not part of the operation's unresolved continuation.

If replacement changes an inherited endogenous consequence into an externally
supplied value, part of the operation has been externalized. Observable output
may remain similar while the organization of ownership changes.

This is a structural counterfactual, not a guaranteed laboratory procedure.
Cutting a process can destroy the regime, introduce slack, or create a new
boundary. The question is what relation the original process occupied, not
whether arbitrary removal leaves behavior unchanged.


## Process Boundaries and Physiological Limits

In organisms, physiology constrains the outer envelope of processes available
for possible organism-level recruitment. That envelope is not itself the UEF
or the subject.

Within the physiological envelope, some processes may participate
constitutively, some may remain recruitable but currently external to the UEF
operation, and others may merely support the substrate. Bilateral membership
determines actual constitutive involvement within the qualifying operation; it
does not turn the whole organism into one undifferentiated participant set.

Outside the physiological envelope, environmental processes can still alter
the organism profoundly. Causal reach is wider than possible organism-level
participation. This blocks expansion of the subject boundary through every
dependency while preserving genuine environmental openness.


## Process Parts Are Not Experiential Parts

A UEF operation can be physically constituted by many processes without being
phenomenally composed from many experiences.

Constitutive participants contribute physical differences, bear global
restriction, and help sustain the continuing operation. They do not each
contribute a smaller experience. Under the IER identity claim, the qualifying
operation as a whole is the total experience-as-process and subject.

If a participating process independently sustains another UEF at another
scale, that lower-scale operation is a numerically distinct subject. Physical
overlap or process parthood does not transfer its phenomenal contents into the
higher-scale UEF and does not provide the higher operation's unity.

The distinction is therefore:

```text
physical processes constitute one operation

not

small experiences combine into a larger experience
```


## Turnover, Recruitment, and Release

Constitutive membership is assessed over a non-zero interval, not inferred
from every instantaneous fluctuation.

A continuing operation may:

* recruit a process into constitutive participation
* release a process from participation
* tolerate changing participation depth
* preserve a process role through material turnover
* change its physical boundary while remaining one operation.

The steady jet illustrates why material turnover need not end a process. It
does not establish the stronger continuity conditions required by a UEF.
Numerical persistence additionally requires that the operation produce and
inherit its successors through the accepted stage-indexed relation
`Adj_dia(a,b)`: continuous closure, operational successor production, typed
consequence inheritance, operation-mediated change, and two-sided unique
continuation. Branching, fusion, reconstruction, or resemblance alone cannot
supply that relation.

This is the diachronic relation summarized by *Intrinsic Closure* and
explained through closure under consequence. Generic uptake, recurrence,
state dependence, gradual change, or causal descent cannot substitute for any
of its jointly required conditions.


## Non-Claims

This process account does not claim that:

* every physical process is a candidate UEF
* every constraint process is experiential
* feedback, recurrence, self-maintenance, or process persistence establishes
  intrinsic closure
* bilateral causal influence establishes bilateral constitutive membership
* every process inside a body belongs to an organism-level UEF
* every constitutive participant is salient, binding, represented, reportable,
  or equally recruited at every moment
* a process description uniquely fixes the correct scale or boundary
* the phosphorylation system or steady jet is experiential
* the phosphorylation or steady-jet example establishes ownership,
  constitutive membership, or `Adj_dia`
* process ontology by itself supplies the identity of experience and subject.


## Summary

IER uses *process* for a physically occurring, temporally extended,
transition-bearing organization. Variables describe aspects of its
configuration; trajectories describe its ordered history; regimes describe
its organized manner of continuation; and operations are regimes as they
physically occur.

A constraint process is a physical process whose organization instantiates or
changes restrictions on admissible continuation. Constraint does not act; the
physical process changes, and constraint language describes the resulting
structure of possibility.

Processes belong constitutively to one operation only when their relation is
bilateral in the stronger sense: they contribute to and bear the same global
restriction through joint resolution, diachronic uptake, and common physical
typing. This relation distinguishes participation from causal influence,
support, reciprocal coupling, and mere indispensability.

One qualifying operation may therefore have many physical participants without
being a sum of experiences. Under the IER identity proposal, the continuing
operation is itself the total experience and subject.


## Intermission - Structural Fact

A process can supply a condition without belonging to the operation it
conditions.
