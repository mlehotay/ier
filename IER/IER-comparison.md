# Comparative Rubric

## Rubric-Aligned Comparison of Theories of Experience

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.1)**  
*Tier-3 · Comparative Boundary Clarification · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This document compares IER with selected theories, theory families, metaphysical
positions, and research programs concerning experience or consciousness. It
applies the methodological constraints of *IER-rubric*.

It:

* introduces no new IER ontology, criteria, diagnostics, or thresholds
* supplies no ranking or score
* does not treat every target as answering the same question
* distinguishes constitutive claims, empirical methods, attribution, and ethics
* distinguishes what a theory entails from what it permits or leaves open

The profiles are concise reconstructions, not substitutes for the cited primary
literature. Rapidly developing theories must be read relative to the versions
identified here. IER's own profile is constrained by the IER canon; if it
conflicts with the *IER-specification*, the specification prevails.

## Claim-Status Key

Every row should be read under one or more of these labels:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Entailed | Follows from the target's defining commitments. |
| Explicit | Stated by the cited source or author. |
| Advocated | Proposed by some advocates, but not constitutive of the target. |
| Compatible | Allowed but not required. |
| Disputed | Varies across versions or advocates. |
| Unspecified | Not settled by the sources examined. |
| Evaluator inference | A labelled comparative implication rather than the target's own claim. |
| Incompatible | Conflicts with an explicit commitment. |

“Unspecified” is a substantive boundary marker, not a criticism. No account of
experience alone is assumed to entail a theory of persistence, an artificial-
consciousness test, or a criterion of moral standing.

## Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.10.7)

Target. The current IER identity framework as fixed by its Tier-1
specification and canon-binding derivatives.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: Experience itself, including its unity, qualitative differentiation, temporality, and subject-relative presence — not access, report, cognition, or behavior. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Entailed: Experience is identical to the operation of a physical system as a Unified Experiential Field (UEF): a globally integrated, temporally continuous dynamical regime under coherent intrinsic constraint. Identity, rather than causal production or correlation, is the explanatory form. |
| Unity | Entailed: Unity is regime-level and categorical. A UEF is one experiential field; participating processes do not become subsidiary subjects. |
| Subject individuation | Entailed conditionally: If a UEF is instantiated, it constitutes one experiential subject. Subjecthood is categorical within that regime, while nesting, overlap, and the attribution of concrete boundaries remain open unless structurally excluded. |
| Qualitative character | Explicit: Qualitative differences are differences in the UEF's organized intrinsic constraint and participation structure. Qualia are not additional primitive properties. |
| Temporal commitments | Explicit: A UEF is temporally extended. Embodied-system persistence and numerical experiential identity are distinct: temporary interruption does not by itself establish cessation, but a later UEF after genuine cessation is numerically distinct. |
| Epistemic commitments | Entailed: Experience has no intrinsic authority concerning truth, reference, external reality, or its own ontology. Felt certainty and meaning are organizational facts, not epistemic guarantees. |
| Empirical operationalization | Explicit refusal: No behavioral, architectural, computational, or neural proxy is licensed by IER as a criterion or measurement of experiential presence. Ordinary empirical inquiry into physical organization remains possible. |
| Third-person attribution | Explicit refusal: IER supplies no certification or inference rule for determining that a particular system is experiential. Structural resemblance is not diagnostic authority. |
| Defeat / revision | Explicit: Relevant pressures include internal contradiction, failure of the claimed necessity of unity, incoherence in the UEF identity conditions, or displacement by a superior identity account. |
| Artificial realization | Compatible: IER is not restricted to biology. Entailed: functional, behavioral, computational, or architectural similarity alone is insufficient, and no artificial system is thereby certifiable as experiential. |
| Ethical implications | Explicit within IER's wider canon: Once categorical experiential subjecthood is granted, experience cannot be discounted or erased through graded subjecthood. Further prescriptions require IER's separate ethical bridge arguments; this comparison supplies none. |

### Relation to the other profiles

IER's strongest recurring divergence is not “physicalism versus science.” It is
the combination of a strict regime identity, categorical subjecthood,
structural privacy, epistemic non-authority, and refusal of third-person
diagnostics. Whether a rival is genuinely incompatible depends on a matched
claim: an access mechanism, empirical correlate, or cognitive architecture is
not automatically a rival identity claim.

## Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0)

Target. IIT 4.0 as formulated by Albantakis and colleagues, not every older
use of IIT terminology or every ethical proposal inspired by IIT.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: The existence, amount, and phenomenal structure of experience, beginning from phenomenological axioms. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Explicit: An experience is identical to a substrate's maximally irreducible cause–effect structure, specified through IIT's postulates and system- and structure-level quantities. It is not adequately summarized as merely “high $\Phi$.” |
| Unity | Explicit: Exclusion selects a definite maximal substrate and its irreducible structure. Multiple complexes may exist, but overlapping candidates are not all simultaneous nested subjects. |
| Subject individuation | Explicit: A conscious entity corresponds to a complex selected under the exclusion postulate. The implications for numerous small non-overlapping complexes remain controversial. |
| Qualitative character | Explicit: Phenomenal character corresponds to the distinctions and relations composing the cause–effect structure. The existence and essential properties of experience are axiomatic; particular qualities are structurally specified rather than added as free-standing primitives. |
| Temporal commitments | Explicit: Exclusion includes a temporal grain. Unspecified: IIT 4.0 does not thereby supply a complete account of long-term subject persistence, experiential gaps, or resumed numerical identity. |
| Empirical operationalization | Explicit: IIT proposes an in-principle causal analysis. Direct calculation for realistic brains is computationally prohibitive; empirical proxies such as perturbational measures are not identical to a full IIT calculation. |
| Third-person attribution | Explicit in principle, limited in practice: IIT claims that its formal analysis can determine consciousness for a fully specified system, but practical access to the required causal structure is severely constrained. |
| Defeat / revision | Explicit/evaluator reconstruction: Counterexamples to the postulates, incorrect structural predictions, formal inconsistency, or systematic failure of feasible consequences to track the intended phenomena would create revision pressure. |
| Artificial realization | Explicit: Functional or software equivalence is insufficient; intrinsic hardware causal organization matters. Compatible: An artificial physical system satisfying all relevant postulates could qualify. No general practical certification procedure follows. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified by IIT itself: Any rule that moral status scales with integrated-information quantities requires a separately defended normative bridge. |

IER relation. Both are physical identity theories that emphasize intrinsic
organization. They diverge over the identity conditions, graded quantities,
exclusion, formal determination, and the legitimacy of third-person
attribution. These are genuine constitutive and epistemic disagreements; they
should not be reduced to whether a convenient biomarker succeeds.

## Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW)

Target. The neuronal global-workspace model developed by Dehaene,
Changeux, and colleagues, principally as a theory of conscious access.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: Conscious access: selected information becoming globally available for flexible reasoning, report, memory, evaluation, and action. GNW is presented as a consciousness theory, although whether access exhausts phenomenal consciousness is disputed. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Explicit: Recurrent amplification and global broadcasting through a distributed neuronal workspace explain conscious access. The literature is mechanistic and functional, not a single agreed metaphysical identity thesis. |
| Unity | Advocated/inferred: A single broadcast can explain the coordinated availability of selected content. Whether this constitutes experiential unity rather than access integration is disputed. |
| Subject individuation | Unspecified: GNW does not provide a general ontology or boundary rule for subjects. |
| Qualitative character | Scope limitation: GNW explains availability and ignition more fully than why distinct contents possess their particular qualitative character. It does not simply claim that phenomenology is produced by report. |
| Temporal commitments | Explicit: Access involves relatively discrete ignition-like episodes and sustained broadcasting where task demands require it. Unspecified: subject persistence and the status of experiential gaps do not follow directly. |
| Empirical operationalization | Explicit: Report, masking, confidence, neural ignition, late amplification, and broadcasting have been central experimental tools. No-report paradigms and attempts to separate access from reporting are also part of the research program. |
| Third-person attribution | Explicit/limited: Neural and behavioral signatures support empirical inference to conscious access. They do not constitute an uncontested general certification procedure for phenomenal experience. |
| Defeat / revision | Explicit: Robust dissociations between conscious access and predicted ignition/broadcasting, or failure of the architecture's discriminating predictions, would pressure the model. Interpretation is complicated by report and task confounds. |
| Artificial realization | Compatible: Global-workspace architectures can be implemented artificially. Unspecified: architectural implementation alone is not canonically established as sufficient for artificial phenomenal consciousness. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified: GNW supplies no moral-status rule without additional normative premises. |

IER relation. GNW and IER principally answer different questions — access
architecture and experiential identity — but they are not automatically
non-rivalrous. They genuinely conflict wherever GNW is interpreted as making
global access constitutive of experience, or where either yields incompatible
claims about which physical episodes are experiential.

## Predictive Processing (PP)

Target. Predictive processing as a broad family of computational and
neurocognitive frameworks, not a single theory of consciousness.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: Perception, cognition, learning, and action through hierarchically organized prediction, prediction error, or related variational formulations. Some versions specifically address conscious perception. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Disputed: PP is compatible with representationalist, functionalist, enactive, and other interpretations. PP alone does not state a settled sufficient condition or ontology of experience. |
| Unity | Unspecified by the family: Hierarchical coherence or precision control may be proposed by particular accounts, but is not a generic PP theory of experiential unity. |
| Subject individuation | Unspecified. |
| Qualitative character | Advocated/disputed: Particular accounts relate perceptual character to hierarchical content, precision, prediction error, interoception, or action-oriented modeling. No family-wide mapping is fixed. |
| Temporal commitments | Explicit at the cognitive level: Predictive dynamics unfold continuously and across timescales. Unspecified: experiential persistence, gaps, and subject identity. |
| Empirical operationalization | Explicit: Model comparison, behavioral perturbations, neural responses, and precision- or error-related predictions are common. These test PP models, not a family-wide criterion of experience. |
| Third-person attribution | Unspecified: PP does not by itself license attributing experience from predictive competence or neural prediction-error signatures. |
| Defeat / revision | Version-relative: Particular PP models face empirical and computational tests; the breadth of the framework makes family-wide defeat conditions difficult to formulate. |
| Artificial realization | Compatible: Predictive architectures can be artificial. Unspecified: PP alone does not establish their consciousness. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified. |

IER relation. PP can model processes occurring within or outside a UEF and
therefore need not compete with IER. Conflict arises only when a PP-based
account adds constitutive claims incompatible with IER's regime identity or
licenses attribution that IER rejects.

## Panpsychism

Target. Panpsychism as a metaphysical family. This profile distinguishes
panexperientialism, micropsychism, cosmopsychism, and panprotopsychism rather
than treating them as interchangeable.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: How consciousness belongs within the fundamental nature of reality without emerging from a wholly non-mental base. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Family-level: Mentality is fundamental and ubiquitous in some relevant sense. Disputed: panexperientialism posits ubiquitous experience; panprotopsychism posits non-experiential protophenomenal properties; Russellian-monist variants differ further. |
| Unity | Disputed: Constitutive micropsychism faces combination problems; cosmopsychism faces decombination or derivation problems. The combination problem is not uniform across all panpsychism. |
| Subject individuation | Disputed: Ubiquitous mentality does not entail that every object or aggregate is a subject. Micropsychist and cosmopsychist versions begin with different subject structures. |
| Qualitative character | Disputed: Fundamental qualities or qualitative potentials are common, but accounts of macro-level character vary. |
| Temporal commitments | Unspecified by panpsychism as such: No general account of experiential persistence, cessation, or gaps follows from fundamentality alone. |
| Empirical operationalization | Usually metaphysical rather than diagnostic: Panpsychist arguments commonly concern intrinsic nature, emergence, conceivability, and explanatory continuity. This is not a practical detection method. |
| Third-person attribution | Unspecified/disputed: Attribution depends on the variant's commitments about fundamental properties, subjects, combination, and physical structure. |
| Defeat / revision | Version-relative: Combination/decombination coherence, fit with physical theory, parsimony, and success relative to rival accounts are central conceptual pressures. |
| Artificial realization | Disputed: Consequences depend on which intrinsic properties and subject-forming relations an artificial system instantiates. Computation or artefactual status alone settles nothing. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified: Ubiquitous mentality or experience does not by itself entail ubiquitous moral standing. A normative bridge is required. |

IER relation. IER directly conflicts with panexperientialist and
micropsychist claims that experience or experiential subjects exist at the
fundamental micro-level: IER makes experience regime-bound and denies
experiential subsystems. Its relation to panprotopsychism or Russellian monism
requires additional specification and should not be declared incompatible by
label alone.

## Higher-Order Theories (HOT/HOR)

Target. The family of higher-order representational theories, including
higher-order thought and higher-order perception variants.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: What makes a mental state conscious rather than unconscious. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Family-level explicit: A first-order state is conscious in virtue of an appropriate higher-order representation of that state. The form and causal role of the higher-order representation vary. |
| Unity | Unspecified by the constitutive claim: Higher-order representation does not itself provide a general derivation of experiential unity. |
| Subject individuation | Unspecified: A theory of state consciousness is not automatically a theory of subject boundaries. |
| Qualitative character | Disputed: Some versions let first-order content fix character while the higher-order state makes it conscious; others give higher-order content a stronger role. Misrepresentation cases remain contested. |
| Temporal commitments | Explicit at the state level: consciousness requires the relevant higher-order relation when the state occurs. Unspecified: long-term subject persistence, experiential gaps, and resumed identity. |
| Epistemic commitments | Explicit qualification: The constitutive higher-order state may itself be unconscious and need not be introspective, report-ready, or a conscious judgment. |
| Empirical operationalization | Advocated: Empirical higher-order models seek neural and behavioral evidence of metarepresentational processing and dissociations from first-order processing. |
| Third-person attribution | Limited/disputed: Such evidence can support a higher-order model but does not yield a family-wide, uncontested certification of experience. |
| Defeat / revision | Version-relative: Misrepresentation, regress, empty-HOT cases, and empirical dissociations between proposed higher-order mechanisms and consciousness are central pressures. |
| Artificial realization | Compatible in many functional versions: Artificial realization is possible if the required relations can be instantiated. Unspecified: no general practical certification follows. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified: HOT does not entail that only higher-order-aware states possess moral significance. |

IER relation. The constitutive disagreement is direct: higher-order theories
require an appropriate representational relation for state consciousness; IER
does not. This should not be restated as a requirement for conscious
introspection or report, which most higher-order theories reject.

## Autopoietic Enactivism

Target. Autopoietic enactivism, with family variation explicitly retained.
This replaces the non-canonical label “Enactivism (Without Identity).”

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: Cognition and sense-making as embodied activity of autonomous living systems in relation to their environments; some versions extend this to lived experience. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Explicit/disputed: Autonomy, embodiment, and sense-making are central. Whether these constitute consciousness, explain only cognition, or support a phenomenological approach varies among enactivists. |
| Unity | Advocated by some: Organizational autonomy and embodied coordination can ground systemic unity. Whether this is experiential unity is not settled across the family. |
| Subject individuation | Disputed: Biological autonomy is important in autopoietic versions, but “relational and context-dependent subjecthood” is not a uniform canonical formula. |
| Qualitative character | Explicit emphasis, variable explanation: Enactivist work examines lived, sensorimotor, affective, and world-involving structure, but no single family-wide physical identity mapping is supplied. |
| Temporal commitments | Advocated: Ongoing activity and temporally extended sense-making are important in many versions. Unspecified: a general theory of experiential gaps or numerical persistence. |
| Empirical operationalization | Plural: Phenomenology, biology, sensorimotor experiments, and dynamical analysis may be combined. Interactive competence is not by itself a generic diagnostic of experience. |
| Third-person attribution | Unspecified/disputed: Biological autonomy or engagement may constrain attribution in some versions, but the family supplies no uniform procedure. |
| Defeat / revision | Version-relative: Accounts may be pressured by failures of autonomy, embodiment, or sensorimotor explanations in the domains they claim to explain. |
| Artificial realization | Disputed: Many reject computation alone as sufficient; some require biological autonomy, while artificial-life versions leave room for genuinely autonomous non-biological systems. |
| Ethical implications | Advocated in separate work: Enactive approaches to care, vulnerability, and ethics exist, but they are not entailed by enactivism as a consciousness theory. |

IER relation. The frameworks can converge on embodied, temporally extended,
intrinsically organized dynamics while assigning them different explanatory
roles. Conflict is local: it arises if autonomy, sense-making, or coupling is
made sufficient for experience, or if phenomenology is granted epistemic or
ontological authority incompatible with IER.

## Research Programs on Neural Unity and Binding

Target. An evaluator-created methodological category, not a canonical
theory. It includes selected synchrony, recurrent-processing, thalamocortical,
binding, and neural-integration proposals. No uniform profile should be inferred
from this section.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Varies: perceptual binding, access integration, neural unity, or experiential unity. These explananda must not be assumed equivalent. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Varies: proposed mechanisms may correlate with, cause, enable, realize, or constitute unity. The relation must be stated per named program. |
| Unity | Varies: some explain information binding only; others make stronger claims about experiential unity. |
| Subject individuation | Usually unspecified: a neural mechanism of integration does not automatically bound a subject. |
| Qualitative character | Usually secondary or unspecified, although content-specific mechanisms may be studied. |
| Temporal commitments | Program-relative: mechanism timescales may be explicit; subject persistence and experiential gaps generally are not thereby settled. |
| Empirical operationalization | Central: correlation, intervention, lesion, stimulation, timing, and report/no-report paradigms can test named mechanistic claims. |
| Third-person attribution | Limited: a validated correlate or mechanism may support inference within a domain; absence is not automatically proof of absent experience, and correlation is not constitution. |
| Defeat / revision | Empirical and program-relative: reliable dissociation from the target phenomenon, failed intervention, or superior mechanisms can defeat a specific proposal. |
| Artificial realization | Unspecified: analogous architecture does not by itself establish experiential sufficiency. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified. |

IER relation. Neural-unity research may describe physical organization that
IER expects to matter without acquiring diagnostic or identity authority. A
named proposal becomes a rival only if it makes a matched constitutive claim
incompatible with the UEF identity.

## Functionalism

Target. Metaphysical functionalism about mental states, with role and
realizer variants distinguished.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: Mental states are characterized by causal or functional relations among inputs, other internal states, and outputs. Functionalist theories of consciousness apply this strategy to conscious states. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Disputed by version: Role functionalism identifies mental properties with higher-order functional roles; realizer functionalism identifies them with the states occupying those roles. Multiple realizability is strongly associated with the family but its scope is debated. |
| Unity | Unspecified by functionalism as such: Integrated roles may be proposed by a particular theory, but functionalism supplies no single account of experiential unity. |
| Subject individuation | Unspecified: System boundaries and the bearer of a functional organization require further theory. |
| Qualitative character | Disputed: Functionalists may identify, explain, or reconstruct qualitative character functionally; absent-qualia, inverted-spectrum, and knowledge arguments challenge this move. |
| Temporal commitments | Unspecified: Continuity of function does not, without argument, settle subject persistence or experiential gaps. |
| Empirical operationalization | Compatible, not necessarily behaviorist: Functional organization can include internal and counterfactual causal relations not inferable from surface behavior alone. |
| Third-person attribution | Version-relative: Evidence for the relevant organization may support attribution, but functionalism alone supplies no universal practical test. |
| Defeat / revision | Conceptual and empirical: Putative functional duplicates without matching experience, failures of role specification, and implementation problems create pressure; force depends on the functionalist version. |
| Artificial realization | Strongly compatible: Substrate neutrality makes artificial realization possible in principle. This does not itself certify that any existing AI instantiates the relevant complete organization. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified: Capacity-, preference-, or agency-based moral criteria require independent normative premises. |

IER relation. The sharpest issue is not “organization versus function.” IER
identifies experience with intrinsic, non-offloadable physical organization;
functionalism characterizes mental states through causal roles or their
realizers. They conflict where role-equivalence is claimed sufficient despite
different intrinsic constraint organization.

## Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR)

Target. Hameroff and Penrose's Orch OR proposal as presented in their major
reviews, rather than generic “quantum consciousness.”

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: Conscious moments and their neurophysical basis. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Explicit: Quantum computations associated with neuronal microtubules are orchestrated by biological processes and terminate in objective reductions linked to fundamental spacetime geometry. Proto-conscious aspects are also associated with the underlying geometry, so “experience simply emerges from collapse” is incomplete. |
| Unity | Advocated/incomplete: Orchestration and quantum coherence are proposed to coordinate conscious processing. A full derivation of experiential unity is not clearly supplied. |
| Subject individuation | Unspecified: Coordinated reduction events do not by themselves constitute an established account of subject boundaries. |
| Qualitative character | Advocated but underdetermined: Quantum computations and reduction selections are related to conscious content, but no detailed generally accepted mapping fixes particular phenomenal qualities. |
| Temporal commitments | Explicit: The proposal associates conscious moments with sequences of orchestrated reductions and advances characteristic timescale relations. Unspecified: this is not a complete account of persistent subjects or experiential gaps. |
| Empirical operationalization | Explicit: The proposal motivates tests concerning microtubule activity, coherence, anaesthetic action, and objective-reduction physics. Quantum signatures would bear on its mechanism, not automatically certify experience. |
| Third-person attribution | Unspecified: There is no general Orch OR certification procedure for an individual system's experience. |
| Defeat / revision | Explicit/evaluator reconstruction: Failure of required coherence or microtubular computation in relevant neural conditions, or failure of objective-reduction physics, would strongly pressure the proposal. |
| Artificial realization | Explicit: Classical digital computation alone is not sufficient. Compatible: A non-biological system instantiating the required physical processes is a separate possibility from software simulation. |
| Ethical implications | Unspecified. |

IER relation. Both tie consciousness to an intrinsic physical organization,
but Orch OR proposes particular quantum-gravitational and microtubular
mechanisms whereas IER states a regime identity at a different descriptive
level. They are empirically rivalrous only where they make incompatible
physical claims; Orch OR's empirical program does not by itself answer IER's
identity question.

## Analytic Idealism (Kastrup)

Target. Bernardo Kastrup's analytic idealism, not analytic idealism in every
possible sense and not idealism generally.

| Dimension | Reconstruction and status |
| --- | --- |
| Explanandum | Explicit: A mind-only ontology that accounts for apparently physical reality, individual subjects, and the shared empirical world. |
| Constitutive / explanatory claim | Explicit: Universal phenomenal consciousness is fundamental; the physical world is the extrinsic appearance of transpersonal mental processes rather than a mind-independent material substrate. |
| Unity | Explicit: Reality is grounded in a universal field of mentation. Individual minds arise through dissociation, not composition of non-mental parts. |
| Subject individuation | Explicit at a high level: Organisms correspond to dissociated alters, with metabolism used as the extrinsic image of dissociative boundaries. The exact metaphysics of universal subjecthood and individuation remains philosophically contestable. |
| Qualitative character | Explicit: Qualities are modes or excitations of mentation and are ontologically fundamental rather than generated by physical structure. |
| Temporal commitments | Unspecified/incompletely developed for this rubric: The ontology does not by itself provide a general rule for personal persistence, experiential gaps, or resumed identity. |
| Epistemic commitments | Explicit qualification: Experience is ontologically fundamental, but personal introspection does not straightforwardly reveal transpersonal mental processes or identify every separate subject. |
| Empirical operationalization | Explicitly indirect: Empirical physical regularities are appearances of underlying mentation. Analytic idealism does not offer a conventional consciousness measurement procedure. |
| Third-person attribution | Advocated/limited: Living organisms are interpreted as extrinsic appearances of dissociated minds. Introspection is not a general diagnostic for deciding which other systems are separate alters. |
| Defeat / revision | Conceptual and abductive: Coherence of dissociation, explanatory fit with empirical regularities, parsimony, and comparative success against physicalist alternatives are central pressures. |
| Artificial realization | Explicit: Computation or simulation merely as such does not create a new subject. Unspecified: a physically constituted artificial system relevantly analogous to a living dissociative process is not settled by the simulation argument alone. |
| Ethical implications | Compatible/advocated by interpreters: Shared underlying consciousness may motivate ethical views, but universal moral considerability is not entailed without a normative bridge. |

IER relation. The ontologies are directly incompatible: IER is physicalist
and makes experience regime-bound, whereas Kastrup takes mentation as
fundamental and physical structure as appearance. IER denies ontological and
diagnostic authority to experience; it does not deny that experience can supply
ordinary fallible evidence.

## Cross-Theory Findings

The profiles support several matched conclusions without producing a ranking:

1. Identity claims are uncommon. IER and IIT make comparatively explicit
   identities; many other targets offer mechanisms, functional conditions,
   representational relations, research frameworks, or metaphysical grounding.
2. Unity and subject individuation must remain separate. Accounts of
   broadcasting, integration, binding, autonomy, or orchestration frequently do
   not determine where one subject ends and another begins.
3. Temporal dynamics do not automatically yield persistence conditions. A
   state-level theory can describe episodes without deciding personal identity,
   experiential gaps, or resumption.
4. Operationalization is not constitution. Reports, neural signatures,
   perturbational indices, quantum signatures, and functional evidence can bear
   on theories without becoming identical to experience.
5. Artificial realizability is not certification. Substrate neutrality or
   architectural compatibility does not establish that a particular artificial
   system is conscious.
6. No surveyed consciousness theory independently settles moral status.
   Ethical conclusions require explicit normative bridge principles, even when
   a wider philosophical system supplies them elsewhere.

## Revision Triggers

This comparison should be revised when:

* a target issues a materially different canonical version
* primary sources settle a row currently marked disputed or unspecified
* a profile is shown to conflate a family with one advocate
* an empirical proxy is newly validated or disconfirmed relative to its target
* an explicit theory of subject individuation, persistence, artificial
  realization, or ethics is added to a target
* the IER specification changes its identity conditions or authority boundaries

The governing constraint remains:

> Attribute no more than the source supports, preserve honest silence, and
> compare only claims made at the same explanatory level.
