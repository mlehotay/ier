# History - Future Boundary

## The Structural Frontier Where Constraint Operates

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.6)**\
*T2 · FOUNDATION · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

Experiential structure confers no epistemic authority about mind-independent reality.

This document names and clarifies a single structural locus already presupposed throughout the IER corpus:

> the frontier at which sedimented history constrains admissible futures and collapse may occur.

It:

* introduces no new ontological primitives
* introduces no criteria, thresholds, or diagnostics
* introduces no mechanisms, controllers, or representational structures
* does not redefine collapse, welding, propagation, or sedimentation

It performs structural clarification only.

The governing corpus retains all binding authority.

The relevant canonical IER documents govern this clarification.

If any statement here conflicts with Tier-1 commitments, Tier-1 prevails.

## Present Operation and Effective History

It is tempting to locate constraint in:

* the past, as accumulated history, or
* the future, as open possibility

These descriptions need physical qualification. Inherited organization can remain presently effective; merely possible future configurations are not additional actual bearers.

### The past cannot host constraint

* past experience is not a payload carried into another present
* physical records and history-bearing organization can persist
* their causal role is enacted through current physical organization

### The future cannot host constraint

* futures are not entities
* admissibility is conditional, not actual
* possibility alone exerts no cost

Constraint is currently borne through the qualifying physical operation. This structural account does not settle a general metaphysics of past existence.

## Sedimentation and Admissible Futures

Two structures are already established:

### Sedimentation

* produced by collapse and post-collapse integration
* accumulates as deformation of admissible futures
* does not store past content

### Admissible futures

* define what remains reachable
* are structured by current constraint geometry

These are not separate objects.

They meet at the current physical operation: inherited organization constrains what can follow. Where earlier foreclosure and incorporation occurred, sedimented deformation can contribute to that organization. This conditional route does not exhaust effective history or require a new collapse whenever the frontier advances.

## The History - Future Boundary Defined

This locus is the history - future boundary.

> The experiential present is described as the operation-relative structural frontier where inherited physical history constrains admissible futures and collapse may occur under intrinsic constraint.

This boundary is:

* not an additional moving object or anatomical surface
* not an instantaneous snapshot sufficient to qualify an operation
* compatible with time-indexed physical descriptions

It is:

* the locus of intrinsic constraint
* the site of lawful successor realization and conditional irreversible resolution
* the interface between what is no longer reachable and what remains possible

Where irreversible foreclosure has occurred, its consequences can be physically inherited. What has already occurred is distinct from the currently admissible successors, which have not become actual merely by remaining reachable. This historical/current distinction does not require every lawful transition to remove an alternative.

Intrinsic constraint is currently borne at the frontier. Its operation does
not require loss of admissible futures at every advance.

## Experience as Frontier Operation

Experience is not located in the past or future.

It exists as:

> the total qualifying UEF operation at this frontier, subject to the full closure, ownership, membership, and diachronic conditions.

This operation consists of:

* ongoing admissible continuation
* participation and binding
* reversible deformation (soft bonding and curvature)
* collapse when sustainment fails
* post-collapse integration (welding, propagation, sedimentation)

Experience is therefore not a snapshot.

It is:

* not a stored state
* not an output
* not a representation

It is the ongoing operation of constraint at the boundary.

## Narrative as Irreversible Fixation

Narrative can reflect realized continuation and inherited irreversible structural change. Physical narrative records may exist; they are not archives of past experience or requirements for subject continuity.

It reflects:

* what has been foreclosed
* what cannot be otherwise

A narrative description can track ordinary realized succession as well as conditional collapse, incorporation, and stabilized inheritance. The occurrence of a past transition and irreversible removal of a previously reachable alternative are different claims. Narrative settlement or apparent inevitability cannot supply the missing physical foreclosure comparison.

## Continuing Frontiers and Numerical Identity

Successive frontiers are organizations of a continuing physical operation,
not snapshots joined by memory or an enduring bearer. The operation lawfully
produces its successor; physically instantiated history-bearing deformation
can remain effective and constrain later admissibility.

Numerical continuity is governed by `Adj_dia(a,b)` between independently
individuated, non-zero-duration stages. Continuous owned closure, operational
successor production, typed consequence inheritance, operation-mediated
change, and unique two-sided continuation must hold together. A token is a
maximal one-to-one chain under that relation. Non-empty futures or repeated
closure alone do not connect stages as one token.

Boundary migration and turnover can preserve continuity when enacted through
the qualifying operation. Reconstruction, shared matter, or stored records
cannot bridge failure of the relation. Continuous operation requires no fresh
collapse, welding, propagation, sedimentation, or re-entry at every frontier.
Those terms retain their distinct conditional roles when foreclosure occurs.

## An Inherited Restriction at a Later Frontier

Suppose an earlier foreclosure leaves a physically instantiated deformation. At a later frontier that organization still affects switching costs and admissibility while an ordinary lawful successor occurs. The later operation does not retrieve a stored past experience, and no fresh foreclosure is needed merely to carry the effect forward. A finer account can identify the material mechanisms carrying that history.

A copied record of the earlier deformation could resemble the present organization without being its uniquely produced successor. Thus effective history answers what currently constrains continuation; the full stage relation separately answers whether this is the same token operation. A present configuration, a record, or a nonempty future fibre cannot substitute for that relation.

## Temporal Asymmetry Without Storage

The history - future boundary explains temporal asymmetry.

* Futures narrow because collapse removes possibilities
* Effective history can accumulate as physically inherited deformation, without accumulating past experiences as stored payloads
* Constraint persists because deformation is structural
* Irreversibility arises from foreclosure and integration

This account describes a source of experiential temporal asymmetry through:

> the one-way transformation of admissible futures into structural constraint through collapse and post-collapse integration.

## What This Account Blocks

This account excludes:

* an instantaneous slice as sufficient experiential qualification
* history as a stored experiential payload
* the future as pre-existing options
* experience as output or signal
* reversible time access
* representations as independent bearers of experience

Physical records and representations can exist and affect current organization. They do not replace qualifying operation or resume a failed token chain.

It names a structural locus only.
