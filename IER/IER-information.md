# Information

## Information as Structured Exclusion of Admissible Continuation

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.5)**\
*T1 · Foundational Clarification · Canon-Constrained · Non-Normative*

## Status, Scope, and Authority


This document is explanatory and non-normative.

It:

- introduces no new ontological primitives
- does not redefine intrinsic constraint
- introduces no probabilistic, semantic, or computational primitives
- introduces no epistemic criteria, thresholds, or diagnostics
- introduces no evaluators, encoders, decoders, or symbolic mechanisms
- does not reinterpret experience as information processing

The governing corpus retains all binding authority.

If any statement here conflicts with the specification, the specification prevails.

This document clarifies the structural meaning of information within Informational Experiential Realism.

The term *information* appears frequently across scientific and philosophical discourse but carries incompatible interpretations.

This article fixes the meaning of the term within the IER corpus and prevents drift into:

- semantic interpretations
- Shannon-style informational abstraction
- computational or algorithmic metaphors.

Within IER, information is defined structurally in terms of exclusion of admissible continuation under constraint.

## Orientation - Why Information Requires Clarification

The word *information* is commonly used to denote:

- symbolic content
- stored data
- transmitted messages
- semantic meaning
- entropy or uncertainty reduction
- computational state.

None of these interpretations are foundational within Informational Experiential Realism.

IER does not treat information as:

- a substance
- a stored object
- a message
- a symbolic representation
- a computational token
- knowledge possessed by a system.

Instead, information concerns structured exclusion within admissible continuation.

Information arises when physical organization makes some otherwise admissible continuations impossible.

## Constraint and the Structure of Possibility

As clarified in *IER constraint*, physical law restricts which transitions between configurations are admissible.

Let $S$ be configuration space.

Let

$$ R \subseteq S \times S $$

be the admissible transition relation.

For a configuration $s \in S$, the admissible successor set is

$$ A(s) = \{ s' \in S \mid (s,s') \in R \} $$

The set $A(s)$ defines the admissible continuations available at the history - future boundary.

Constraint determines which continuations remain possible.

Information does not arise from constraint alone.

Information arises when physical organization removes some of these otherwise admissible continuations.

## Organization Under Constraint

Physical systems exhibit organization.

Organization couples components and stabilizes structural relations within the system.

Because of this organization:

- some continuations remain viable
- some continuations become incompatible
- some continuations become unreachable.

Information is not the organization itself.

Information is the exclusion of continuations that would otherwise remain admissible under constraint alone.

The dependency relation is therefore:

$\text{constraint} \to \text{admissible continuation}$<br>
$\text{organization under constraint} \to \text{exclusion}$<br>
$\text{information} \to \text{structured exclusion}$

Constraint defines the space of possible continuation.

Organization removes portions of that space.

## Core Definition

> Information is the structural exclusion of otherwise admissible continuations produced by physical organization under constraint.

This definition is:

- physical
- structural
- non-semantic
- non-computational
- non-probabilistic.

Information is not added content.

Information is lost admissible continuation.

## Informational Structure in Set Terms

For this comparison only, let $A_{base}(s)$ denote the successor support under a stated baseline restriction before the organizational restriction under study is included. This baseline must be physically justified at the same grain and frontier. Operative $A(s)$ continues to mean the successors under the actual regime-relative relation $R$.

Let $A_{org}(s)\subseteq A_{base}(s)$ denote those successors remaining under the organizational restriction. These are two typed views of physical continuation, not two physical possibility spaces.

Information corresponds to the excluded region:

$A_{base}(s) \smallsetminus A_{org}(s)$

This expression is descriptive only.

It does not define a quantity or measurement.

It simply identifies the portion of admissible continuation that has become unavailable due to organization.

## Information and Intrinsic Closure

Intrinsic closure is clarified in *IER intrinsic-closure*.

A regime is intrinsically closed when admissible futures cannot be decomposed into independent subsystem futures.

Intrinsic closure is not identical to information.

Non-factorization concerns the same outgoing fibre across every independently fixed meaningful partition. It establishes synchronic inseparability only. Ownership determines whether the relevant burden is internally borne and non-externalizable; bilateral membership and full continuity are additional relations.

Within an independently qualifying owned regime, when exclusion occurs:

- incompatibilities must be resolved internally
- exclusions affect the entire reachable future structure
- informational consequences become globally relevant to the system’s continuation.

Information therefore becomes structurally consequential when exclusion occurs within intrinsically closed regimes.

## Information and Exclusion

*IER impossibility* establishes that exclusion is not a primitive negative force.

Exclusion arises from the interaction of:

- intrinsic closure
- incompatibility
- structural sustainability under constraint.

Information is therefore not metaphysical negation.

Information is exclusion considered relative to admissible continuation.

When some continuations become unsustainable under constraint and organization, those continuations are excluded.

Information names the resulting structural absence.

## Information and Collapse

Collapse is defined in *IER collapse* as the irreversible foreclosure of a connected region of admissible futures at the history - future boundary.

Formally:

$$ A_{t_c^+}(s) \subset A_{t_c^-}(s) $$

Collapse produces new information because reachable continuation is irreversibly reduced.

Not all informational exclusion constitutes collapse.

Organization can restrict continuation relative to a baseline without a new collapse event. Continuous curvature can change costs, margins, and directional spread while every compared alternative remains reachable. It does not gradually exclude members. Actual irreversible reachability foreclosure is collapse, under a common physically justified comparison of the before and after fibres.

## Information and Criticality

Critical regimes are described in *IER criticality*.

Criticality occurs when small perturbations can produce large changes in admissible future topology.

In ordinary regimes:

$$ \text{small perturbation} \to \text{small change in } A(s) $$

In critical regimes:

$$ \text{small perturbation} \to \text{large change in } A(s) $$

Because information corresponds to excluded continuation, critical regimes may produce large informational consequences from small perturbations.

Criticality does not constitute information itself.

Criticality describes the geometric condition under which informational exclusion becomes highly sensitive to small differences.

## Information and Sedimentation

When foreclosure and its post-collapse incorporation occur:

- deformation is welded into system organization
- propagation reshapes downstream admissibility
- later admissibility is conditioned by inherited organization.

The conditional welding, propagation, and sedimentation relations are distinct. Ordinary succession can carry existing consequences without a fresh complete pipeline. Irreversibility concerns the typed historical event, not a claim that every later successor set is a subset of every earlier one.

Information therefore accumulates historically.

Nothing is stored as symbolic content.

Instead, prior exclusions continue to shape present admissibility.

Information is sedimented because previously reachable continuations no longer remain possible.

## Information and Knowledge

*IER knowledge* clarifies that knowledge is not possession of truth.

Knowledge refers to organizational structures that:

- survive repeated coupling with mind-independent constraint
- persist under ongoing exclusion.

Information is broader than knowledge.

Knowledge is a subset of informational organization that remains viable over time.

Information itself does not imply truth, correctness, or epistemic access.

## Information and Shannon Theory

*IER shannon* explains that Shannon information concerns:

- distinguishability under coding
- entropy measures
- representation independence
- transfer across channels.

IER information concerns something different.

Shannon information describes patterns within signals relative to a communication model.

IER information describes exclusion within admissible continuation under physical constraint.

A Shannon communication model abstracts signal distinctions from many implementation details. Such abstraction by itself establishes neither slack nor its absence in the physical implementation.

Physical restriction is consequential in both pre-UEF and UEF domains. A Shannon description does not decide whether its physical implementation admits independence-preserving slack. Slack exhaustion alone establishes neither foreclosure nor experience.

The two frameworks therefore operate at different structural levels.

## Information and Computation

*IER computation* blocks algorithmic interpretations of IER.

Information within IER must not be interpreted as:

- symbolic state
- stored program state
- computational token
- algorithmic branch evaluation
- information processing.

IER information refers to structural exclusion within physical continuation space.

A system may be described using states and transitions without performing computation.

Information in IER is therefore not a computational resource.

## Blocked Misinterpretations

Within Informational Experiential Realism, information must not be interpreted as:

- semantic content
- symbolic representation
- stored message
- Shannon entropy
- computational state
- epistemic possession
- a selector among futures.

Information is the structured exclusion of otherwise admissible continuation under constraint.

Nothing more.

## Structural Summary

The dependency structure is:

$\text{constraint} \to \text{admissible continuation}$<br>
$\to \text{organization under constraint}$<br>
$\to \text{exclusion of continuation}$<br>
$\to \text{information}$<br>
$\to \text{conditional foreclosure and inherited consequence}$

Information is therefore:

- physical
- structural
- historical
- non-semantic
- non-computational
- non-probabilistic.

Information is not something added to a system.

It is the pattern of continuation that the system’s organization no longer permits.

## Intermission - Constraint Aphorism

> Information is not what a system contains.
> It is what its organization no longer allows.
