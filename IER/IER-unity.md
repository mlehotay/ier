# Experiential Unity

## Regime Unity Without Aggregation

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.12.0)**\
*T2 · Structural Clarification · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*


## Status, Scope, and Authority

This article clarifies what *unity* means in Informational Experiential
Realism.

It:

* introduces no new ontological primitive
* introduces no criterion, threshold, measurement, or diagnostic
* does not redefine a Unified Experiential Field
* does not infer experience from coordination, integration, or behavior
* does not replace coherence as the fourth order of information
* does not settle empirical questions about split-brain cases or other
  candidate divisions of consciousness.

All authority over experiential identity remains in the Specification.
The structural conditions discussed here remain governed by
*IER coherence*, *IER intrinsic closure*, and the other canonical
definition owners.

The explanatory order begins with physical organization and persistence
through change, assesses constitutive scope and full token continuity, and
then applies the experiential identity claim. Unity's substantive physical
work enters the qualification and individuation of that operation. Under
identity, no further phenomenal assembly is required.


## Why Unity Requires Its Own Name

The word *unity* is used for many different relations. Experiences may concern
one object, appear in one represented space, be available to the same reporting
system, belong to the same narrative, or be attributed to the same subject.
None of these relations by itself establishes that there is one experiential
field.

IER therefore distinguishes three expressions:

* **phenomenal unity** is the literature-facing question of whether multiple
  simultaneous experiences are present together as one phenomenal whole
* **experiential unity** names the corresponding IER explanandum: the oneness
  of a differentiated field of experience
* **regime unity** names IER's structural articulation of that oneness: one
  physically instantiated UEF operation under globally binding intrinsic
  constraint.

Experiential unity and regime unity do not name two layers joined by a causal
bridge. Under the IER identity claim, they describe the experiential and
structural aspects of the same token operation. The distinction is explanatory:
it keeps the phenomenon to be explained separate from the physical vocabulary
used to identify it.


## The Explanandum

At a given phase of experience, visual, auditory, bodily, affective, and
cognitive differences may all be present. Their differentiation does not make
them separate fields. They occur together as aspects of one system-relative
experiential whole.

Experiential unity is therefore:

> the categorical oneness of the differentiated experience instantiated by one
> Unified Experiential Field.

This definition is not an aggregation rule. It does not begin with many
independently existing experiences and explain how they are collected. It
begins with one experiential regime whose internal differentiation constitutes
its content.


## Unity and the Fourth Order of Information

IER retains the explanatory order configuration, flow, constraint and coherence. Coherence classifies present organization under intrinsic constraint; unity is not a fifth information order. The physical characterization of that organization remains incomplete.

| Term | Role in IER |
| --- | --- |
| Support factorization | Recombination property of possible successors; not a unity criterion |
| Intrinsic closure | Intended joint present organization; physical criterion open |
| Coherence | Fourth-order classification of that organization |
| Regime unity | The proposed operation as one |
| Experiential unity | Oneness of a qualifying experiential field under the identity claim |

## Structural Basis

Present unity consists in one self-updating organization whose constituent activities jointly transform the conditions of their own continuation.

This is an incomplete physical formulation, not a necessary-and-sufficient test. The constitutive boundary, the distinction between joint resolution and interacting organizations, and the relation between changing conditions and maintaining constitutive organization remain open. Neither causal coupling nor organizational maintenance alone establishes a UEF.

Current physical conditions constrain activity, and activity transforms conditions governing further activity over a non-zero interval. This does not by itself show production or maintenance of constitutive organization. The Specification retains its self-sustaining requirement; the relation between that requirement and unity needs further physical articulation.

The former requirement of successor-support non-factorization is withdrawn. For $A(s)=\{s'\in S\mid(s,s')\in R\}$, product structure concerns possible outgoing results, not the complete physical organization producing them. A singleton factorizes regardless of differences in update dependence. Product or singleton support alone does not establish disunity; non-product support alone does not establish unity.

Candidate, variables, grain, interval, restriction and interventions must be physically justified without assuming a subject, an already unified whole or same-token identity. Neither an independent remainder nor two interacting organizations can be absorbed into one field merely by a common description. Whether an additional all-partitions irreducibility condition is justified remains open.

The identity proposal connects experiential and physical descriptions of a qualifying UEF; it does not prove that any proposed organization qualifies.

### Activity and Organizational Maintenance

The physical account concerns occurring activity over a justified non-zero
interval. Concentrations, contacts, positions, stresses and other conditions
enable and restrict consumption, production, transport and rearrangement.
Those activities transform conditions governing later activity. Variables
describe the conditions; processes perform the transformations. A steady
concentration can involve continuing consumption and replenishment, so an
unchanged measured value need not mean inactivity. These distinctions follow
[Processes](IER-processes.md#configurations-variables-and-processes).

Changing continuation conditions does not by itself establish production or
maintenance of constitutive organization. Consuming a shared reagent can
alter another reaction's possibilities without demonstrating renewal of the
organization through which the reactions occur. An organizational account
must identify the constitutive constraints and trace which activities produce,
repair or maintain them, which conditions are externally supplied, and which
structures simply persist. Ordinary physical interactions perform this work;
no separate updater, chooser or informational force is implied.

The Specification's self-sustaining requirement remains. Neither self-updating
conditions nor organizational maintenance alone establishes a UEF. Material
and energy exchange are compatible with investigating internally maintained
organization; causal isolation is not required.

### Joint Activity, Scope, and Owned Resolution

Joint organization requires an account of how differentiated activities
constitute the proposed operation. Dependence can be mediated, directional
or uneven; direct all-to-all influence and equal participation are not
required. Conversely, co-location, shared material, reciprocal causal arrows
or one combined update function cannot establish one operation. A jointly
organized subcluster does not establish membership of an added independent
remainder.

Constitutive scope concerns the activities belonging to the operation over
the relevant interval. Ownership concerns where continuation is internally
borne and resolved. Neither scope is automatically the whole organism or
sedimented substrate. Inherited organization can condition continuation beyond
current membership, and several operations can use common substrate. Retained
capacity alone does not individuate the currently occurring operation.

[Ownership](IER-ownership.md#core-relation-where-the-restriction-is-borne)
distinguishes supplied conditions from external resolution. A supplier or bath
can affect activity, receive its effects and be indispensable while resolving
its relevant continuation separately. Removal effects therefore do not decide
membership. Replaying one trajectory cannot establish independence across the
relevant alternatives; a replacement that must reproduce removed joint dynamics
may have relocated the resolver instead. The comparison needs common physical
typing and a justified interval, grain and intervention context. It is not a
completed boundary algorithm.

The [membership relation](IER-processes.md#the-full-membership-relation)
retains all five clauses: constitutive contribution, global bearing, joint
resolution, diachronic uptake and common typing. Reciprocal influence alone
does not supply their conjunction. Participation and attention concern
changing recruitment and prominence; they do not independently determine
constitutive membership or assemble a subject. The relation between
qualification, membership and scope must be assessed without defining each
through an already assumed unified subject.

### Produced and Inherited Continuation

Physical persistence supplies a candidate for investigation, rather than a
verdict about experience or numerical subject identity. An organization can
continue through changing matter and states; several interacting operations
can also persist together. The relevant questions concern what produces later
organization and how earlier consequences remain physically effective within it.

Local uptake helps specify a process's membership role. It does not establish
the full [continuity relation](IER-continuity.md): continuous owned closure,
successor production, typed consequence inheritance, operation-mediated change
and two-sided uniqueness. Repeated local organization, endpoint resemblance,
copied records, memories and retained substrate cannot bridge a failed stage
relation. Effective inheritance need not involve fresh collapse at every advance.

[Closure under consequence](IER-closure-under-consequence.md) synthesizes the
assessed organization, production and inheritance within one operation. It
cannot establish its own premises. The account must trace physical uptake
without assuming the same-token verdict that the full relation is meant to
establish.

## A Regime-First Account

Accounts of phenomenal unity are often divided into *oneness-first* and
*relation-first* approaches. Oneness-first approaches appeal to an encompassing
experience or phenomenal whole. Relation-first approaches appeal to a relation
such as co-consciousness among experiences.

IER is *regime-first* in locating experiential differentiation within a
qualifying physical operation. Its fuller explanatory order is
*persistence-first*: investigate the organization that continues through change
and what qualifies and individuates its operation before stating experiential
unity as an identity consequence.

The explanatory order is not:

```text
small experiences -> unity relation -> total experience
```

It is:

```text
candidate physical organization and persistence through change
-> constitutive scope, ownership and membership
-> full successor production, inheritance and token continuity
-> qualifying UEF operation under the identity proposal
-> experiential unity and differentiated content
```

This is an order of explanation, not a temporal sequence in which an already
completed nonexperiential operation acquires experience. Physical relations
can require joint assessment and revision. Ordinary persistence does not
establish UEF qualification, and token continuity cannot establish its own
closure or membership premises.

The physical proposal is that the conditions qualifying and individuating the
continuing UEF operation also determine its constitutive scope and ownership
boundary. Its adequacy remains open. Calling a collection one operation,
placing its variables in a common vector or enclosing it within an anatomical
surface does not establish those conditions.

The differentiated contents do not first exist as subjects or self-standing
experiences. They are ways in which one experiential regime is organized.
Relations among contents may structure that field, but they do not create its
unity.

This blocks both an aggregation model and a regress in which a further subject,
container, or binding operation must unify already experiential parts.

There is no further phenomenal assembly after qualification and individuation.
That does not mean unity has no explanatory work. The physical account must
explain what makes this one operation, which differentiated activities belong
to it, and how its organization continues through change. The preceding
structural account carries this work into qualification and individuation;
the identity consequence does not discharge it by stipulation. Any additional
unity condition requires a specific physical and explanatory justification.


## Relation to the Bayne-Chalmers Taxonomy

### Subject Unity

States are subject-unified when they belong to the same subject. This is useful
as a classification after the subject has been individuated, but it cannot
explain IER unity. If one begins by saying that several states belong to the
same subject, the relevant oneness has already been assumed.

IER therefore does not define a UEF as whatever belongs to one subject. It
identifies the subject with the token UEF operation and must individuate that
operation physically without presupposing the subject it is meant to explain.


### Phenomenal and Subsumptive Unity

Bayne and Chalmers characterize substantive phenomenal unity through a total
phenomenal state that subsumes the subject's simultaneous phenomenal states.
This captures the target intuition that there is something it is like to have
the differentiated contents together.

IER accepts the target but does not require the phenomenal mereology. The UEF
is not a container holding smaller experiences, and its contents need not be
independently existing phenomenal states that become parts of a larger state.
The closest IER counterpart to a total phenomenal state is the differentiated
operation of the UEF itself.


### Access Unity

States are access-unified when their contents are jointly available to the same
reporting, reasoning, or action-guiding systems. Access unity can fail while
phenomenal unity remains an open possibility. Conversely, global availability
or coordinated report can occur without establishing a UEF.

IER therefore permits no inference in either direction:

```text
access unity      -/-> experiential unity
access disunity   -/-> experiential disunity
```

Access evidence may constrain an empirical account of a candidate system, but
it is not the identity condition.


### Objectual, Spatial, Representational, and Attentional Unity

Contents within one field may concern the same object, occupy one represented
space, combine into a single representation, or compete for attention. These
are forms of organization within experience. None is coextensive with
experiential unity.

A pain and a visual experience can belong to one field without presenting one
object. A field can remain unified while attention is divided. Representational
integration can also occur in a non-experiential system. These forms must
therefore remain distinct from regime unity.


## Unity Is Not Binding

IER uses *binding* for frontier relevance: a process is binding when its state
affects the admissible continuation of the relevant regime. Perceptual feature
binding concerns the correct association of represented features. Neural or
computational binding concerns mechanisms of coordination.

None of these meanings is identical to experiential unity.

Binding may describe how differentiated content is organized within a UEF or
how a physical system realizes coordinated behavior. Unity concerns why the
relevant operation is one experiential field at all. A mechanism of binding may
enable or alter unity without defining it.


## Unity Without Homogeneity

Unity does not require sameness among contents or processes.

A UEF may contain:

* distinct sensory modalities
* incompatible tendencies
* uneven participation
* local specialization
* conflict, ambiguity, and unresolved tension
* differential access and reportability.

These differences alter the organization and content of the field. They do not
constitute multiple fields merely by being different. Unity concerns the
categorical scope of the regime; homogeneity concerns similarity within it.

Participation can therefore vary by degree while experiential unity remains
categorical. A process may participate weakly, strongly, intermittently, or not
at all. A participating process does not become a smaller subject inside the
field.


## Synchronic and Diachronic Unity

The philosophical literature distinguishes synchronic unity from diachronic
unity. IER accepts the distinction but does not treat synchronic unity as a
dimensionless instant.

### Synchronic Unity

Synchronic unity concerns the oneness of the differentiated field at a given
phase of UEF operation. Because a UEF is temporally extended, the relevant
present is an organized frontier condition, not a mathematical point or a
container in which instantaneous experiences are collected.


### Diachronic Continuity

Diachronic continuity concerns whether successive phases belong to the same
continuing UEF operation. It depends on regime continuity, not memory,
narrative consistency, qualitative sameness, or retrospective self-ascription.

The accepted stage-indexed relation is `Adj_dia(a,b)`. It requires continuous
closure, operational successor production, typed consequence inheritance,
operation-mediated change, and unique two-sided continuation. Ordinary
successor realization need not involve fresh collapse at each stage, and
synchronic closure alone does not establish this diachronic relation.

Unity and continuity must therefore remain distinct:

* unity asks whether this is one field
* continuity asks whether this is the same continuing field across change.

A field may change radically while remaining one continuing field. If the UEF
genuinely ceases, narrative or biological continuity cannot retrospectively
make a later UEF numerically identical to it.


## Unity, Subjecthood, and Counting

Under the IER identity claim, one token UEF is one experiential subject. This
does not mean that subject unity explains experiential unity. Both are
consequences of identifying experience and subjecthood with one bounded UEF
operation.

Nor does unity by itself solve the boundary problem. Physical overlap,
coordination, or causal connection does not determine whether candidate
processes belong to one UEF, several UEFs, or none. Each candidate regime must
be individuated using the full canonical structure. Participation in one UEF
does not make a local process an additional subject, and the existence of a
lower-scale UEF does not by itself create or exclude a higher-scale UEF.

Unity therefore constrains subject counting only after the relevant regime has
been non-circularly individuated.


## Apparent Disunity and Split-Brain Cases

Behavioral conflict, divided access, disrupted report, and hemispheric
specialization do not by themselves decide whether experiential unity has
failed. They may indicate access disunity without establishing phenomenal
disunity.

IER states the conditional structure:

* if one coherent UEF operation persists, there is one experiential field
  despite differentiated or divided access
* if one branch has the unique qualifying two-sided continuation, that branch
  may continue the predecessor while another later operation begins
* if a symmetric fission leaves no unique qualifying continuation, the
  predecessor ends and any later qualifying operations are new token fields
* if no candidate satisfies the UEF conditions, unity language does not create
  experience by attribution.

Which description applies to a particular split-brain case is an empirical and
individuation question. This article supplies no diagnosis.

IER also treats unity as categorical at the level of a token UEF. Reports of
partial unity may track partial access, unstable boundaries, overlapping
candidate descriptions, or a genuine transition between regimes. They cannot
be mapped directly onto fractional subjecthood without further argument.


## What Experiential Unity Is Not

Experiential unity is not:

* ordinary causal interaction
* statistical dependence or mutual information
* synchronization or oscillatory coherence
* shared representation or global broadcast
* behavioral coordination
* common embodiment
* narrative identity or autobiographical memory
* consistency among beliefs or contents
* an observer's decision to treat a system as one
* a sum, collection, or fusion of prior experiences.

These features may accompany, support, express, or fail to track a UEF. None is
substitutable for regime unity.


## Explanatory Limits and Remaining Burdens

The persistence-first order locates unity's physical work within qualification
and individuation. It does not remove that work or complete the physical account.

IER still owes:

* an account of how activity produces or maintains constitutive organization,
  distinguished from changing continuation conditions and passive persistence
* sufficiently precise physical individuation of candidate UEF boundaries
* evidence that the proposed intrinsic-constraint structure is instantiated in
  concrete systems
* an empirical account of difficult division, overlap, interruption, and
  resumption cases
* detailed correspondence between variations in physical organization and
  variations in phenomenal organization
* an abductive defense of the identity claim against the objection that
  the proposed physical organization is merely integration rather than phenomenal
  unity.

The identity claim terminates a demand for an additional producer of unity. It
does not make the identity self-evident, convert structural description into a
deduction of phenomenology, or license unity to be introduced by stipulation.


## Summary

IER's account can be compressed as follows:

* coherence remains the fourth order of information
* experiential unity is the oneness of one differentiated experiential field
* regime unity is the structural articulation of that oneness in IER
* the persistence-first order carries the physical work on organization,
  scope, ownership, membership and continuity into qualification and individuation
* unity is constituted at the level of the UEF, not assembled from experiences
* access, binding, representation, synchronization, and common subject
  attribution do not establish unity
* unity is categorical while content and participation remain differentiated
* synchronic unity and diachronic continuity are related but distinct
* one UEF is one subject, once the UEF has been non-circularly individuated
* physical integration does not silently substitute for phenomenal unity.


## External Orientation

The distinctions among phenomenal, access, representational, objectual,
spatial, attentional, mereological, and subject unity are surveyed in the
[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on the unity of
consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness-unity/).

The distinction between trivial subject unity and substantive phenomenal or
subsumptive unity follows Tim Bayne and David Chalmers,
[“What Is the Unity of Consciousness?”](https://consc.net/papers/unity.html).
These sources provide the external taxonomy. They do not supply authority for
the IER identity claim or its physical individuation conditions.
