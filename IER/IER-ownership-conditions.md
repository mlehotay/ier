## Ownership Is Invariant

Under IER:

* Intrinsic constraint is borne at a single history — future boundary.
* Collapse is atomic and exclusive.
* All collapse at the frontier is owned.

Ownership does not scale.
Ownership does not weaken.
Ownership does not admit degrees.

If collapse occurs at a Unified Experiential Field (UEF), it is borne there.

There are not two kinds of collapse:

* not “internal” collapse
* not “external” collapse

There is only:

> Atomic collapse at an owned frontier.

What varies is not ownership itself.

What varies is:

> The deformation lineage shaping the geometry in which collapse occurs.

Frontier geometry is never uncaused.

* Prior atomic collapses within the same UEF.

* Ongoing coupling with other systems and environments.

All of these lawfully contribute to present fragility geometry.

No additional field is required.

Only:

* atomic collapse
* welded deformation
* propagation
* continuous traversal

Ownership conditions describe how this lineage distributes.

## Internally Dominated Foreclosure

In some collapse events:

* Present frontier geometry is shaped predominantly by prior atomic collapses within the same UEF.
* Fragility gradients reflect long-horizon internal deformation.
* External coupling influences but does not dominate geometry.

Collapse remains fully determined.

Ownership remains invariant.

But deformation lineage is primarily internal.

Structurally:

> Present admissible-future geometry depends chiefly on prior owned collapse within the same regime.

Phenomenologically, such cases often correspond to:

* strong authorship
* coherent self-boundary
* high integration
* “I did this”

No chooser is involved.

Only internal deformation dominance.

## Externally Dominated Foreclosure

In other collapse events:

* Present frontier geometry is sharply reshaped by strong external coupling.
* Switching costs are rapidly altered by imposed constraint.
* Fragility asymmetry reflects deformation lineage heavily influenced by external systems.

Collapse remains atomic.
Ownership remains intact.

But deformation lineage is predominantly external.

Structurally:

> Present admissible-future geometry depends primarily on deformation introduced through external coupling rather than prior internally welded exclusion.

Phenomenologically, such cases may correspond to:

* coercion
* invasion
* constraint imposition
* “this happened to me”

Ownership does not disappear.

Authorship lineage shifts.

## Mixed Regimes

Most collapse events occur in mixed regimes.

Frontier geometry is shaped by:

* sedimented internal collapse lineage
* external coupling
* ongoing propagation

Ownership phenomenology reflects:

> The relative dominance of internal versus external deformation lineage in shaping collapse geometry.

This is not a quantitative scale.

It is a structural configuration of lineage within fragility geometry.
