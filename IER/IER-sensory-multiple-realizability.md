# Sensory Multiple Realizability

## Different Channels, Shared Constraint Modes

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.5)**\
*Tier-2 · Explanatory · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This article is explanatory and non-normative.

It introduces:

- no new ontological primitives
- no new criteria for experience
- no sensory diagnostics
- no claim that any particular magnetoreceptive organism is experiential
- no claim that sensory channels determine phenomenal kinds
- no claim that coarse structural similarity establishes phenomenal identity

Its purpose is to clarify a consequence of the IER identity claim:

> Different physical sensory channels may instantiate the same experiential organization, while similar sensory channels may instantiate different experiential organizations.

The argument extends the treatment of multiple realizability already developed in IER.

IER identifies experience with the organization of intrinsic constraint within a Unified Experiential Field, not with any particular biological implementation.

This article applies that distinction specifically to sensory organization.


## Abstract

External referent, transduction, UEF-relative constraint organization, and
phenomenology have distinct roles. Different causes can share a constraint
mode, and one cause can participate in different modes as organization changes.
Coarse mode equivalence does not establish complete phenomenal identity.
Phenomenal organization consequently does not disclose the intrinsic nature
of the external referent or supply an inverse map to its ultimate constitution.

## Orientation: The Assumption of Sensory Uniqueness

Sensory systems are usually classified by how they couple an organism to its environment.

Examples include:

- vision
- audition
- touch
- olfaction
- taste
- electroreception
- magnetoreception

This encourages a natural but unwarranted inference:

```text
different sensory channel
→ different kind of experience
```

Vision is therefore assumed to produce visual phenomenology.

Hearing is assumed to produce auditory phenomenology.

A hypothetical magnetoreceptive subject is then imagined as having a distinctively magnetic phenomenology unavailable to humans.

Under Informational Experiential Realism, this inference does not follow.

A sensory channel describes how objective physical structure enters into the dynamics of a system.

Phenomenology, by contrast, is identified with the organization of intrinsic constraint within a Unified Experiential Field.

These are different explanatory levels.

A novel transduction mechanism need not produce a novel experiential organization.

Conversely, the same transduction mechanism may participate in many different experiential organizations.

The sensory channel does not determine the phenomenal kind.


## Four Structures That Must Be Distinguished

Sensory discussions often collapse several distinct structures into one.

IER requires them to remain separate.

### External referent

The external referent is the objective physical structure to which the system is coupled.

Examples include:

- electromagnetic radiation
- pressure waves
- mechanical deformation
- chemical concentration
- geomagnetic orientation

These structures belong to objective physical reality.

They exist independently of whether they are experienced.


### Transduction mechanism

A transduction mechanism is the physical organization through which an external distinction alters the system.

Examples may include:

- retinal phototransduction
- cochlear mechanics
- mechanoreception
- chemoreception
- magnetosome-mediated coupling

These mechanisms are implementation-level structures.

They determine how an environmental difference becomes physically consequential for the system.

They do not by themselves determine whether the resulting organization is experiential.


### Constraint organization

Once a perturbation participates in the system, it may reorganize:

- participation
- admissible continuation
- salience
- switching cost
- urgency
- attraction
- conflict
- narrowing
- stabilization
- binding

This is the level at which IER describes experiential organization.

Different sensory mechanisms may converge on similar or identical constraint organizations.

The same sensory mechanism may also participate in very different organizations under different conditions.


### Phenomenology

Under IER, phenomenology is not added after constraint organization occurs.

Where a Unified Experiential Field exists:

> the experiential character is identical to the relevant organization of intrinsic constraint.

Thus the structure is not:

```text
external stimulus
→ neural mechanism
→ constraint organization
→ representation
→ phenomenology
```

The IER form is instead:

```text
external structure
→ physical coupling
→ UEF-relative intrinsic constraint organization
= experiential character
```

The final equality is the identity claim.


## Multiple Realizability as the Precedent

IER already accepts multiple realizability.

The classical objection to identity theories assumes that a mental state must be identical to a particular mechanism.

If pain were identical to one specific neural state, then a system lacking that state could not instantiate pain.

IER rejects that form of identity.

Experience is not identified with:

- neurons
- cortical regions
- particular sensory organs
- specific computational mechanisms
- behavioral roles

It is identified with physical organization.

Different systems may therefore instantiate similar or identical intrinsic-constraint organizations through radically different implementations.

Multiple realizability is not an exception to IER.

It follows naturally from an organizational identity theory.


## Pain as the Canonical Example

Pain provides the clearest existing example.

Under IER, pain is not identical to:

- tissue damage
- nociception
- C-fiber activity
- one sensory pathway
- one biological mechanism

Pain is identified organizationally with:

- escalating intrinsic constraint
- conflicting participation demands
- narrowing admissible futures
- increasing urgency of resolution

A burn may instantiate this organization.

A mechanical injury may instantiate it.

Neuropathic activity may instantiate it.

A physically different organism may instantiate it through another mechanism entirely.

If the relevant experiential organization is instantiated, the experience is pain regardless of the implementation.

The important relation is therefore:

```text
different physical causes
        ↓
different implementations
        ↓
same relevant intrinsic-constraint organization
        ↓
same experiential kind
```

This already shows that external cause does not fix phenomenal identity.

Sensory multiple realizability extends the same principle to sensory channels.


## Sexual Salience as a Second Constraint Mode

Sexual salience provides a different kind of example.

The relevant IER account does not define sexual distraction as:

- knowledge
- symbolic interpretation
- desire
- intention
- explicit evaluation

Instead, certain visual configurations can produce steep recruitment gradients.

These may involve:

- involuntary orienting
- rapid participation recruitment
- attentional capture
- narrowing of competing affordances
- increased switching cost
- persistent or recurrent salience

The experience is structured by constraint dynamics rather than by a stored proposition such as:

```text
this object means sex
```

The system is instead recruited into a particular continuation organization.

The Alexandra salience example strengthens this point.

Repeated salience can persist even when novelty is absent and the relevant visual information is already familiar.

Thus:

```text
salience
is not
novelty
```

and:

```text
participation recruitment
is not
information acquisition
```

A stimulus may remain strongly recruitable simply because the active organization continues to support steep participation gradients and local affordance narrowing.

This provides a candidate constraint mode:

> salience capture through steep participation recruitment.


## Constraint Modes

A constraint mode is not a new ontological primitive.

The term can be used descriptively for a recurring organization of intrinsic constraint that may appear across different contents, sensory channels, and implementations.

Examples may include:

- capture
- narrowing
- escalation
- attraction
- conflict
- orientation
- stabilization
- binding
- release

These labels describe organizational patterns.

They do not specify:

- external referent
- receptor type
- anatomical pathway
- representational content

A constraint mode therefore cuts across ordinary sensory classifications.

The same mode may occur through vision, audition, touch, chemical coupling, or another channel.

Conversely, one sensory channel may support many different modes.


## Magnetoreception as a Structural Thought Experiment

Magnetoreception provides a useful thought experiment because its external referent is unfamiliar from the standpoint of ordinary human sensory experience.

Suppose a physical system is coupled to geomagnetic orientation through some magnetoreceptive mechanism.

The ordinary question is:

> What does magnetic north feel like?

That framing assumes that an unfamiliar environmental variable must generate a correspondingly unfamiliar phenomenal quality.

IER suggests a different question:

> What intrinsic-constraint organization does magnetoreceptive coupling instantiate within the experiential system?

Possible organizations might include:

- directional attraction
- orienting capture
- resistance to deviation
- ease of continuation along one direction
- narrowing of viable alternatives
- persistent directional salience
- stabilization around alignment

None of these possibilities is asserted here as an empirical fact.

The point is only structural:

> magnetoreception does not, by itself, entail a unique magnetic phenomenal kind.

If magnetoreceptive coupling instantiates an organization already present elsewhere, the resulting phenomenology may not be novel.


## North and Sexual Salience

Consider two radically different external conditions.

In one case:

```text
geomagnetic field
→ magnetoreceptive coupling
→ organization O
```

In another:

```text
sexual visual stimulus
→ visual and bodily coupling
→ organization O
```

The external referents are different.

The sensory mechanisms are different.

The histories of the systems may be different.

Yet the resulting organization \(O\) could in principle be structurally equivalent.

Suppose both instantiate:

- rapid orienting
- steep participation recruitment
- narrowing of competing continuations
- increased switching cost
- persistent attraction toward a direction or source

Then the two cases may instantiate the same constraint mode.

This does not mean magnetic north is sexual.

It does not mean sexual salience is magnetic.

Those descriptions belong to the external referent and interpretive content.

The shared structure would lie at the level of UEF-relative organization.

The provocative possibility is therefore:

> magnetic north and sexual salience could instantiate the same experiential mode despite having unrelated external causes.

This is a direct sensory extension of multiple realizability.


## Same Mode Does Not Automatically Mean Same Phenomenology

A crucial distinction is required.

Two systems may share a coarse structural motif without sharing their complete constitutive organization.

For example:

```text
orientation
→ recruitment
→ narrowing
```

may occur in:

- attraction
- fear
- curiosity
- pain
- startle
- magnetoreception

This common motif does not by itself establish phenomenal identity.

IER must distinguish at least three levels.

### Shared motif

The systems exhibit one similar local organization.

Example:

```text
attention capture
```

### Shared regime structure

The systems share a richer organization involving multiple interacting constraint relations.

### Complete constitutive equivalence

The relevant organization of intrinsic constraint is fully equivalent at the level constitutive of phenomenology.

Only the strongest case would support phenomenal identity under the IER identity claim.

Thus:

```text
same graph shape
does not guarantee
same experience
```

Topological equivalence can be coarse.

Phenomenal identity requires equivalence at the constitutive level.


## Sensory Taxonomy and Constraint Taxonomy

Traditional sensory classification groups experience according to external coupling mechanisms.

A simplified sensory taxonomy looks like:

```text
vision
hearing
touch
taste
smell
magnetoreception
electroreception
```

IER allows a second, cross-cutting classification based on experiential organization:

```text
capture
orientation
narrowing
escalation
binding
conflict
release
attraction
```

These two taxonomies need not align one-to-one.

A single constraint mode may be instantiated through multiple sensory channels.

A single sensory channel may participate in multiple constraint modes.

Illustratively:

| Constraint mode | Visual | Auditory | Somatic | Chemical | Magnetic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| salience capture | possible | possible | possible | possible | possible |
| orientation | possible | possible | possible | possible | possible |
| escalation | possible | possible | possible | possible | possible |
| attraction | possible | possible | possible | possible | possible |
| narrowing | possible | possible | possible | possible | possible |

This table is schematic only.

It introduces no diagnostic claim.

Its purpose is to make clear that sensory channels classify implementation, while constraint modes classify organization.


## Sensory Novelty Does Not Entail Phenomenal Novelty

The central result follows directly.

A novel sensory channel may expose a system to an environmental variable unavailable through its existing coupling mechanisms.

But that does not imply that the resulting experiential organization is new.

Thus:

```text
sensory novelty
does not entail
phenomenal novelty
```

A magnetoreceptive animal might therefore instantiate:

- a genuinely novel experiential organization,
- a familiar constraint mode through an unfamiliar channel,
- or a combination of familiar and unfamiliar organizations.

IER does not determine which possibility obtains.

The theory only blocks the inference from novel channel to novel phenomenal kind.

The converse also follows.

The same sensory mechanism may produce radically different experiential organizations under different conditions.

Thus:

```text
sensory sameness
does not entail
phenomenal sameness
```

A visual input may participate in:

- attraction
- pain-related urgency
- fear
- curiosity
- orientation
- boredom
- recognition
- neutral continuation

depending on the organization of the UEF.

The channel does not determine the mode.


## Experiential Worlds and Objective Reality

This distinction has consequences for experiential worlds.

Objective physical reality contains more structure than any one experiential system can couple into.

A system interacts only with the distinctions that can participate in its own dynamics.

Different systems therefore have access to different system-relative regions of objective structure.

A magnetoreceptive organism may be dynamically sensitive to distinctions that play no corresponding role in ordinary human experiential organization.

A human may be sensitive to other distinctions that are irrelevant to that organism.

This does not imply multiple objective realities.

There remains one physical world.

But experiential worlds differ because different environmental distinctions participate in different UEF-relative constraint organizations.

The relation can be written schematically:

```text
objective reality
        ↓
system-specific coupling
        ↓
system-relative constraint organization
        ↓
experiential world, if UEF
```

The external world is richer than any experiential world.

Yet experiential worlds may reuse the same local constraint modes across radically different couplings.


## The Brain-in-a-Vat Consequence

A brain-in-a-vat scenario makes the same principle visible from another direction.

Suppose two different external arrangements sustain the same complete
relevant constitutive organization within a UEF, including physically effective
history, owned boundary, and dynamics. This is a conditional identity claim,
not a claim that matching selected observables or a coarse mode suffices.

One might involve:

```text
real object
→ ordinary sensory coupling
→ organization O
```

The other:

```text
simulation
→ artificial stimulation
→ organization O
```

If the complete constitutive organization is preserved, the difference in external provenance does not require a phenomenal difference.

Thus:

```text
different provenance
→ same constitutive organization
→ same phenomenology
```

Sensory multiple realizability makes a parallel point:

```text
different transduction channels
→ same constitutive organization
→ same phenomenology
```

The two cases differ in where implementation changes occur.

The structural lesson is the same.

Phenomenology follows organization, not semantic origin.


## External Referent Does Not Fix Phenomenal Kind

This blocks several intuitive mappings.

IER does not require:

```text
magnetic field
→ magnetic quale
```

or:

```text
tissue damage
→ pain quale
```

or:

```text
sexual stimulus
→ sexual quale
```

The external referent is not itself the experiential category.

Instead:

```text
external referent
→ physical coupling
→ intrinsic-constraint organization
= phenomenology
```

Different referents may converge on the same organization.

The same referent may generate different organizations.

Phenomenal type therefore cannot be inferred directly from stimulus type.


## Content and Mode

This allows a useful distinction between content-level descriptions and organizational descriptions.

Content-level descriptions include:

- breast
- north
- burn
- voice
- face
- odor
- pressure

Constraint-mode descriptions include:

- capture
- attraction
- narrowing
- urgency
- orientation
- conflict
- release

Content describes what the experience is about, how it is interpreted, or which external structure participates in it.

Mode describes how intrinsic constraint is organized.

The distinction is not absolute.

Content itself must be physically instantiated within the UEF.

But the distinction is explanatorily useful because identical modes may occur across different contents.

Thus:

> Different contents may share experiential organization.

And:

> Similar contents may participate in different experiential organizations.


## Reframing Alien Experience

Questions about unfamiliar sensory systems are often framed as inaccessible phenomenal mysteries.

For example:

> What is it like to sense magnetic north?

IER does not eliminate the epistemic difficulty.

But it changes the question.

Instead of assuming a unique unknown quale, the research problem becomes:

> What UEF-relative intrinsic-constraint organization would magnetoreceptive coupling instantiate, and does that organization occur elsewhere?

Possible outcomes include:

1. the organization is genuinely novel;
2. it partially overlaps familiar human modes;
3. it closely matches an existing human experiential organization;
4. it differs in some dimensions while sharing others.

The existence of an unfamiliar receptor or sensory pathway does not decide among these possibilities.


## Comparative Phenomenology as Structural Comparison

This suggests a broader programme for comparative phenomenology.

Rather than classifying experience only by sensory organs or stimulus type, systems may be compared through:

- continuation structure
- participation recruitment
- narrowing dynamics
- switching cost
- binding structure
- attractor organization
- escalation
- release
- temporal persistence

A human and a nonhuman organism may therefore be:

- implementation-dissimilar but organizationally similar,
- implementation-similar but organizationally different,
- partially equivalent in one constraint mode and different in others.

This follows the same logic already accepted by IER for pain and multiple realizability.


## Epistemic Limits

Sensory multiple realizability does not make alien experience externally transparent.

It does not imply that:

- similar behavior establishes similar phenomenology,
- similar receptors establish similar phenomenology,
- shared topology at one descriptive level establishes full equivalence,
- subjective reports can reveal implementation identity,
- human analogy provides diagnostic access.

IER's existing non-diagnostic commitments remain intact.

The argument establishes only a structural possibility:

> different channels can realize the same experiential organization.

Whether they actually do in any particular case is a separate empirical and theoretical problem.


## Relationship to Pain and Salience

Pain demonstrates that a single experiential organization can be instantiated through different mechanisms.

Sexual salience demonstrates that participation recruitment and affordance narrowing can persist independently of novelty or explicit informational content.

Together they support a broader principle.

Pain shows:

```text
different causes
→ same experiential regime
```

Sexual salience shows:

```text
same familiar content
→ persistent recruitment without new information
```

Sensory multiple realizability combines these lessons:

```text
different sensory causes
+
different transduction mechanisms
→ potentially same experiential constraint mode
```

The phenomenal organization does not belong intrinsically to the stimulus category.

It belongs to the UEF organization that bears the resulting constraint.


## Compression

Sensory channels classify how objective physical structure becomes coupled into a system.

Phenomenal kinds classify how that structure is organized within a Unified Experiential Field.

Because physical implementation and experiential organization need not vary together:

```text
new sensory channel
does not entail
new phenomenal kind
```

and:

```text
same sensory channel
does not entail
same phenomenal kind
```

A magnetoreceptive system might instantiate a genuinely unfamiliar experiential organization.

It might also instantiate a constraint mode already present in human experience.

Magnetic orientation and sexual visual salience could, in principle, share a mode such as directional recruitment, attraction, narrowing, or persistent salience while remaining completely different in external referent and physical implementation.

The important question is therefore not:

> What should this unfamiliar sense feel like?

It is:

> What intrinsic-constraint organization does this coupling instantiate?

Under IER, that organization is where phenomenology resides.

## Epistemic Consequence: No Access to External Intrinsic Nature

The four-role distinction blocks inference from phenomenal mode to an external
referent's categorical intrinsic constitution. Phenomenology is UEF-relative
organization under the identity claim; it is not an inspection of external
intrinsic nature.

Different-cause/same-mode cases show that a mode need not pick out one external
cause. Same-cause/different-mode cases show that organization, current context,
and effective history mediate how a cause participates. These are structural
possibilities, not evidence that any named organisms have identical experiences.

Shared urgency, attraction, or another coarse mode does not establish complete
phenomenal identity. Even complete relevant organizational equivalence would
not turn phenomenal character into privileged knowledge of external stuff.
Sensory access can be informative without carrying a correctness signal or
epistemic authority. `IER-reality.md` preserves one-world realism;
`IER-russellian-monism.md` separates this limitation from the external demand
for a categorical ground.

## Intermission - Structural Fact

> A new way for the world to enter a system need not create a new way for experience to be organized.

The world may enter through different doors and still take the same shape inside.
