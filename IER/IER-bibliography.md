# Learning IER: A Guided Annotated Reading List

## A Short Route into the Theory and Its Philosophical Questions

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.6)**\
*T2 · Reading Support · Non-Normative · Non-Criterial*

## Purpose and Use

This guide is for readers entering IER or philosophy of mind. Follow the short
route first; use the later branches only when a question interests you. Each
step explains what to read, what it helps you understand and how it prepares
the next step. You need not finish a general consciousness bibliography before
reading IER.

A *physicalist identity proposal* says that an experience and a physical
occurrence are one occurrence. A *subject* is whose experience it is; the
proposal need not add a separate bearer behind that occurrence. A *process*
continues through time and can change while continuing. Those distinctions,
rather than familiarity with technical vocabulary, are the starting point.

The [Specification](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-specification.md) controls IER's normative commitments;
canonical definition owners control their physical relations. This list adds
no theory or empirical attribution rule. Readers already comfortable with the
debates can instead use [IER in Philosophical Context: A Reading Map](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/apps/philosophy/IER-philosophy-of-mind-curriculum.md)
to enter by argument, precedent or objection.

## The Short Reading Route

### 1. Meet the Proposal in IER's Own Words

Begin with the [Quick Start](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-quick-start.md), then the [Précis](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-precis.md).
Use the [Specification](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-specification.md) to check the identity commitment,
UEF conditions and inference limits as questions arise; a first reading need
not master every definition.

The distinction to carry forward is between a candidate physical system,
its operation, its changing contents and the subject. IER proposes one
qualifying continuing operation as total experience and subject. It does not
infer experience from integration, feedback, complexity or a compelling analogy.

For physical intuition, read the [Stapled Net collection](https://philpeople.org/profiles/michael-lehotay/teaching_materials)
or [Billowed Sail](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/examples/analogies/billowed-sail.md). Motion under inherited
constraints can continue without a new irreversible event at every moment.
Neither example establishes experience. Hello World is another schematic
illustration with explicit current-use limits, rather than a completed physical
instantiation of the theory.

### 2. Understand What an Identity Claim Says

Read U. T. Place, “Is Consciousness a Brain Process?” (1956), *British Journal
of Psychology* 47(1), 44–50. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8295.1956.tb00560.x).
Read for the difference between identifying two descriptions of one occurrence
and merely discovering a correlation. This prepares you to ask which occurrence
IER proposes, rather than assume that physicalism already supplies the answer.

Then read J. J. C. Smart, “Sensations and Brain Processes” (1959), *The
Philosophical Review* 68(2), 141–156. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2182164).
Notice why two descriptions can differ in meaning without referring to two
things. That possibility does not establish that a particular pair actually
co-refers. If the debate is unfamiliar, consult the
[SEP identity-theory entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-identity/)
before these articles.

### 3. Move from a Conscious State to Its Subject

Read Galen Strawson, “What Is the Relation Between an Experience, the Subject
of the Experience, and the Content of the Experience?” (2003), *Philosophical
Issues* 13, 279–315. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1533-6077.00015).
The central lesson is that the subject need not be an additional owner receiving
experience: subject, experience and physical process may be one occurrence.
This is a direct precedent for the form of IER's proposal. The important
persistence difference concerns Strawson's part/content-sensitive criterion,
not simply a claim that his subjects must be brief.

Consult Bayne and Chalmers, “What Is the Unity of Consciousness?” (2003),
[author text](https://consc.net/papers/unity.html), or the
[SEP unity entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness-unity/), to
separate unity of experience from coordination or integration. The next step
asks how one subject could continue while its constituents change.

### 4. Understand Continuity through Change

Start with Thomas Pradeu, “Genidentity and Biological Processes” (2018), in
*Everything Flows*, 96–112. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0005).
*Genidentity* concerns identity through a history of transformations. Use it to
see why replacing matter need not automatically replace a process.

James DiFrisco, “Biological Processes: Criteria of Identity and Persistence”
(2018), in *Everything Flows*, 76–95,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0004), is a useful next reading
for separating identification at one stage from persistence between stages.
Return to [IER Continuity](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-continuity.md): its produced-and-inherited
relation includes continuous owned closure and unique qualifying continuation
in both directions. Recurrence, memory and retained capacity alone do not
establish that relation. Biological or personal continuity can answer different
questions from numerical identity of an experiential subject.

### 5. See What Organization and History Contribute

Read the constraint-dependence and closure definitions, then the openness and
changing-organization discussion in Montévil and Mossio, “Biological Organisation
as Closure of Constraints” (2015), *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 372, 179–191.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029).
The reviewed copy was a 2016 author manuscript with different pagination;
published-edition equivalence was not independently audited. Select by section
and subject matter rather than transfer PDF page numbers between editions.

This reading makes reciprocal maintenance of constraints intelligible.
That relation differs from IER's non-product outgoing successor support;
biological closure alone does not establish experience. For a further step,
read DiFrisco and Mossio, “Diachronic Identity in Complex Life Cycles: An
Organizational Perspective” (2020), in *Biological Identity*.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781351066389-10).
Its productive transitions and fission/fusion cases clarify what effective
history can do. The reviewed copy was the 2019 manuscript; published-edition
equivalence was not verified. Neither source supplies IER's complete subject rule.

### 6. Keep the Identity Burden Open

Read Joseph Levine, “Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap” (1983),
*Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 64, 354–361.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.1983.tb00207.x).
A physical candidate can be precisely described while a question remains about
why to identify it with experience. *Qualitative character* means what an
experience is like; a structural description is not automatically a complete
account of particular qualities.

Chalmers, “Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness” (1995), *Journal of
Consciousness Studies* 2(3), 200–219,
[author text](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html), develops a related challenge.
Read it after Levine if you want the broader dispute. IER's proposal remains
abductive and a posteriori: supported, if successful, by defeasible explanatory
fit and evidence rather than a definition proving its own application.

### 7. Compare One Alternative Carefully

Read the introductory aims and the axioms/postulates relationship in
Albantakis et al., “Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0: Formulating the
Properties of Phenomenal Existence in Physical Terms” (2023), *PLoS Computational
Biology*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465).
The technical development can wait until you want to study that comparison.
Ask what each theory proposes as the physical occurrence and how it identifies
its boundaries. Probability-sensitive integration and non-product admissible
support are different objects; IIT composition need not mean summing conscious
microsubjects. This is a versioned comparison, not a ranking or a verdict on all IIT.

For a wider map after this example, consult Seth and Bayne, “Theories of
Consciousness” (2022), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 23, 439–452.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4).
Different programmes may address access, report, subjecthood or qualitative
organization. Compare those targets before treating a difference as a refutation.

### 8. Ask Whether the Physical Description Is Substantive

Read Peter M. Ainsworth, “Newman's Objection” (2009), *British Journal for the
Philosophy of Science*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axn051).
The practical question for IER is whether physical variables and relations are
independently justified, rather than selected because an abstract graph fits
a desired experience. The reading map supplies the original Newman route and
more detailed individuation objections.

You can now return to the IER sources with four separate questions: what the
operation is, what makes it one continuing subject, what qualitative relations
are proposed, and what warrants experiential identity. Their unresolved parts
are research burdens, not reasons to fill gaps with the teaching analogies.

## Optional Reading Branches

Choose a branch for a question raised by the short route. The references below
extend existing reading support; none is an additional prerequisite. The
philosophical reading map supplies further argument-level contrasts and links.

### Physical monism and identity rather than correlation

Feigl, H. (1958). *The “mental” and the “physical”*. In H. Feigl, M. Scriven, & G. Maxwell (Eds.), *Minnesota studies in the philosophy of science, Vol. 2: Concepts, theories, and the mind-body problem* (pp. 370-497). University of Minnesota Press. Feigl supplies the broadest philosophical architecture for psychophysical identity, especially the refusal of dualist “danglers.” IER readers will find this useful as the most systematic classical statement of why identity is explanatorily cleaner than bridge-law dualism.

Lewis, D. (1966). *An argument for the identity theory*. *The Journal of Philosophy, 63*(1), 17-25. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2024559). Lewis matters because he helps move identity theory into dialogue with multiple realizability and more flexible realization talk. He is adjacent rather than fully convergent with IER, but essential for understanding why a strict identity view now needs a more organizational rather than merely local relatum.

Armstrong, D. M. (1968). *A materialist theory of the mind*. Routledge & Kegan Paul. Armstrong systematizes Australian materialism and is especially useful for readers interested in “central-state” physicalism. IER differs sharply from Armstrong’s more state-centered and functionalized picture, but the book remains one of the clearest attempts to make physicalism do full ontological work.

Papineau, D. (2002). *Thinking about consciousness*. Oxford University Press. perhaps the single most useful contemporary book for readers who want a serious physicalism without eliminativist rhetoric. Papineau is especially valuable for IER because he treats the conceptual resistance to identity as a feature of our concepts rather than evidence against physical monism.

### Subject identity, organizational closure and productive history

Papineau, D. (2006). *Comments on Galen Strawson: “Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism”.* *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 13(10-11), 100-109. [Author text](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/papineau_realistic_monism.pdf). Physical identity and causal arguments allow complex physical consciousness without experiential simples. This does not supply the complete continuing-subject relation.

Ashby, W. R. (1968). *Variety, Constraint, and the Law of Requisite Variety.* In W. Buckley (ed.), *Modern Systems Research for the Behavioral Scientist*, 129-136. [Sourcebook record](https://www.routledge.com/Systems-Research-for-Behavioral-Science-A-Sourcebook/Buckley/p/book/9780202362809). Consulted through the 2011 reprint, 200-207, drawing from the 1956 cybernetics book. Alternatives and restricted variety are established resources; observer-relative distinguishability is not IER's physical primitive.

Di Paolo, E. A. (2005). *Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency.* *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 4, 429-452. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y). Distinguishes self-production, viability and adaptive regulation; historically effective organization is a positive comparison, without a consciousness attribution from agency alone.

Bich, L., Mossio, M., Ruiz-Mirazo, K., and Moreno, A. (2016; online 2015). *Biological Regulation: Controlling the System from Within.* *Biology & Philosophy* 31, 237-265. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-015-9497-8). Endogenous regulation and constitutive organization clarify internal/external roles. The reviewed author manuscript does not establish non-externalizable subject ownership.

Mossio, M., and Pontarotti, G. (2022 issue; online 2019). *Conserving Functions across Generations: Heredity in Light of Biological Organization.* *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 73(1), 249-278. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axz031). The reviewed published issue copy distinguishes constitutive functional heredity from independently stable environmental conditions. Heredity may relate coexisting distinct generations, rather than preserve one numerical subject.

### Ownership, qualities and live objections

Guillot, M. (2017; online 2016). *I Me Mine: on a Confusion Concerning the Subjective Character of Experience.* *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 8, 23-53. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-016-0313-4). Distinguishes subjective relations that should not be conflated with physical bearing. Zahavi/Kriegel's 2016 *For-me-ness* and Metzinger's 2000 representational analysis supply complementary resources; see the curriculum for primary links and reviewed-copy limits.

Loorits, K. (2014). *Structural Qualia: a Solution to the Hard Problem of Consciousness.* *Frontiers in Psychology* 5, 237. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00237). Structural-quality identity is a substantive antecedent, not itself a complete total-subject persistence theory.

Lyre, H. (2022). *Neurophenomenal Structuralism. A Philosophical Agenda for a Structuralist Neuroscience of Consciousness.* *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2022(1), niac012. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niac012). Structural-quality programme with a different target from full operation identity. Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021), *A Structural Constraint on Neural Correlates of Consciousness*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.33735/phimisci.2021.79), provides correspondence constraints without supplying an exhaustive IER quality mapping.

Austin, C. J. (2020). *Organisms, Activity, and Being: On the Substance of Process Ontology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-020-0278-0). Tests whether continuity tracks an independently justified kind or presupposes an already selected individual. DiFrisco (2018; online 2017), *Token Physicalism and Functional Individuation*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-017-0188-y), adds a substantive individuation challenge.

McIntyre, J. H. (2026). *Consciousness Multiplied.* *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 113(2), 465-479. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.70146). Counting and overlap objections remain substantive; naming a maximal scope does not by itself settle them.

Negro, N. (2025). *Three Arguments against Metaphysical Structuralism in Consciousness Research.* *Synthese* 206, 8. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05103-6). A critic of structural-quality accounts, to be addressed on his actual premises rather than dismissed by invoking identity.

Brakel, L. A. W. (2021). *Can Neuroscientists Test a New Physicalist Mind/Body View: DiCoToP (Diachronic Conjunctive Token Physicalism)?* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.786133). Event identity and diachronic content conjunction are positive comparisons, with different relata from one continuing total subject. The predecessor text was not independently reviewed.

### Hard-problem pressure and the anti-mysterian turn

Nagel, T. (1974). *What is it like to be a bat?* *The Philosophical Review, 83*(4), 435-450. still the cleanest statement of why subjectivity resists purely third-person redescription. IER does not follow Nagel into anti-physicalism, but any reader needs Nagel to see what the identity thesis must preserve rather than explain away.

Dennett, D. C. (1991). *Consciousness explained*. Little, Brown. a major anti-mysterian landmark and a necessary foil for IER’s rejection of both Cartesian spectatorship and purely access- or narrative-based accounts. Useful especially for readers comparing IER’s anti-Cartesianism with its refusal to dissolve experience into functional organization alone.

### Unified dynamical regimes, global integration, and major contemporary comparators

Baars, B. J. (1988). *A cognitive theory of consciousness*. Cambridge University Press. the foundational workspace text. Readers should include it not because it supports IER directly, but because it represents the clearest alternative in which consciousness is tied to global availability and coordination rather than to a strict experiential identity condition.

Dehaene, S., Changeux, J.-P., Naccache, L., Sackur, J., & Sergent, C. (2006). *Conscious, preconscious, and subliminal processing: A testable taxonomy*. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 10*(5), 204-211. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2006.03.007). the most efficient statement of the GNW family in article form. It is especially useful for IER readers because it lays out a strong empirical theory of conscious access that IER must distinguish from subjecthood itself.

Dehaene, S., Lau, H., & Kouider, S. (2017). *What is consciousness, and could machines have it?* *Science, 358*(6362), 486-492. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871). a concise mature statement of the GNW-style scientific program, including its attitude toward report, signatures, and machine consciousness. IER readers will likely disagree with its detectability emphasis, which is exactly why it belongs here.

Tononi, G. (2004). *An information integration theory of consciousness*. *BMC Neuroscience, 5*, 42. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2202-5-42). an early IIT formulation identifying consciousness with an integrative property. Distinguish graded quantities from the separate issue of categorical subject existence.

Oizumi, M., Albantakis, L., & Tononi, G. (2014). *From the phenomenology to the mechanisms of consciousness: Integrated information theory 3.0*. *PLoS Computational Biology, 10*(5), e1003588. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588). the best single technical exposition of the phenomenology-to-mechanism move in IIT. Readers interested in how an identity proposal can be built from axioms and postulates should read this alongside IER’s categorical subject qualification, distinguished from graded enabling variables and experiential organization.

Tononi, G., Boly, M., Massimini, M., & Koch, C. (2016). *Integrated information theory: From consciousness to its physical substrate*. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 17*(7), 450-461. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.44). the cleanest mature overview of IIT in a major review venue. Essential for understanding both why IIT is so often treated as the strongest current identity-like theory and how intrinsic causal selection differs from IER’s categorical local subjecthood and independently typed multiscale operations.

Marshall, W., Findlay, G., Albantakis, L., and Tononi, G. (2026). *Intrinsic Units: Identifying a System's Causal Grain.* *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2026(1), niag013. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag013). The reviewed published article extends grain analysis. Its specified noisy four-unit example permits full positive successor support despite reported positive integration. This conditional support comparison does not reproduce integration calculations, prove a global grain optimum, or establish a brain result. Supplements and the precursor remain unreviewed.

### Intrinsic constraint, self-maintenance, embodiment, and temporal continuity

Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and cognition: The realization of the living*. D. Reidel. the baseline text on self-producing and operationally closed systems. It does not identify experience with autopoiesis, but it is a background source on self-production. Autopoiesis and biological self-maintenance are not themselves necessary or sufficient conditions in IER's full relation stack.

Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The embodied mind: Cognitive science and human experience*. MIT Press. foundational for embodied and enactive naturalism, and central to the “new anti-mysterian” family resemblance named in the IER corpus. Readers should use it to understand how bodily organization and lived temporality were reintroduced into consciousness theory without reverting to dualism.

Varela, F. J. (1996). *Neurophenomenology: A methodological remedy for the hard problem*. *Journal of Consciousness Studies, 3*(4), 330-349. crucial for anyone who wants phenomenal seriousness without metaphysical exceptionalism. IER does not adopt Varela’s methodological program wholesale, but it belongs in the same broad anti-dualist, non-dismissive atmosphere.

Thompson, E. (2007). *Mind in life: Biology, phenomenology, and the sciences of mind*. Harvard University Press. a major synthesis of autopoiesis, embodied cognition, and phenomenology. It is probably the single best adjacent book for readers exploring whether a processual, living, self-maintaining account can be pushed closer to a strict identity thesis.

Friston, K. (2010). *The free-energy principle: A unified brain theory?* *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 11*(2), 127-138. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787). very important for IER readers because it provides a powerful account of self-organizing systems as ongoing, temporally extended, world-coupled regulators. It is not an identity theory of experience, but it is one of the strongest modern resources for thinking about self-sustaining organization and intrinsic regulation.

Whyte, C. J., Corcoran, A. W., Robinson, J., Smith, R., Moran, R. J., Parr, T., Friston, K. J., Seth, A. K., & Hohwy, J. (2024). *On the minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference*. arXiv. especially useful for readers comparing IER with active-inference-based consciousness work. It makes explicit what is otherwise only implicit in many predictive-processing models and is therefore a good bridge text for evaluating convergence and divergence.

Chang, A. Y. C., Biehl, M., Yu, Y., & Kanai, R. (2019). *Information closure theory of consciousness*. arXiv. a particularly interesting adjacent attempt because it uses informational closure as the central explanatory idea. It is not IER, but readers interested in “informational slack,” closure, and system-boundedness should include it in the next-ring search set.

### Structure-first metaphysics, panpsychism, and the limits of organization-alone

Ladyman, J., & Ross, D. (2007). *Every thing must go: Metaphysics naturalized*. Oxford University Press. a landmark structure-first metaphysical text. It is useful for IER readers as a sophisticated example of why a general priority of structure or relations is not yet a theory of consciousness.

Strawson, G. (2006). *Realistic monism: Why physicalism entails panpsychism*. *Journal of Consciousness Studies, 13*(10-11), 3-31. perhaps the most important modern argument that a serious physicalism is pushed toward panpsychism or panprotopsychism. IER readers should consult it because it defines one of the strongest rivals to closed regime-identity: consciousness as fundamental rather than regime-specific.

Goff, P. (2017). *Consciousness and fundamental reality*. Oxford University Press. a clear contemporary defense of panpsychist and Russellian-monist options. Useful as a foil for IER because it preserves ontological seriousness about experience but abandons IER’s attempt to localize subjecthood in a specific physical regime.

### Ethical implication of experiential identity

Birch, J. (2024). *The edge of sentience: Risk and precaution in humans, other animals, and AI*. Oxford University Press. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001). the best current book for readers interested in what follows ethically once consciousness is treated as real but uncertainly attributable. It is especially relevant to IER because it frames moral decision-making around precaution rather than certainty, a natural fit for a theory that declines simple detection criteria.

DeGrazia, D. (1996). *Taking animals seriously: Mental life and moral status*. Cambridge University Press. a classic in moral-status theory that treats mental life and sentience as central to ethical consideration. Readers can use it to connect metaphysical questions about what experience is with practical questions about which beings matter.

Sebo, J. (2025). *The moral circle: Who matters, what matters, and why*. W. W. Norton. a contemporary extension of moral-circle reasoning to animals, AI, and uncertainty. It is not specific to IER, but it is a strong adjacent text for thinking about how a physically realist view of experience should ramify into public ethics.

## Comparisons, Examples and Source Limits

[Kierkegaard](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-kierkegaard.md) and [McLuhan](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/IER/IER-mcluhan.md) reconstruct
selected primary passages in their own contexts. Their source links and
comparison limits remain with those articles. Existential self-relation,
media participation and IER's numerical subject identity are different relations.
The [examples index](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/examples/README.md) distinguishes physical analogies
from qualified everyday interpretations; these are not experiments establishing
IER or clinical/collective-subject diagnoses.

The accepted research synthesis includes substantial positive antecedents and
combinations; its bounded nonfinding of a complete equivalent establishes no
historical priority, smallest novel conjunction or empirical superiority.
Older background references here are reading support, not a claim that every
book and edition was fully audited. Unacquired Dainton/Schechter texts, the
Brakel predecessor, IIT supplements/precursor and wider citation chains retain
their recorded access and coverage limits. No new search or primary-text audit
is claimed by reorganizing these materials.

Physical individuation, empirical attribution, complete quality correspondence
and the identity warrant remain open at their stated scopes. The
[problems companion](https://github.com/mlehotay/ier/blob/main/apps/philosophy/IER-and-problems-of-physicalism.md)
distinguishes articulated responses from unresolved obligations. Citations
support their attributed passages, not a second defining claim source.
