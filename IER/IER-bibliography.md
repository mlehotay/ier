# IER Bibliography

## Status, scope and current use

This reader-facing annotated bibliography is synchronized on 29 September 2026
with the implemented v10.11.5 formulation and accepted antecedents research.
It adds no theory, criteria, source-classification award or novelty claim.
The Specification governs theory. The [philosophy curriculum](../apps/philosophy/IER-philosophy-of-mind-curriculum.md)
is the selective beginner route; this bibliography provides a wider reference
surface, with established positive antecedents as well as objections.

The contribution is the developed physicalist framework, including its
particular formulation, organization, arguments and implications. Historical
novelty of individual ideas or established combinations is not claimed.
The accepted 44-source synthesis overlaps with the historical 32-source
Strawson corpus and five-source cybernetics package; counts are not added.
The full private citation audit remains under `_work/antecedents/`.

The updated selections below distinguish physical process-subject identity,
organizational closure, productive succession, effective inherited history,
quality correspondence and concrete individuation. A biological or cybernetic
account need not attribute consciousness. Probability-sensitive integration,
maintenance-dependency closure, statistical boundaries, phenomenal mineness
and non-externalizable physical bearing are distinct relations.

## Comparison dimensions

These comparisons locate the reviewed relations; they neither rank theories
nor treat every author in a tradition as holding one doctrine.

| Literature | Established resource | Comparison boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classical and contemporary identity physicalism | State/event/experience identity and causal efficacy | The selected total subject and its persistence need independent individuation. |
| Strawson's process-subject account | Subject, experience and physical process identity | The 2003 part/content criterion differs from turnover-compatible continuation. |
| Organizational biology and autonomy | Reciprocal maintenance, productive regimes, adaptive history, constitutive heredity | An organism or lineage is not automatically one continuing experiential operation. |
| IIT and causal-grain research | Intrinsic irreducibility, unit/complex selection and experience structure | Probability-sensitive integration and exclusion differ from fixed support, owned membership and two-sided continuity. IIT composition need not sum independently conscious microsubjects. |
| Structural-quality and selfhood research | Quality relations, givenness, represented mineness | NCC correspondence and self-model structure do not independently establish total-operation identity or physical ownership. |
| Panpsychist and Russellian approaches | Experience realism and intrinsic-nature challenges | IER rejects phenomenal inheritance; it does not refute every variant or disclose ultimate constitution. |
| Empirical access and inference programmes | Broadcast, regulation and model-based explanation | Roles or statistical boundaries alone do not establish IER's full relation stack. |

## Annotated bibliography

What follows is the broader bibliography proper. It is organized by theme rather than by simple chronology so that it more closely mirrors IER’s own architecture.

### Physical monism and identity rather than correlation

These works are the basic canon for any reader trying to see what IER inherits and what it changes. The SEP identity-theory entry continues to treat Place, Feigl, and Smart as the canonical launching points, and it explicitly emphasizes the identity theorist’s claim that experiences are brain processes, not merely accompanied by them. Papineau’s work is useful because it explains why identity can be ontologically true while still feeling conceptually resistant.

Place, U. T. (1956). *Is consciousness a brain process?* *British Journal of Psychology, 47*(1), 44-50. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1111/j.2044-8295.1956.tb00560.x. Relevance: the clearest early statement that conscious experience is to be identified with physical process rather than left as an accompanying residue. For IER readers, this is the indispensable starting point because it establishes the ontological form of the thesis even though it lacks IER’s regime-level dynamical specificity. Tags: identity-theory; physicalism; classic.

Feigl, H. (1958). *The “mental” and the “physical”*. In H. Feigl, M. Scriven, & G. Maxwell (Eds.), *Minnesota studies in the philosophy of science, Vol. 2: Concepts, theories, and the mind-body problem* (pp. 370-497). University of Minnesota Press. Relevance: Feigl supplies the broadest philosophical architecture for psychophysical identity, especially the refusal of dualist “danglers.” IER readers will find this useful as the most systematic classical statement of why identity is explanatorily cleaner than bridge-law dualism. Tags: identity-theory; physicalism; classic.

Smart, J. J. C. (1959). *Sensations and brain processes*. *The Philosophical Review, 68*(2), 141-156. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.2307/2182164. Relevance: Smart’s paper is crucial for its defense of contingent identity and for its insistence that the meanings of phenomenal and physical descriptions can differ while their referent is one and the same. IER readers should especially note how much of the later hard-problem dialectic already appears here in compressed form. Tags: identity-theory; contingent-identity; classic.

Lewis, D. (1966). *An argument for the identity theory*. *The Journal of Philosophy, 63*(1), 17-25. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.2307/2024559. Relevance: Lewis matters because he helps move identity theory into dialogue with multiple realizability and more flexible realization talk. He is adjacent rather than fully convergent with IER, but essential for understanding why a strict identity view now needs a more organizational rather than merely local relatum. Tags: identity-theory; type-token; physicalism.

Armstrong, D. M. (1968). *A materialist theory of the mind*. Routledge & Kegan Paul. Relevance: Armstrong systematizes Australian materialism and is especially useful for readers interested in “central-state” physicalism. IER differs sharply from Armstrong’s more state-centered and functionalized picture, but the book remains one of the clearest attempts to make physicalism do full ontological work. Tags: materialism; central-state-theory; identity-theory.

Papineau, D. (2002). *Thinking about consciousness*. Oxford University Press. Relevance: perhaps the single most useful contemporary book for readers who want a serious physicalism without eliminativist rhetoric. Papineau is especially valuable for IER because he treats the conceptual resistance to identity as a feature of our concepts rather than evidence against physical monism. Tags: physicalism; phenomenal-concepts; anti-dualism.

### Subject identity, organizational closure and productive history

Strawson, G. (2003). *What Is the Relation Between an Experience, the Subject of the Experience, and the Content of the Experience?* *Philosophical Issues* 13, 279–315. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1533-6077.00015). Exact core process-subject identity precedent. His part/content-sensitive criterion, rather than a mandatory short duration, is the important persistence contrast.

Papineau, D. (2006). *Comments on Galen Strawson: “Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism”.* *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 13(10–11), 100–109. [Author text](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/papineau_realistic_monism.pdf). Physical identity and causal arguments allow complex physical consciousness without experiential simples. This does not supply the complete continuing-subject relation.

Ashby, W. R. (1968). *Variety, Constraint, and the Law of Requisite Variety.* In W. Buckley (ed.), *Modern Systems Research for the Behavioral Scientist*, 129–136. [Sourcebook record](https://www.routledge.com/Systems-Research-for-Behavioral-Science-A-Sourcebook/Buckley/p/book/9780202362809). Consulted through the 2011 reprint, 200–207, drawing from the 1956 cybernetics book. Alternatives and restricted variety are established resources; observer-relative distinguishability is not IER's physical primitive.

Montévil, M., and Mossio, M. (2015). *Biological Organisation as Closure of Constraints.* *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 372, 179–191. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029). Constraints channel and maintain processes across scales. The reviewed 2016 manuscript's PDF pagination differs from published pagination; edition equivalence was not independently audited. Dependency closure is not automatically non-product admissible support.

Di Paolo, E. A. (2005). *Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency.* *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 4, 429–452. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y). Distinguishes self-production, viability and adaptive regulation; historically effective organization is a positive comparison, without a consciousness attribution from agency alone.

Bich, L., Mossio, M., Ruiz-Mirazo, K., and Moreno, A. (2016; online 2015). *Biological Regulation: Controlling the System from Within.* *Biology & Philosophy* 31, 237–265. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-015-9497-8). Endogenous regulation and constitutive organization clarify internal/external roles. The reviewed author manuscript does not establish non-externalizable subject ownership.

Pradeu, T. (2018). *Genidentity and Biological Processes.* In *Everything Flows*, 96–112. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0005). Historical transformation can preserve a process through material change. Biological genidentity does not automatically identify the experiential subject.

DiFrisco, J. (2018). *Biological Processes: Criteria of Identity and Persistence.* In *Everything Flows*, 76–95. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0004). Supplies individuation and persistence distinctions, including branching cases, rather than a ready-made IER rule.

DiFrisco, J., and Mossio, M. (2020). *Diachronic Identity in Complex Life Cycles: An Organizational Perspective.* In *Biological Identity*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781351066389-10). Productive transitions between changing regimes, incorporation and fission/fusion are close positive comparisons. The reviewed copy is the 2019 manuscript; published-edition equivalence was not verified. Context-sensitive biological continuity differs from the fixed-domain five-clause subject relation.

Mossio, M., and Pontarotti, G. (2022 issue; online 2019). *Conserving Functions across Generations: Heredity in Light of Biological Organization.* *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 73(1), 249–278. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axz031). The reviewed published issue copy distinguishes constitutive functional heredity from independently stable environmental conditions. Heredity may relate coexisting distinct generations, rather than preserve one numerical subject.

### Ownership, qualities and live objections

Guillot, M. (2017; online 2016). *I Me Mine: on a Confusion Concerning the Subjective Character of Experience.* *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 8, 23–53. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-016-0313-4). Distinguishes subjective relations that should not be conflated with physical bearing. Zahavi/Kriegel's 2016 *For-me-ness* and Metzinger's 2000 representational analysis supply complementary resources; see the curriculum for primary links and reviewed-copy limits.

Loorits, K. (2014). *Structural Qualia: a Solution to the Hard Problem of Consciousness.* *Frontiers in Psychology* 5, 237. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00237). Structural-quality identity is a substantive antecedent, not itself a complete total-subject persistence theory.

Lyre, H. (2022). *Neurophenomenal Structuralism. A Philosophical Agenda for a Structuralist Neuroscience of Consciousness.* *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2022(1), niac012. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niac012). Structural-quality programme with a different target from full operation identity. Fink, Kob and Lyre (2021), *A Structural Constraint on Neural Correlates of Consciousness*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.33735/phimisci.2021.79), provides correspondence constraints without supplying an exhaustive IER quality mapping.

Austin, C. J. (2020). *Organisms, Activity, and Being: On the Substance of Process Ontology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-020-0278-0). Tests whether continuity tracks an independently justified kind or presupposes an already selected individual. DiFrisco (2018; online 2017), *Token Physicalism and Functional Individuation*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-017-0188-y), adds a substantive individuation challenge.

McIntyre, J. H. (2026). *Consciousness Multiplied.* *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 113(2), 465–479. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.70146). Counting and overlap objections remain substantive; naming a maximal scope does not by itself settle them.

Negro, N. (2025). *Three Arguments against Metaphysical Structuralism in Consciousness Research.* *Synthese* 206, 8. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05103-6). A critic of structural-quality accounts, to be addressed on his actual premises rather than dismissed by invoking identity.

Brakel, L. A. W. (2021). *Can Neuroscientists Test a New Physicalist Mind/Body View: DiCoToP (Diachronic Conjunctive Token Physicalism)?* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.786133). Event identity and diachronic content conjunction are positive comparisons, with different relata from one continuing total subject. The predecessor text was not independently reviewed.

### Hard-problem pressure and the anti-mysterian turn

*IER-history* and *IER-new-anti-mysterianism* present the hard-problem era as a forcing function rather than a solution, and place IER closer to the later naturalistic turn that seeks to preserve phenomenological seriousness without dualism. Nagel and Chalmers are therefore indispensable pressure sources; Dennett is a key anti-mysterian foil; and Seth and Bayne provide the best high-level map of the contemporary theory space.

Nagel, T. (1974). *What is it like to be a bat?* *The Philosophical Review, 83*(4), 435-450. Relevance: still the cleanest statement of why subjectivity resists purely third-person redescription. IER does not follow Nagel into anti-physicalism, but any reader needs Nagel to see what the identity thesis must preserve rather than explain away. Tags: subjectivity; phenomenology; hard-problem-precursor.

Chalmers, D. J. (1995). *Facing up to the problem of consciousness*. *Journal of Consciousness Studies, 2*(3), 200-219. Relevance: the article that most forcefully separated “easy” functional problems from the phenomenal one. IER is easiest to understand as a direct response to this pressure: it agrees that phenomenology is real and not exhausted by access-talk, while denying that this forces dualism or fundamentalism. Tags: hard-problem; anti-reductionism; foil.

Dennett, D. C. (1991). *Consciousness explained*. Little, Brown. Relevance: a major anti-mysterian landmark and a necessary foil for IER’s rejection of both Cartesian spectatorship and purely access- or narrative-based accounts. Useful especially for readers comparing IER’s anti-Cartesianism with its refusal to dissolve experience into functional organization alone. Tags: anti-mysterianism; functionalism; foil.

Seth, A. K., & Bayne, T. (2022). *Theories of consciousness*. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 23*(7), 439-452. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4. Relevance: the best concise overview of the contemporary empirical landscape, including IIT, GNW, higher-order, recurrent, and predictive approaches. This is the right “map” text for readers entering IER from outside philosophy of mind. Tags: overview; contemporary-theories; comparison.

### Unified dynamical regimes, global integration, and major contemporary comparators

This cluster matters because IER’s UEF claim is regime-level, system-wide, and unity-centered. IIT is the closest nearby theory because it also proposes an identity between experience and a system-level intrinsic structure; GNW is the most important contrast because it explains conscious access through broadcast and ignition rather than through a closed ontological identity. Contemporary adversarial and comparative work shows that these theory families remain live and contested.

Baars, B. J. (1988). *A cognitive theory of consciousness*. Cambridge University Press. Relevance: the foundational workspace text. Readers should include it not because it supports IER directly, but because it represents the clearest alternative in which consciousness is tied to global availability and coordination rather than to a strict experiential identity condition. Tags: global-workspace; access; classic-competitor.

Dehaene, S., Changeux, J.-P., Naccache, L., Sackur, J., & Sergent, C. (2006). *Conscious, preconscious, and subliminal processing: A testable taxonomy*. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 10*(5), 204-211. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2006.03.007. Relevance: the most efficient statement of the GNW family in article form. It is especially useful for IER readers because it lays out a strong empirical theory of conscious access that IER must distinguish from subjecthood itself. Tags: GNW; access-consciousness; global-integration.

Dehaene, S., Lau, H., & Kouider, S. (2017). *What is consciousness, and could machines have it?* *Science, 358*(6362), 486-492. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1126/science.aan8871. Relevance: a concise mature statement of the GNW-style scientific program, including its attitude toward report, signatures, and machine consciousness. IER readers will likely disagree with its detectability emphasis, which is exactly why it belongs here. Tags: GNW; machine-consciousness; empirical-program.

Tononi, G. (2004). *An information integration theory of consciousness*. *BMC Neuroscience, 5*, 42. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1186/1471-2202-5-42. Relevance: an early IIT formulation identifying consciousness with an integrative property. Distinguish graded quantities from the separate issue of categorical subject existence. Tags: IIT; integration; identity-claim.

Oizumi, M., Albantakis, L., & Tononi, G. (2014). *From the phenomenology to the mechanisms of consciousness: Integrated information theory 3.0*. *PLoS Computational Biology, 10*(5), e1003588. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588. Relevance: the best single technical exposition of the phenomenology-to-mechanism move in IIT. Readers interested in how an identity proposal can be built from axioms and postulates should read this alongside IER’s stricter anti-graded stance. Tags: IIT; phenomenology-to-mechanism; causal-structure.

Tononi, G., Boly, M., Massimini, M., & Koch, C. (2016). *Integrated information theory: From consciousness to its physical substrate*. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 17*(7), 450-461. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1038/nrn.2016.44. Relevance: the cleanest mature overview of IIT in a major review venue. Essential for understanding both why IIT is so often treated as the strongest current identity-like theory and how intrinsic causal selection differs from IER’s categorical local subjecthood and independently typed multiscale operations. Tags: IIT; review; intrinsic-causation.

Albantakis, L., Barbosa, L., Findlay, G., Grasso, M., Haun, A. M., Marshall, W., Mayner, W. G. P., Zaeemzadeh, A., Boly, M., Juel, B. E., Sasai, S., Fujii, K., David, I., Hendren, J., Lang, J. P., & Tononi, G. (2023). *Integrated information theory (IIT) 4.0: Formulating the properties of phenomenal existence in physical terms*. *PLoS Computational Biology*. Relevance: the 2023 axioms/postulates formulation is a central intrinsic-organization comparator. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465). Read as the 2023 version, not the endpoint of causal-grain work. Tags: IIT; contemporary-primary-source; intrinsic-existence.

Marshall, W., Findlay, G., Albantakis, L., and Tononi, G. (2026). *Intrinsic Units: Identifying a System's Causal Grain.* *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2026(1), niag013. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag013). The reviewed published article extends grain analysis. Its specified noisy four-unit example permits full positive successor support despite reported positive integration. This conditional support comparison does not reproduce integration calculations, prove a global grain optimum, or establish a brain result. Supplements and the precursor remain unreviewed.

### Intrinsic constraint, self-maintenance, embodiment, and temporal continuity

This is the cluster most likely to matter for future IER development. The classical identity theorists named the relation; the autopoietic, enactive, and active-inference traditions supply more of the physical organization that IER wants to identify with experience. These sources are not all identity theories, but they are the best adjacent literature for self-sustaining regulation, ongoing organization, and temporally extended subjecthood.

Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1980). *Autopoiesis and cognition: The realization of the living*. D. Reidel. Relevance: the baseline text on self-producing and operationally closed systems. It does not identify experience with autopoiesis, but it is one of the deepest sources for the kind of self-sustaining organization that IER treats as necessary background for experiential regime identity. Tags: autopoiesis; operational-closure; self-maintenance.

Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The embodied mind: Cognitive science and human experience*. MIT Press. Relevance: foundational for embodied and enactive naturalism, and central to the “new anti-mysterian” family resemblance named in the IER corpus. Readers should use it to understand how bodily organization and lived temporality were reintroduced into consciousness theory without reverting to dualism. Tags: enactivism; embodiment; anti-mysterianism.

Varela, F. J. (1996). *Neurophenomenology: A methodological remedy for the hard problem*. *Journal of Consciousness Studies, 3*(4), 330-349. Relevance: crucial for anyone who wants phenomenal seriousness without metaphysical exceptionalism. IER does not adopt Varela’s methodological program wholesale, but it belongs in the same broad anti-dualist, non-dismissive atmosphere. Tags: neurophenomenology; anti-mysterianism; methodology.

Thompson, E. (2007). *Mind in life: Biology, phenomenology, and the sciences of mind*. Harvard University Press. Relevance: a major synthesis of autopoiesis, embodied cognition, and phenomenology. It is probably the single best adjacent book for readers exploring whether a processual, living, self-maintaining account can be pushed closer to a strict identity thesis. Tags: biology; phenomenology; enactivism.

Friston, K. (2010). *The free-energy principle: A unified brain theory?* *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 11*(2), 127-138. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1038/nrn2787. Relevance: very important for IER readers because it provides a powerful account of self-organizing systems as ongoing, temporally extended, world-coupled regulators. It is not an identity theory of experience, but it is one of the strongest modern resources for thinking about self-sustaining organization and intrinsic regulation. Tags: predictive-processing; self-organization; temporal-continuity.

Whyte, C. J., Corcoran, A. W., Robinson, J., Smith, R., Moran, R. J., Parr, T., Friston, K. J., Seth, A. K., & Hohwy, J. (2024). *On the minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference*. arXiv. Relevance: especially useful for readers comparing IER with active-inference-based consciousness work. It makes explicit what is otherwise only implicit in many predictive-processing models and is therefore a good bridge text for evaluating convergence and divergence. Tags: active-inference; minimal-theory; comparative.

Chang, A. Y. C., Biehl, M., Yu, Y., & Kanai, R. (2019). *Information closure theory of consciousness*. arXiv. Relevance: a particularly interesting adjacent attempt because it uses informational closure as the central explanatory idea. It is not IER, but readers interested in “informational slack,” closure, and system-boundedness should include it in the next-ring search set. Tags: information-closure; intrinsic-constraint; adjacency.

### Structure-first metaphysics, panpsychism, and the limits of organization-alone

*IER-structuralism* is unusually clear that shared structural intuitions do not by themselves imply shared experiential ontology. Readers should therefore separate three different claims that are often run together: that structure matters, that structure is ontologically basic, and that certain structure is identical with experience. Structural realism and panpsychism are essential nearby literatures precisely because they illuminate those distinctions.

Ladyman, J., & Ross, D. (2007). *Every thing must go: Metaphysics naturalized*. Oxford University Press. Relevance: a landmark structure-first metaphysical text. It is useful for IER readers as a sophisticated example of why a general priority of structure or relations is not yet a theory of consciousness. Tags: structuralism; metaphysics; structure-first.

Strawson, G. (2006). *Realistic monism: Why physicalism entails panpsychism*. *Journal of Consciousness Studies, 13*(10-11), 3-31. Relevance: perhaps the most important modern argument that a serious physicalism is pushed toward panpsychism or panprotopsychism. IER readers should consult it because it defines one of the strongest rivals to closed regime-identity: consciousness as fundamental rather than regime-specific. Tags: panpsychism; realistic-monism; foil.

Goff, P. (2017). *Consciousness and fundamental reality*. Oxford University Press. Relevance: a clear contemporary defense of panpsychist and Russellian-monist options. Useful as a foil for IER because it preserves ontological seriousness about experience but abandons IER’s attempt to localize subjecthood in a specific physical regime. Tags: panpsychism; Russellian-monism; foil.

### Ethical implication of experiential identity

IER’s ethics section is brief but not empty: if conscious existence is identical with a physically real, internally burdened regime, then interfering with such regimes matters morally by identity, not by abstraction. The most relevant contemporary literature therefore concerns how to reason under uncertainty about sentience and how far the moral circle should extend. Birch is the strongest direct fit because his framework is designed for exactly the kind of risk-sensitive moral reasoning IER will eventually need.

Birch, J. (2024). *The edge of sentience: Risk and precaution in humans, other animals, and AI*. Oxford University Press. Canonical URL/DOI: 10.1093/9780191966729.001.0001. Relevance: the best current book for readers interested in what follows ethically once consciousness is treated as real but uncertainly attributable. It is especially relevant to IER because it frames moral decision-making around precaution rather than certainty, a natural fit for a theory that declines simple detection criteria. Tags: ethics; sentience; precaution.

DeGrazia, D. (1996). *Taking animals seriously: Mental life and moral status*. Cambridge University Press. Relevance: a classic in moral-status theory that treats mental life and sentience as central to ethical consideration. Readers can use it to connect metaphysical questions about what experience is with practical questions about which beings matter. Tags: ethics; moral-status; animals.

Sebo, J. (2025). *The moral circle: Who matters, what matters, and why*. W. W. Norton. Relevance: a contemporary extension of moral-circle reasoning to animals, AI, and uncertainty. It is not specific to IER, but it is a strong adjacent text for thinking about how a physically realist view of experience should ramify into public ethics. Tags: ethics; moral-circle; AI.

## Research closure and residual limits

The organizational, process-subject and historical-continuity literature is no
longer merely an unsearched next step: the accepted review includes substantial
positive lineages and combinations. It demonstrates no complete equivalent of
the amended joint account in its bounded corpus. That nonfinding establishes
no first priority, smallest novel conjunction or empirical superiority.

Older background entries retained here are reading references, not evidence
that every cited book, edition or contemporary theory has been audited by the
44-source project. Unacquired Dainton/Schechter texts, the Brakel predecessor,
IIT supplements/precursor and wider citation chains retain their access and
coverage limits. No new broad search or download batch is scheduled here.

Independent physical typing and meaningful partitions, concrete ownership and
membership, produced successors, effective inherited consequences, two-sided
