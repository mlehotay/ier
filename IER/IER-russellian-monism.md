# Russellian Monism

## Intrinsic Nature, Structural Access, and Epistemic Non-Authority

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.5)**\
*Tier-2 Explanatory · Non-Normative · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This article explains the accepted IER position. It introduces no primitives,
criteria for experience, diagnostic procedures, or epistemic authority.
`IER-specification.md` and the governing canonical owners retain authority.
Its comparisons do not establish novelty or refute every neighboring theory.

## Abstract

IER distinguishes a structural description of mind-independent reality from
a thesis about its categorical intrinsic constitution. It accepts that
structural access does not disclose that ultimate constitution, while rejecting
the inference that experience identifies it or provides privileged access to it.
Phenomenology concerns UEF-relative constraint organization; sensory multiple
realizability blocks its use as an inverse map to external intrinsic nature.
The Russellian demand remains an external dialectical disagreement rather
than an unfinished internal IER mechanism.

## The External Question

Russellian approaches distinguish structural or dispositional descriptions
from the categorical intrinsic nature of what bears those relations and
dispositions. Experiential variants propose an experiential ground for that
nature. This comparative distinction does not identify all Russellian views
with panpsychism.
[Chalmers, “The Combination Problem for Panpsychism”](https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf)

Strawson's argument treats experience as wholly physical and challenges radical
emergence, motivating experiential physical ultimates. That is a demand about
the being of physical reality, not merely its causal organization.
[Strawson, “Realistic Monism”](https://informationphilosopher.com/solutions/philosophers/strawsong/Strawson_JCS_Why_Panpsychism.pdf)

IER shares a commitment to physical reality and the reality of experience.
It does not undertake to identify what reality is ultimately made of, nor
supply categorical experiential stuff to complete its ontology.

## Three Different Uses of “Intrinsic”

Strawson's intrinsic nature concerns categorical or qualitative being.
IER intrinsic constraint concerns physically borne restrictions on the
continuation of an operation. IER intrinsic closure specifies a synchronic
nonfactorization relation at a fixed physical scope.

The latter two do not disclose the former. Nor does the adjective “intrinsic”
convert a relation into privileged acquaintance with ultimate constitution.
Closure, ownership, and continuity must retain their accepted meanings:
none introduces a categorical ground or a new substance.

## The Premise Accepted and the Inference Rejected

IER accepts that structural access does not reveal ultimate intrinsic
constitution. Constraint geometry describes how reality constrains and how
physical systems can continue. Calling reality-in-itself constraining does
not assert that its constitution is exhausted by a known geometry.

IER rejects the inference that experience identifies, or provides privileged
access to, reality's categorical ground. A phenomenal organization is not
an inspection of the ultimate nature of its external cause. This rejection
is epistemic; it neither denies that reality has a constitution nor proves
that all possible categorical metaphysics are false.

Under IER the intrinsic constitution of reality-in-itself is unavailable
to experiential access. This is a position of the theory, not a missing
producer of experience awaiting metaphysical completion. Indirect physical
inquiry can improve structural knowledge without granting phenomenology
authority over ultimate being.

## One World, Two Descriptive Relations

`IER-reality.md` distinguishes reality-in-itself from reality-for-a-UEF.
The former is mind-independent and constraining; the latter is the same
world as frontier-relevant for a particular operation. These are not two
worlds or mental and physical substances.

Phenomenology reveals UEF-relative organization in the sense that it is
identical with the relevant organization. It does not supply an infallible
description, a correctness signal, or a revelation of the world's
categorical constitution. Being experience is not the same as judging
correctly about experience or its causes.

`IER-constraint-modes.md` describes organizations such as urgency, attraction,
and conflict. A mode is not a sample of an external object's intrinsic stuff.

## Why Sensory Multiple Realizability Matters

`IER-sensory-multiple-realizability.md` distinguishes four roles:

1. An external referent or physical cause.
2. A transduction or coupling mechanism.
3. UEF-relative constraint organization.
4. Phenomenology, under the conditional identity claim where a UEF exists.

Different causes and channels may converge on a shared constraint mode.
The same cause can participate in different modes as the operation's
organization and relevant history change. Accordingly, phenomenal mode does
not uniquely identify its external cause, let alone that cause's ultimate
intrinsic nature.

Coarse mode equivalence is not complete phenomenal identity. Identity of
phenomenal organization would require the full relevant constitutive
organization, including history, boundary, and dynamics, rather than a
matching label or selected observable. These distinctions block an inference
to external intrinsic nature without denying informative sensory relations
or making an empirical claim that particular organisms share experiences.

## Epistemic Non-Authority

Reality constrains even when beliefs, reports, and felt certainty disagree.
Neither salience nor irreversible consequence licenses a truth verdict.
No experiential intensity, mode, unity, or persistence grants categorical
insight. This preserves `IER-nonentailment.md`: structural identity does not
confer epistemic certification.

The restriction applies to claims about both external objects and physical
grounds. It is not permission to fill the resulting uncertainty with a
positive categorical thesis.

## Internal Burdens and External Disagreement

IER still owes worked physical instantiation, operational individuation,
qualitative correspondence, empirical attribution, and an abductive defense
of identity. Those obligations can be pursued within its accepted ontology.

A Russellian critic can remain dissatisfied because IER does not supply
the desired nonrelational ground. Failure to persuade that critic is an
external dialectical result. It must not be entered as a missing IER
mechanism, confused with the qualitative mapping burden, or treated as a
refutation of every Russellian view. Conversely, labeling the objection
external does not demonstrate that the critic is wrong.

## Intermission - Structural Fact

A world can constrain experience without experience revealing what that
world is ultimately made of.
