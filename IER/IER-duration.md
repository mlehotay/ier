# Duration

## Registered Organization and Experienced Length

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.6)**\
*T2 · Elaboration · Explanatory · Non-Normative*

## Abstract

This article proposes that the organization and cognitive registration of consequential deformation can contribute to experienced duration. Immediate organization and retrospective reconstruction may differ. Collapse count, activity and switching alone supply no duration law; physical timing mechanisms and records are permitted without storing past experience. Examples of boredom, novelty, crisis and flow remain conditional comparisons, with quantitative and qualitative mapping unresolved.

## Status, Scope, and Authority

Named full-corpus owners fix the technical meanings used here. [Theory](IER-theory.md) provides the public integrated account; this article states the premises needed for its own explanation.

*Time* (`IER-time.md`) and [Continuity](IER-continuity.md) govern physical temporal relations. This article develops a possible phenomenological explanation, rather than a proven constitutive definition of felt duration or a ban on physical timing mechanisms. It adds no threshold, internal time substance or diagnostic.

## Orientation — The Variability of Duration

An interval can feel different while occurring and when recalled. Novelty, routine, crisis, boredom and narrative pacing motivate comparisons, but labels are not measurements of deformation or evidence of a universal pattern.

The proposed question is how present participation and reconstructible organization affect experienced length. Ordinary clock-based tasks and physical interval estimation can be investigated alongside that question. A clock reading is not automatically the experience of duration.

## Proposed Account — Registration and Organization

The organization and cognitive registration of consequential deformation may contribute to experienced duration. Specify the operation, interval, physical consequences, current reproduction and immediate or retrospective comparison. The proposal does not claim that all duration consists exclusively of freshly welded change.

Where a collapse episode occurs, welding incorporates its deformation and propagation redistributes effects; sedimentation concerns stabilized inheritance. Previously inherited effects can remain effective without a new collapse. Only a separately justified experiential re-entry counts as episodic memory, and all four cognitive conditions must be established for cognition.

“Density” here is a descriptive handle for how differentiated consequential organization participates in the studied interval. No scalar, metric or monotonic function from deformation to reported seconds has been derived. More activity can produce less differentiated later reconstruction; many boundaries can be compressed into one familiar episode.

## Collapse, Segmentation and Duration Have Different Roles

Collapse establishes atomic irreversible reachability loss. Event Segmentation treats the conditional case where its consequences become articulated at a cognitively accessible scale. Duration asks how current or reconstructed organization contributes to felt length.

Neither raw collapse count nor a count of reported events supplies the third result. Psychological event segmentation may occur without an established collapse, and duration can concern ongoing organization between fresh irreversible episodes.

## Immediate and Retrospective Comparisons

Immediate duration can be compared with current recruitment, salience and sustained organization. Retrospective duration can be compared with the differentiated organization physically available for present reconstruction. Records and timing processes can contribute to either, without replaying an earlier experience.

Consider a stipulated waiting interval. Repeated checking keeps delay salient during the wait, but its repeated organization may be reconstructed later as one undifferentiated episode. A second interval can contain many differentiated encounters and be reconstructed more extensively. This illustrates how online and retrospective comparisons might diverge; it neither proves the proposal nor establishes which interval must feel longer.

## Characteristic Illustrations

| Label used to motivate inquiry | Conditional organizational comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Boredom | Persistent delay-oriented recruitment can coexist with sparse later differentiation. |
| Novelty | Changed organization may support differentiated reconstruction if registered and maintained. |
| Routine | Familiar organization may permit compression without implying weak experience or low standing. |
| Crisis | Strong salience can alter recruitment and registration; no fixed direction of duration change follows. |
| Flow | Coherent task organization can change attention to elapsed time without suspending physical succession. |

Each needs actual evidence; none is a clinical classification or a universal duration regime. Coherent organization does not always shorten duration, nor does diffuse organization always lengthen it.

## External Scaffolding and Nested Scales

Music, conversational rhythm, ritual and narrative pacing can physically affect current participation, event descriptions and later reconstruction. They do not supply an extra time primitive. Their causal influence is compatible with IER and must be assessed under its own physical scope.

Organization can be compared at perceptual, action and narrative scales. These are no fixed universal hierarchy and no measured threshold of subjecthood. A felt interval cannot prove continuous closure or unique produced-and-inherited token continuation.

## Summary and Remaining Burden

Registration and reconstruction provide a possible account of duration differences, while activity and boundary counts alone are insufficient. Establishing a quantitative or qualitative correspondence, comparing alternative timing models and testing cases remain research work. No empirical result is admitted here simply because the account uses physical vocabulary.
