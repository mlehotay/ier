# Informational Experiential Realism

## Précis

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.11.1)**\
*Framework Compression - Non-Normative - Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority




This précis is a standalone conceptual overview of Informational Experiential
Realism. It introduces no new definitions, criteria, mechanisms, diagnostic
claims, or normative permissions. The canonical specification is
authoritative; supporting documents are canon-constrained and explanatory. If
this précis conflicts with the specification, the specification prevails.

For public-facing review, this précis is one of the initial trusted public
review documents, alongside the philosophy-of-mind curriculum. That status
means it should receive narrow synchronization and review priority as a reader
entry point. It does not make the précis an independent authority, a complete
coverage proof, or a replacement for the canonical specification, registries,
Seed Book, papers, or public repository selection.

IER is a physicalist identity theory of experience. Its central proposal is ontological:

> Experience is identical to the operation of a physical system as a Unified Experiential Field under coherent intrinsic constraint.

IER does not offer a behavioral test, neural correlate, computational
signature, engineering recipe, or general procedure for attributing experience
to unfamiliar systems. It asks what physical organization experience could be
if experience is real but is neither non-physical nor an additional product of
physical activity. Canon defines admissibility conditions before asserting
existence claims; it does not enumerate all actual UEFs.

## Experience as physical regime

IER accepts one objective, mind-independent physical reality. Experience is real, but it is not a second substance, an emergent accompaniment, an internal representation, or an output added to physical dynamics. When the relevant physical regime exists, its operation does not produce experience: its operation is the experience.

IER calls this regime a Unified Experiential Field (UEF). “Field” does not mean a fundamental physical field, a spatial container, or an entity inside a system. It names a system-level organization in which intrinsic constraint is:

- globally integrated,
- sustained across a non-zero interval,
- generated and borne within the system’s own dynamics,
- non-factorizable into independently resolvable subsystem constraints,
- unavailable for indefinite deferral or external resolution, and
- binding on the continuation of the system as a whole.

This is IER’s proposed physical identity condition, not a behavioral definition of consciousness. Integration, complexity, feedback, information processing, and causal influence are not sufficient. The relevant question is whether constraint can still be localized, independently resolved, deferred, or offloaded, or whether it has become globally unavoidable for one continuing physical regime.

## Constraint, information, and slack

IER uses constraint in a strictly physical and structural sense. Constraint does not push, choose, evaluate, optimize, or select an outcome. It is the lawful restriction of physically admissible continuation: the difference between transitions that the system can and cannot undergo from its present configuration.

IER likewise does not treat information as a substance, message, representation, stored content, probability, or knowledge possessed by a system. Information is the structured exclusion of otherwise admissible continuation produced by physical organization under constraint. It is not what a system contains; it is what its organization no longer allows.

This differs from Shannon information. Shannon theory measures distinctions relative to coding and transmission schemes and deliberately abstracts from physical realization, meaning, and identity. IER does not identify experience with communication, computation, entropy, or information processing. Its informational language refers to physically instantiated differences that alter the system’s own possible continuation.

Informational slack is the availability of an independence-preserving way for constraint to be resolved without becoming globally binding. Slack exists when constraint can be localized, indefinitely deferred, or resolved outside the proposed system boundary. The number of admissible futures is a separate matter: a system may have several possible continuations without having slack if none can be resolved independently of the whole.

Saturation names the exhaustion of these independence-preserving local pathways. It is not maximal activity, overload, a single remaining future, or a degree of consciousness. Under IER’s identity proposal, slack and UEF operation are incompatible: where relevant slack remains, constraint is not yet globally intrinsic in the required sense.

## Unity, subjecthood, and participation

A UEF is a regime of operation, not a component within a system. Its boundary is not fixed by anatomy, location, function, or an observer’s preferred partition. IER characterizes the structural conditions for an admissible individuation; it does not, from this précis alone, enumerate the actual subject boundary in every case.

IER treats subjecthood as categorical:

> One UEF constitutes one experiential subject.

The physical conditions that enable a UEF may change continuously, and processes may participate in it to different degrees. Subjecthood itself is not graded. A process participates experientially when it both bears and contributes to the field’s globally integrated constraint. Changes in participation may alter the content, intensity, salience, or organization of experience without creating another subject, provided the field remains unified and continuous.

This distinguishes graded participation from graded subjecthood. There is no
partial subject before a UEF exists and no collection of fractional subjects
within one field. Nor is unity the binding of representations. Representations
may occur within an experiential system, but the UEF does not represent its
intrinsic constraint to an inner witness. It bears that constraint directly.

This is a structural rule for admissible individuation, not a claim that every
concrete boundary is externally identifiable. Nested, overlapping, biological,
artificial, and distributed candidate organizations remain open unless their
relevant structure is excluded by established canon. One UEF, if instantiated,
constitutes one subject; the theory does not infer which ordinary biological,
social, or legal unit is a UEF merely from its category or behavior.

Ownership has the same structural sense. It does not imply an inner owner, voluntary control, or privileged self-knowledge. Constraint and its resolution are owned when they are borne non-externalizably by the same continuing physical regime.

## The history - future boundary

At any physical configuration, law and organization delimit a set of admissible continuations. These are not probabilities, predictions, contemplated options, or alternatives offered to an internal chooser. They are the physically available directions in which the system can continue.

Experience operates at the boundary between:

- sedimented history - prior change persisting as present physical organization; and
- admissible continuation - the structured future still physically reachable.

The admissible frontier has a geometry. The organization of the system can
alter reachability, viability, anticipation, projection, or switching costs.
These are typed structural relations, not synonyms: raw lawful successors and
future cones are distinct from Choice Space, anticipated futures, and other
subject-dependent projections. Their complete formal relationship remains a
continuing formalization programme. This geometry can deform continuously
before irreversible resolution.

IER calls the irreversible foreclosure of admissible continuation collapse. Collapse is the binary, atomic, and exclusive contraction of the reachable successor set at the relevant frontier: incompatible continuations cease to be reachable. Collapse is distinct from successor realization, which is the lawful continuation from a boundary configuration to an admissible successor. “Atomic” does not mean instantaneous, microscopic, or quantum-mechanical; it means that foreclosure at that frontier is one indivisible reachability update.

Collapse is not random selection, a decision by an internal executive, or an intervention by experience into physical law. It changes which continuations remain admissible; lawful dynamics determine successor realization without introducing a hidden selection layer. After collapse, its consequences are integrated into the continuing regime and reshape what remains reachable. History therefore persists not as a stored copy of the past but as deformation of present and future possibility.

Repeated deformation, collapse, integration, and sedimentation constitute the system’s temporally extended trajectory. This account does not place machinery beneath experience. It describes the physical organization whose operation IER identifies with experience.

## One organization, several consequences

IER treats familiar features of experience as consequences or aspects of one physical regime:

- unity: constraint is borne by one non-factorizable organization;
- ownership: constraint and resolution cannot be externalized from that organization;
- continuity: successive states remain dynamically dependent through time;
- qualitative difference: the internal organization of intrinsic constraint differs;
- temporality: sedimented history and admissible continuation meet at an active boundary;
- agency: where present, experiential agency is a derived UEF capacity for bearing and resolving intrinsic constraint;
- memory: prior resolution persists in present reachability rather than as a recovered copy of the past; and
- possibility: unrealized continuation remains structurally available before foreclosure.

IER describes this organization from related perspectives. Its geometry concerns the structure of admissible continuation; its dynamics concern deformation, resolution, and persistence. Its accounts of memory, identity, agency, history, and possibility concern different consequences of the same organization. These perspectives distinguish relations within the theory without adding entities or ingredients to experience.

## UEF continuity, cessation, and numerical identity

UEF persistence requires continuous instantiation of the globally coherent
intrinsic-constraint regime. Changes in content, participation, or organization
do not by themselves end the UEF. Failure is a breakdown within an extant UEF
and is not itself cessation.

UEF cessation occurs when the constitutive regime is no longer instantiated.
The embodied, sedimented physical system may persist through a UEF-null
interval, and it may retain the capacity to sustain a future UEF. If a later
UEF is instantiated after genuine cessation, it is a numerically distinct
experiential subject. This separates embodied-system continuity, capacity
continuity, and numerical experiential identity.

IER identifies qualitative differences with physical differences in the
organization of intrinsic constraint. It does not seek a further fact
explaining why one organization is experienced as red, painful, or otherwise
qualitatively particular. On the identity claim, the organized physical
operation is that particular experience. Demanding another bridge between the
organization and its character would deny the proposed identity and restart
the regress.

This does not settle the descriptive correspondence problem: mapping physical
structural signatures and dynamics to particular qualitative characters remains
a continuing research programme. It is distinct from asking what additional
entity produces a quality beyond the constitutive physical organization.

Explanatory regress therefore terminates at the owned history - future boundary: at constraint, irreversible resolution, and the continuing physical organization that bears their consequences. Determining which physical organizations correspond to which reported qualities is a separate problem of description or classification. It is not a deeper ontological explanation of why those organizations have their qualitative character.

## Consequences and limits

IER’s identity claim is strict. If the complete physical organization constitutive of a UEF is duplicated, experiential presence and character cannot vary independently. Behavioral, functional, computational, representational, or observational equivalence does not establish such duplication, because each may leave the relevant intrinsic organization unsettled.

Experience does not by itself entail intelligence, language, reportability, life, sophisticated cognition, truth, justification, adaptive function, voluntary control, or privileged access to mind-independent reality. A system may be highly capable without being experiential, and experience need not provide the capacities required to recognize or report itself.

Experiential agency, in its specification-level sense, is a derived capacity of
a UEF. Its exercise and its effective, deliberative, phenomenological, or
functional expressions may be impaired or absent; functional control can also
occur without experience.

IER’s refusal of diagnostics is a structural epistemic boundary of the
framework, not a temporary limitation or a retreat from physical identity.
Non-diagnosability concerns observational or descriptive equivalence, not
complete physical identity. The framework does not turn outward behavior or
abstract functional organization into a certification procedure for either
experiential presence or absence.

IER also distinguishes experiential ontology from ethics. Its canonical ethical commitments follow from the identity claim: subjecthood entails categorical moral standing, and damage to a UEF’s organization constitutes experiential harm. These commitments do not by themselves provide a complete moral theory, rank subjects, settle conflicts, assign responsibility, or determine permissible action.


IER can state its proposed regime entirely in physical terms: lawful configurations and transitions, admissible continuation, non-factorizable constraint, internally borne resolution, temporal continuity, and irreversible foreclosure. If those notions can be specified without experiential vocabulary, the proposal is not definitionally circular. It does not define a UEF merely as whatever is conscious.

That achievement would not by itself prove the identity claim. Showing that a physical regime is coherently describable, or that familiar features of experience follow if the identity is accepted, does not establish that experience must be identical with that regime. The proposal still bears the central philosophical burden of an identity theory: explaining why it should be accepted as identity rather than correlation, realization, or structural analogy.

The formal individuation of a UEF remains a continuing research burden. A
successful account must state non-arbitrary admissibility conditions and
adequate partitions without building global unity into the analysis by
stipulation. Whether a particular cell, organ, tree, organism, artificial
system, distributed architecture, or corporate superorganism instantiates a
UEF is generally an epistemically underdetermined attribution question unless
structural analysis or theorem rules it out. These are open questions about
precision, justification, and application, not a failure to define the
theory’s structural necessities or an invitation to replace the proposal with
an empirical consciousness detector.

Ordinary corporations remain excluded only under their currently described
delegated, decomposable, slack-admitting, and externally mediated
organization. A future artificial or corporate superorganism could instantiate
a UEF if its physical organization satisfies the same conditions; legal status
does not decide experiential status.

IER should therefore be read neither as a completed proof nor as a loose metaphor. It is a specific identity proposal:

> Experience is not something produced by sufficiently organized matter. It is the operation of a physical system when constraint becomes globally unavoidable, internally borne, temporally continuous, and constitutive of one unified trajectory.
