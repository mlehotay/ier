# Neural Unity and Binding: A Conditional Research Program

**Status:** exploratory proposal · non-canonical · no experiential diagnostic

## Question and scope

Could competition for the physical means of neural continuation help organize
distributed processes into one jointly constrained operation? If so, what
additional evidence would distinguish that operation from processes that merely
share fuel, communicate, synchronize, or depend on the same supplier?

This proposal develops a possible **neural implementation** of IER's existing
structural questions. It neither adds a UEF criterion nor asserts that a human
UEF has a neural boundary, a single biochemical resource, or any particular
anatomical location. A physical mechanism can be studied without treating its
measurement as a test for experience. The [Specification](../../IER/IER-specification.md),
[Intrinsic Closure](../../IER/IER-intrinsic-closure.md),
[Ownership](../../IER/IER-ownership.md),
[Processes](../../IER/IER-processes.md), and
[Continuity](../../IER/IER-continuity.md) retain authority over those relations.

## Related work and attribution

Francis G. Smith Jr.'s [*No Spoon Framework: Conscious Experience as a
Structurally Realized Coherent Field*](https://philpapers.org/rec/SMINSF-4)
is a related, separately authored theory. Smith proposes that coherent neural
and subcellular phase configurations realize experience when they satisfy a
non-dynamical admissibility constraint, Φq. That is a specific structural
realization claim, and it should be credited to Smith.

The present proposal develops a different, still unverified question from the
IER articles cited below: whether physical support and neural activity jointly
produce and bear one continuing restriction. It does not adopt Smith's Φq,
phase-topology account, or realization criterion, and it makes no claim of
priority over his work. Shared terms such as *constraint*, *coherence*, and
*unity* do not by themselves establish that the two accounts have the same
mechanism or that either was derived from the other. A fuller comparison would
require a version-specific reading of Smith's manuscript.

## Proposed mechanism

The initial conjecture is that candidate neural processes depend on limited,
renewed physical support for their continuation. Consumption, delivery,
replenishment, and local activity alter which combinations of those processes
can continue. The relevant support might involve energy substrates, ionic
restoration, transport, or several coupled processes; **the proposal has not
identified a single global pool**. “Resource” here means a physically specified
process and its available states, not an amount of experience, willpower, or
spoons.

Competition becomes a candidate *unity mechanism* only if the participating
processes and support processes jointly produce and bear the same restriction on
their next physically possible organization. The claim is stronger than a common
budget: a common supplier can limit two otherwise independent operations. In
the proposed case, changing one participant must change the possibilities of
the others *through the candidate operation*, and the resulting changes must
feed into that operation's later continuation. The [resource-loop comparison in
Processes](../../IER/IER-processes.md#phosphorylation-in-a-beaker) gives the
relevant distinction between endogenous interlock and external supply.

The proposed chain is:

```text
physical support and current neural organization
    → bounded possibilities for joint participation
    → changing demand, coupling, and sustainment geometry
    → ordinary continuation, or a conditional foreclosure event
    → if foreclosure occurs: welding → propagation → sedimentation
    → inherited organization constrains later participation and support use
```

The IER Constraint Cycle describes multiplicity, deformation, *conditional*
collapse, and ratification. It does not require a new collapse on every neural
update. IER's welding account incorporates
the consequences of an established collapse; later ordinary succession can
inherit them without another welding event. Thus recruitment can change load
without being identified with collapse or welding.

## Why spoons, capacities, and headroom matter

IER's Spoons article describes the margin for further participation
within an already qualifying UEF. Rate Control
connects added participation to proximity to a coherence boundary under
accumulating irreversible consequences. Human Integration
adds a tempo question: when new demands arrive faster than collapse, welding,
and propagation can be integrated, later demands encounter an organization
already under deformation. Together, these articles supply a **conditional
spoon-to-welding bridge**: additional recruitment may increase the demands that
must be integrated, and prior welded consequences can change what further
recruitment costs. Neither step says that every demand causes foreclosure.

IER's Capacities article distinguishes participation,
attenuation, governance, welding, rate control, and energetic support. These
are different boundaries, not interchangeable amounts drawn from one reserve.
Headroom describes how approach to each boundary
may be lived *within* an existing UEF. The proposed neural mechanism therefore
must keep two questions apart:

1. **Constitution:** Do the physical processes form one internally owned,
   non-factorizable, temporally continuing operation?
2. **Load within an operation:** How do support, recruitment, rate, and inherited
   deformation affect its possible continuations?

The second question can constrain an answer to the first. Spoon reports or
headroom descriptions cannot establish the first answer.

## What a physical model must specify

Before testing a unity claim, fix the candidate operation, variables, grain,
interval, physical boundary, intervention family, and meaningful partitions
without choosing them to secure a desired result. The model then needs the
following pieces.

| Missing piece | Required account |
| --- | --- |
| Physical support | Identify the substances or processes, their delivery and replenishment, and the time and spatial scales on which availability limits continuation. Test whether one or several limits matter. |
| Coupling rule | Show how changes in one neural process alter the *possible continuations* of others through the proposed support-and-activity loop. Covariation, competition, or a scalar budget alone is insufficient. |
| Closure | Compare the global outgoing successor set with projections from that same set across independently meaningful partitions. Show where the proposed joint support fails to factorize and where it does factorize. |
| Ownership and membership | Distinguish internal production and bearing of the restriction from indispensable external supply. Assess contribution, global bearing, joint resolution, diachronic uptake, and common physical typing for each proposed participant. |
| Temporal production | Show how current activity and support organization physically produce later organization, including inherited consequences and any actual boundary change. A repeated pattern or persistent supplier does not establish token continuity. |
| Conditional events | Identify any irreversible foreclosure independently of greater cost, slowing, selection, or a single observed outcome. Attribute welding and propagation only after that event. |
| Scale and boundaries | Compare neural, neural–glial, vascular, and wider organism candidates. Shared matter, anatomy, and nesting do not predetermine the number or extent of qualifying operations. |
| Phenomenal bridge | State separately what would warrant identifying a completely characterized physical operation with experience. A successful resource model could remain an account of neural coordination alone. |

The constitutive comparison should use the same operation and interval for all
of these claims. One cannot combine a resource interaction at one scale with
closure at another and persistence at a third to manufacture a UEF.

## Research sequence and discriminating observations

1. **Build a bounded physical model.** Start with a tractable neural system
   whose activity, supply, replenishment, and relevant successor possibilities
   can be independently described. Specify alternative independent-supplier
   and shared-operation models before fitting outcomes.
2. **Vary demand and supply separately.** Perturb one candidate process's demand,
   then perturb supply or replenishment while tracking the other processes' live
   possibilities and later organization. Separate common slowing from changes
   in their joint continuation structure.
3. **Test partitions and replacement.** Ask whether the modeled successors of
   proposed parts recombine freely. Replace or bypass a proposed support process
   while preserving the relevant alternatives, not just one observed input
   sequence. If independently completed supply preserves those alternatives,
   that supplier was external to the tested restriction.
4. **Test tempo.** Compare demand arrival and incorporation over intervals in
   which conditional irreversible events can be independently identified. Look
   for distinctive effects of event rate, recruitment breadth, and inherited
   deformation instead of treating all overload as depletion of one pool.
5. **Compare boundary cases.** Model coordinated activity with independent
   supplies, a common supply without joint resolution, and an endogenous loop.
   Any proposed criterion should distinguish these cases before it is applied
   to complex brains.

Evidence for supply-sensitive coordination would support a neural mechanism.
Evidence for a non-factorizable, internally borne, continuing operation would
support a stronger structural reconstruction. Neither result, nor neural
recordings, behavior, reports, or a perturbation effect, would by itself certify
experience or locate a subject. See [Deep Brain Localization](IER-deep-brain-localization.md)
for the distinction between causal support and constitutive participation.

## Connections to the other neuroscience notes

The existing IER reinterpretations help **design and delimit** this program.
They do not supply evidence that resource competition already unifies a UEF.

| Topic | Contribution to this proposal | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Deep-brain localization](IER-deep-brain-localization.md) | Thalamic perturbation, distributed arousal, split-brain cases, and hemispherotomy motivate comparisons among enabling supply, coupling pathways, and constitutive participation. Disconnection makes candidate boundaries a physical question. | A necessary hub or successful perturbation does not locate the subject or show that a resource process is constitutive. |
| [Four durations, not one](IER-four-durations-not-one.md) | Its separation of stimulus time, fitted cortical response, report criterion, and experienced duration supplies a model for keeping resource dynamics, neural coordination, behavior, and phenomenology distinct. Its proposed task manipulations can help vary recruitment while controlling sensory input and decisions. | Distributed response profiles and cross-region covariance do not establish causal flow, shared successor structure, collapse, or experiential unity. |
| [Neuroscience primer](neuroscience-primer.md) | Its measurement ledger shows why a fitted BOLD parameter or correlation cluster cannot be read as a millisecond-scale trajectory or an intervention result. A unity study needs measurements at the timescale of its proposed coupling and incorporation. | The duration experiment did not measure resource flows or the IER constraint cycle. |
| [The past is in the present](IER-past-in-the-present.md) | Organoid developmental results illustrate how earlier physical changes can alter present organization and later possibilities. This suggests testing whether a proposed neural support loop carries *causally effective* history rather than merely a marker of elapsed time. | Developmental lineage and retained competence do not establish welding, one continuing UEF token, or experience in organoids. |
| [Ambien and complex sleep behaviors](IER-ambien-hypothesis.md) | Preserved routine coordination alongside possibly impaired redirection is a useful contrast case for testing whether local behavioral organization can continue when wider recruitment or governance changes. Later amnesia should be measured separately. | Coordinated behavior, impaired control, drug exposure, and absent recall do not determine whether a UEF continued or ceased. |

Taken together, these notes suggest three refinements to the research sequence:

1. Compare proposed resource and coupling boundaries across intervention and
   disconnection cases, including deep structures and wider support processes.
2. Measure supply, neural activity, recruitment, and inherited change at their
   relevant timescales; keep behavior and reports as separate outcomes.
3. Use preserved local function, altered global coordination, and effective
   physical history as contrast cases. Require a model that predicts their
   differences before assigning them to IER's stages.

These reinterpretations make the hypothesis more discriminating. None shows
that neural support is one global pool, that resource competition passes the
closure and ownership tests, or that a measured neural organization is
identical with experience.

## Current assessment

The proposal has an explanatory route from physical support through
participation, conditional settlement, and inherited organization. Its decisive
missing piece is a **specified physical coupling law** showing why the candidate
processes have one jointly resolved successor problem. The exact support
processes, their boundary, and the causal role of glial or vascular support are
also open. The later step from that physical account to experiential identity
remains an independent argument. Until those pieces are supplied, this is a
research program for neural unity and binding, not a theory that a scarce
biochemical resource unifies the UEF.
