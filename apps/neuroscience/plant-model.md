# Distributed Regulation in Plants
## Transport Gates, Growth, and Conditions of Continuation

**Informational Experiential Realism (IER v10.12.3)**\
*Research Proposal · Non-Normative · Non-Canonical · Canon-Constrained*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This proposal examines plant regulation as a non-neural comparison for the
`IER-competition.md` and
`IER-semaphores.md` hypotheses. Its question is how local
signalling, transport, and growth processes change the conditions under which
other activity proceeds, and how that regulation is maintained through time.

The document proposes an application of constraint cartography and a bounded
investigation design. No new plant experiment,
simulation, or source-data reanalysis has been performed. Published mechanisms,
candidate correspondences, and unresolved physical questions retain separate
status. This application introduces no canonical mechanism, unity test,
experiential attribution, or criterion for plant consciousness. Theory
authority remains with the canonical corpus.

The document belongs alongside the
[neurotransmitter proposal](neurotransmitter-proposal.md) because both examine
regulated access and persistent changes in recruitment conditions. The
plant comparison does not require a neural implementation. Existing
[biological transport assessments](biological-transport.md) provide molecular
context without establishing the new correspondences proposed here.

## Research Method and Its Applications

Constraint cartography reconstructs how present physical organization
facilitates, impedes, stabilizes, redirects, or prevents specified transitions
within a declared model, boundary, and interval. It admits rival maps and
requires each proposed relation to retain its evidence and failure conditions.
“Constraint” at this stage is model-relative physical restriction, not an
automatic attribution of canonical intrinsic constraint.

Paper 02 (`pub/articles/paper-02-constraint-cartography.md`) is a worked
application to *Physarum*, with a methodological exposition in section 2.
Paper 05 (`pub/articles/paper-05-slime-mold-intelligence.md`) applies IER
interpretation to that reconstruction and examines which stronger descriptions
the evidence warrants. The plant proposal is another application of the
reusable cartographic approach. It does not treat the slime-mold materials,
mechanisms, or manuscript-specific assumptions as requirements of the method.
Current canonical sources control any disagreement with older manuscript
formulations, including retired successor-support unity criteria.

The controlled-investigation SOP
(`apps/research/controlled-investigation-sop.md`)
supplies the maintained local procedure for testing a selected discrimination:
bounded question, material declarations, justified comparison rule, qualified
apparatus, preserved evidence, inspectable derivation, and a bounded result.
Cartography organizes the proposed explanation; controlled investigation tests
particular relations or rival maps. A completed map is not itself a passed
experiment, and repository lint checks verify neither biological mechanisms
nor experiential identity.

These manuscripts and the SOP are repository-only references, outside the
public export. The methodological commitments used here are stated below so
that the proposal can be read independently.

### Cartographic Target and Present Readiness

An instance map explains a particular recorded plant response. A mechanism
map examines a recurring relation, such as localization-dependent transport.
An organizational map examines how mechanisms compose, compete, and change
across scales. The first selected target here is a mechanism map for hypocotyl
phototropism. Whole-plant composition and the IER correspondence are distinct
later questions; a population-level regularity does not reconstruct one
individual trial.

The present mode is exploratory source reconstruction and protocol development.
No confirmatory protocol is frozen and no experimental result is assigned.

| Question | Current readiness and next burden |
| --- | --- |
| What relation between PIN3 localization, transport, and growth does the selected plant literature establish? | `PARTIAL`: the question is bounded, but full source reading and exact intervention/measurement reconstruction remain necessary. |
| Does a specified localization change contribute to subsequent directional response beyond changes in total transporter abundance or general growth capacity? | `PARTIAL`: a defensible comparison requires a selected preparation, independently justified perturbation, qualified readouts, and a prespecified decision rule. |
| Does a second demand compete for the same enabling condition? | `UNDER-SPECIFIED` for the present design: the condition, competing activities, and discriminating intervention have not been fixed. Opposite growth effects alone cannot answer it. |
| Does the resulting plant organization constitute experiential unity? | `NON-EMPIRICAL` relative to these proposed measurements: they do not decide identity or establish full qualification. |

These are readiness assessments, not execution outcomes. Source reconstruction
must distinguish the source's original claims from methodological structure
added here; it does not retroactively preregister a published study.

## Organization Without a Brain

The absence of a brain does not prevent describing organization at the scale
of an organism. A physical model can connect local sensing, biochemical
responses, transport between cells, and changes in tissue growth or physiology.
Regulation may be distributed across the participating tissues rather than
concentrated in one anatomical controller.

Plant models therefore need not introduce a final decision-making organ. They
can specify how current conditions affect local activity, how that activity
changes conditions elsewhere, and how those consequences affect subsequent
activity. The plant examples below provide different instances of such
relations; they are not interchangeable components of one demonstrated cycle.

Three explanatory scopes must remain distinct:

- A local mechanism, such as transporter polarization or ion-channel activation.
- Coordination across cells, tissues, or organs through specified interactions.
- The proposed constitutive organization of a qualifying continuing operation.

The first two can constrain the third without diagnosing it. Neither the
anatomical outline of a plant nor a signal traveling across that outline
establishes the boundary of one UEF. Conversely, an independently decisive
one-organization classifier is not a prerequisite that this proposal promises
to supply before identity applies.

## Light Responses and Physiological Stress

Phototropism is directional growth in relation to light. It supplies a useful
case for rooted plants because the response changes tissue shape and the
conditions under which later light is encountered. Phototaxis concerns
directed movement of an organism. In the green alga *Chlamydomonas*,
channelrhodopsin-mediated light responses participate in swimming regulation.
That is a separate model system with a different motor implementation.
[Primary algal study](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.108.057919).

Plant stress describes physiological conditions and responses, such as those
associated with water limitation or injury. A study should name the imposed
condition and its measured consequences rather than treating “stress” as one
variable. Physiological stress is not by itself evidence of experienced fear,
pain, or distress; that distinction supplies no general verdict about plant
experience or moral significance.

## Candidate Mechanisms

### Transporter Localization and Phototropism

Ding et al. (2011) report that light changes the cellular polarization of the
auxin efflux transporter PIN3 in *Arabidopsis* hypocotyl endodermis cells.
Their account connects regulated trafficking, auxin redistribution, and
differential growth. The relevant enabling arrangement includes where a
transporter is located, rather than only its presence or total abundance.
[Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb2208).

For the semaphore comparison, the candidate is a transporter at a physically
specified membrane location, together with the processes maintaining or
changing that location. Retention can sustain a route; redistribution can
alter which route is effective. It remains necessary to distinguish actual
transport, transporter localization, hormone-response readouts, and growth.
None is an automatic substitute for the others.

### Guard-Cell Regulation and Stomatal Access

Brandt et al. (2012) reconstitute ABA-dependent activation of the SLAC1 anion
channel through kinase/phosphatase signalling routes. Their study supplies a
mechanistic lead for regulation of guard-cell channels associated with stomatal
closure, rather than a complete measurement of all stomatal control in an
intact plant.
[Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1116590109).

This comparison contains distinct levels of gating: receptor and signalling
states regulate channels, while guard-cell physiology regulates the stomatal
pore. Calling the pore a gateway does not identify a mobile successor
indicator. The proposed access factor, site, downstream process, and conditions
of release must be identified separately.

### Local Signals and Distant Responses

Toyota et al. (2018) show that glutamate acts as a wound signal and that
glutamate-receptor-like channels participate in calcium signals propagating
to distant organs, where defense responses are induced. The result provides
a concrete lead for distributed coordination across a plant.
[Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat7744).

The shared chemical name does not make this a neural circuit or establish an
experiential interpretation. The questions for a map concern release, channel
activity, propagation, local response, and feedback. Long-distance influence
alone does not establish constitutive membership of every affected tissue.

## Connection to Existing Biological Transport Work

The existing MON1-CCZ1/Rab7 assessment of
[Cui et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.114.123141)
distinguishes regulator recruitment, nucleotide exchange, compartment identity,
and cargo delivery in plant trafficking. The
ALIX/retromer assessment of
[Hu et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200492119)
distinguishes component abundance, membrane recruitment, receptor retrieval,
and cargo sorting. These distinctions are directly useful for constructing a
map of effective access.

The detailed reading records are repository-only source notes,
`references/notes/cui-et-al-2014-mon1-ccz1-rab7-activation.md` and
`references/notes/hu-et-al-2022-alix-retromer-vacuolar-sorting.md`;
they are not included in the public export. The
[biological transport companion](biological-transport.md) provides the shared
assessment and its limits.

Those studies do not establish the complete PIN3 phototropism mechanism or
join Rab7 activation and ALIX-dependent retrieval into one observed sequence.
The new proposal must obtain evidence for each particular connection. A shared
role in plant membrane traffic is a research lead, not proof that one assessed
mechanism controls every light or stress response.

The comparison with the mouse hypothalamus is likewise organizational rather
than a claim of chemical equivalence. The neural study examines opposing
receptor routes and persistent cAMP effects; the plant proposal examines
specified transport and signalling arrangements that change later activity.
Shared vocabulary does not establish one molecular implementation across them.

## A Bounded First Study: Arabidopsis Phototropism

The proposed first case is an explicitly delimited hypocotyl phototropic
response. Whole-plant organization remains a wider question. Begin with the
local route and expand the candidate scope only where the evidence requires it.

The bounded empirical question is whether a physically specified change in
PIN3 localization contributes to a subsequent directional auxin-response and
growth difference beyond changes in total transporter abundance and general
growth capacity. This is a question about a causal contribution under stated
conditions, not a universal necessity or sufficiency claim about PIN3,
phototropism, or unity.

The initial mapping hypothesis is:

```text
light distribution and current tissue state
    → light perception and signalling
    → transporter localization and effective auxin transport
    → spatial differences in auxin response
    → differential growth and changed tissue shape
    → changed conditions for subsequent sensing and growth
```

This is a proposed explanatory loop, not a single published experiment that
has measured every arrow. Each connection needs a source and an evidence
status. Physical bending is a growth outcome; it is not automatically IER
curvature. A change in growth direction is not automatically refusal or collapse.

### Rival Maps and Discriminating Evidence

The reconstruction must preserve alternatives capable of explaining the same
observed bending. The following are candidate rivals to assess against the
full sources, rather than findings already established in this proposal.

| Candidate map | Distinguishing burden |
| --- | --- |
| Localization-mediated route | Changing the relevant localization changes subsequent spatial response in a way not explained by total abundance or broad growth impairment. |
| Abundance or supply explanation | Differences in transporter amount or available auxin account for the response without the proposed localization effect being independently established. |
| General developmental or physiological impairment | The perturbation changes overall growth capacity or health, making reduced bending an inadequate witness for a specific transport route. |
| Measurement explanation | Reporter kinetics, light-dependent fluorescence, segmentation, or tissue movement account for the apparent spatial or temporal relation. |

Several maps may contribute at once. A useful discrimination can reject an
exclusive explanation or identify an unresolved mixture without selecting
one complete mechanism. If feasible interventions cannot separate localization
from abundance, the intended causal comparison remains indeterminate rather
than being settled by a marker image.

### Establish a Common Preparation and Baseline

Specify plant line, developmental stage, tissue, illumination history, light
direction and intensity, temperature, water conditions, and observation interval.
Record baseline growth and reporter behavior. Separate developmental
differences from responses to a change in illumination.

Use matched controls and an analysis plan that recognizes plants, cells,
and repeated images as different sampling units. A large collection of image
frames does not supply that many independently tested plants.

Keep the relevant history and environment within the explanation. Genotype,
development, prior illumination, hydration, and existing morphology are
standing conditions; the imposed light field and growth medium are
environmental conditions. Changes occurring during the trial are online
organization. A later acquired state requires evidence of what present
physical difference persists and affects subsequent response. Neither the
growth chamber nor the entire organism belongs inside every candidate scope
merely because it matters causally.

### Track Enabling Arrangements and Outcomes Separately

Follow transporter localization, an appropriately characterized auxin-response
readout, and growth through time. Record total abundance as well as spatial
distribution where the assay permits it. A fluorescent localization marker
does not directly measure transport flux; an auxin-response reporter does not
automatically report hormone concentration. The exact reporter and measurement
model must be justified before interpreting the observations.

The comparison asks whether changes in an enabling arrangement precede and
help explain changed activity. Correlated timing is useful but insufficient
for a causal claim.

Before decisive measurement, qualify the smallest apparatus needed for the
question. Check localization analysis against inspectable reference images,
assess reporter calibration and delay, and examine movement, illumination,
and bleaching artifacts. Missing required frames or an uncalibrated reporter
must not produce a positive localization-to-growth verdict. Controls should
address these actual failure modes; no universal control bundle is required.

### Perturb, Redirect, and Assess Recovery

Compare a stable light condition with a controlled change or reversal of
light direction. Investigate a justified perturbation of the receptor,
signalling, or transporter-localization process, with controls for general
growth impairment and tissue damage. Appropriate genetic comparisons or
validated reversible manipulations should distinguish the intended target
from broad disruption; rescue or complementation can add evidence where
available.

Measure onset, amplitude, persistence, and recovery of the response. Recovery
concerns subsequent regulation and growth capacity: already-produced tissue
shape need not return to its earlier form. Irreversible physical growth alone
does not establish canonical irreversible foreclosure under intrinsic constraint.

### Fix the Decision Rule Before Confirmation

Source reconstruction and pilot observations must first justify the primary
contrast, intervention specificity, time windows, biologically meaningful
effect range, uncertainty analysis, and exclusions. This proposal supplies no
arbitrary numerical threshold in their place. A confirmatory version must fix
these material choices before examining its decisive outcomes.

A positive witness would combine a verified change in the specified
localization arrangement with the predicted subsequent spatial-response
contrast under controls that distinguish abundance and general-growth rivals.
A negative witness requires enough measurement precision and target engagement
to contradict the stated prediction at the tested scope. A failed reporter,
ineffective intervention, or unresolved broad impairment supplies neither.

Preserve raw images, timestamps, environmental and intervention records,
sampling units, exclusions, analysis versions, and intermediate measurements.
Expose the derivation from images to localization and response estimates,
from those estimates to the declared contrast, and from the contrast to the
result. A fitted growth curve or final map label cannot replace that trace.

Use the SOP's narrow result classes when an investigation is executed:
`SUPPORT` for a witnessed local prediction, `COUNTEREXAMPLE` for a controlled
conflict with it, `INDETERMINATE` for unresolved alternatives,
`APPARATUS-FAIL` for failed measurement or analysis, and `OUT-OF-SCOPE` where
applicability conditions fail. An unlicensed decision rule yields
`UNDER-SPECIFIED`; evidence incapable of deciding the claim may yield
`NOT-TESTABLE`. No result class is claimed by this document.

Material changes after decisive execution require a new protocol version.
Retain earlier observations and outcomes as exploratory or diagnostic work
where appropriate. Reproduction from preserved inputs and an independent
repeat of the biological comparison remain separate achievements.

### Add a Second Demand After the Local Route Is Understood

A later experiment can introduce a controlled water-availability condition
while holding the light comparison interpretable. Ask whether the added
condition changes growth, transport organization, signalling efficacy, or
recovery. If stomatal regulation is included, give it a separate measured
route and timescale rather than inserting an assumed connection to PIN3.

The second-demand extension needs its own bounded question, readiness review,
and rival-map comparison before execution. The aim is to test interaction
between continuation conditions. Two treatments
having opposite growth effects does not by itself establish competition for a
shared resource or one constitutive resolution process. The mechanism of
interaction remains an experimental question.

## Building the Process Map and Model

The map should distinguish biochemical processes within compartments,
transport across interfaces, tissue mechanics, and the external conditions
they encounter. A model may combine reaction dynamics, transport relations,
and growth mechanics. Such a model needs physically interpreted variables
and parameters; putting everything into one equation does not establish unity.

| Map element | Required description |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate scope | Cells, tissues, and interfaces under investigation, without a presumed subject boundary |
| Activity | Production, transport, binding interaction, modification, consumption, or growth actually occurring |
| Enabling site or state | The membrane location, channel state, concentration, or structural condition affecting access |
| Recruitment and compatibility | Which activities can proceed together and what prevents or limits other combinations |
| Retention and redistribution | What stays in place, what moves or changes configuration, and how this affects subsequent access |
| Maintenance | Which processes produce or repair the enabling organization; what merely persists or is externally supplied |
| History | Onset, persistence, turnover, recovery, and consequences affecting later activity |
| Evidence | Species, preparation, source passage, assay, intervention, sampling unit, and alternative explanations |
| Origin | Standing, online, acquired, environmental, or developmental/lifecycle-conditioned organization, with its actual bearer |
| Transition effect | Which specified transition is facilitated, impeded, stabilized, redirected, or unavailable relative to the comparison |
| Rival and failure condition | Which alternative accounts for the same observation, what discriminates it, and what would revise or reject this relation |
| Evidence status | Measured association, intervention-supported relation, mechanistic interpretation, or proposed correspondence |

These are question-dependent recording prompts for this application, not a
new mandatory schema or universal dossier. They preserve the cartographic
distinctions of bearer, locus, timescale, origin, evidence, transition effect,
rival, and failure without forcing every investigation into identical fields.

The model should keep ligand availability, receptor activation, transporter
placement, transport throughput, and growth capacity distinct. None supplies a
universal conserved currency. Where an edge is unknown, preserve the gap rather
than completing it from a shared protein name or the desired whole-plant account.

Composition needs evidence of its own. A localization mechanism demonstrated
in one tissue and a channel mechanism demonstrated in another do not form a
single organizational map until their connecting transport, influence, timing,
and relevant physical context are justified. Record inferred or absent bridges
explicitly. A map may remain mechanistically plural while explaining a
particular response; there is no requirement to reduce all plant behavior to
one semaphore mechanism.

## Frontier Geometry and Unity: Proposed Interpretation

The IER comparison follows the SOP's bounded relational-transfer procedure.
Its source relation must first be reconstructed in plant-biological terms.
The candidate extracted relation is that spatial recruitment or retention of
an enabling component changes the efficacy of a later transport-dependent
response. This is the relation to investigate, not a transfer of auxin, PIN3,
or the plant's anatomical outline into IER.

Compare relevant cases of redistribution, retained localization with changed
throughput, recovery, and persistent downstream response after an enabling
state changes, where source evidence permits them. Preserve failures or
partial correspondences. If the comparison only makes an existing IER
distinction easier to explain, record explanatory value rather than support
for the identity theory. This is not a blind-convergence study or independent
confirmation of unity.

The empirical model can investigate which subsequent activities remain possible
under specified physical conditions and interventions. It cannot exhaust all
possible plant futures. These bounded comparisons concern physiological
continuation and are not automatically a UEF's canonical future cone.

The frontier-geometry interpretation asks how occurring activity changes the
conditions, compatibility, and costs of later continuation. Bias and curvature
retain their canonical meanings; growth angle, hormone concentration, or
channel count does not directly measure either. The proposal requires a
physically justified correspondence rather than a terminological relabelling.

The semaphore interpretation asks whether specific enabling factors are
retained, released, or redistributed in that organization. It must distinguish
a physical factor moving between sites from a changed state persisting at one
site. A gate may regulate local activity while depending on a separately
continuing supplier. A common environmental condition can also affect several
independent activities. Neither relation alone establishes one operation.

The stronger competition/unity hypothesis concerns constitutive activities
whose continuation is jointly organized. Its physical articulation remains
open. Changing continuation conditions does not by itself establish maintenance
of constitutive organization, full membership, ownership, or token continuity.
Under IER's identity proposal, experiential unity follows conditionally for a
qualifying operation; this plant investigation supplies no independent unity
classifier or experiential verdict.

## Source Standing and Research Outputs

The existing plant transport papers have dedicated source notes with explicit
reading limits. The phototropism, stomatal, systemic-defense, and algal studies
above are primary-source leads assessed here at the available abstract or
summary level. Their full methods, figures, and supplements require dedicated
reading before selecting experimental parameters or asserting detailed
cross-study mechanisms. They have not been added to the acquired-source
collection or treated as completed full-paper assessments.

The first useful outputs would be a bounded source-linked process map,
explicit assay and intervention choices, a model distinguishing localization
from effective access, and a record of supported and unsupported semaphore
correspondences. Later expansion could investigate communication between
tissues and maintenance through changing conditions. A distributed regulation
model would be an explanatory result in its own right, without requiring a
claim that the plant is a subject or that the proposed unity account is proven.

The immediate next step is full source reconstruction and readiness assessment,
not commissioning the proposed plant experiment. A later investigation record
must identify the exact question and source span, its mode, material conditions,
qualified apparatus, comparison rule, primary evidence, derivation, bounded
outcome, and remaining reproduction or transfer work. This can be kept in one
readable record with links to existing evidence. Missing empirical evidence
returns to the plant-source question; ambiguous semaphore meanings return to
their canonical owners without being repaired inside a model or analysis.
