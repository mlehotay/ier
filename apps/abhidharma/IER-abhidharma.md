# Abhidharma as a Layered Identification Problem

## A Source-Constrained IER Reconstruction

*Informational Experiential Realism (IER)* - *Comparative Reconstruction | Source-Constrained | Non-Normative | Non-Criterial*

## Status, Scope, and Authority

This document develops a framework for reconstructing selected Abhidharma analyses under Informational Experiential Realism (IER). It is a comparative research document, not an extension of IER canon and not an interpretation of Buddhist doctrine as authoritative.

It introduces:

- no new IER ontology, mechanism, criterion, diagnostic, or mathematical quantity;
- no claim that Buddhist thinkers anticipated IER;
- no claim that IER validates an Abhidharma school;
- no one-to-one translation between Abhidharma and IER terms; and
- no inference from structural analogy to ontological or numerical identity.

The historical and cross-school claims in this document rely primarily on the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP) article on Abhidharma. Detailed examples from the mature Theravāda Abhidhamma system use *A Comprehensive Manual of Abhidhamma*, a modern edition and guide to Anuruddha's post-canonical *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha*.[^sources]

These sources have different authority and scope. The SEP article supplies a critical history of several Abhidharma traditions. Bodhi's manual presents a Theravāda system from within its commentarial and manual tradition. Claims taken from the manual therefore concern that tradition unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Readers seeking historical orientation should begin with [*An IER Introduction to Abhidharma*](./IER-abhidharma-introduction.md). Readers seeking a comparison of the frameworks' commitments should consult [*IER and Abhidharma: Axes of Agreement, Divergence, and Non-Correspondence*](./IER-abhidharma-agreement-and-divergence.md). The present document has a different task:

> It specifies the analytical layers that must be kept separate before any particular Abhidharma distinction can be reconstructed under IER.

## The Replacement of the Atlas Thesis

The previous interpretation described Abhidharma as a phenomenological atlas and assigned IER the task of explaining why its experiential regions recur.

That proposal contained a useful intuition: recurring classifications may track recurring organization. But it compressed several different things into one supposed map:

- dhamma types;
- particular dhamma occurrences;
- citta-cetasika assemblages;
- conditional relations;
- sequences of consciousness moments;
- intersecting doctrinal classifications; and
- soteriological distinctions concerning what is to be abandoned or developed.

These are not different names for regions in one experiential state space. They are different analytical objects with different identity conditions.

The old division of labor was also mistaken:

```text
Abhidharma maps
IER explains
```

Abhidharma already classifies, individuates, relates, and explains within its own causal, metaphysical, epistemological, contemplative, and soteriological projects. IER has not derived its categories or conditional structures. Until it does, applying IER vocabulary may redescribe a traditional distinction without explaining it.

The stronger formulation is:

> Abhidharma supplies historically developed analyses whose proposed objects, boundaries, relations, and purposes can become identification problems for IER.

## The Central Identification Problem

IER identifies experience with the operation of a physical system as a Unified Experiential Field (UEF) under globally integrated intrinsic constraint. The physical organization does not first occur and then generate phenomenology as a second product. The physical and experiential descriptions concern one organization.

IER also permits several descriptive organizations over that one reality:

- structural signatures compress local admissible-future geometry;
- structural taxonomies group configurations by similarity of signature;
- coordinate systems organize explanatory language; and
- projections foreground bounded aspects of the same UEF organization.

These resources make comparison possible, but they do not settle it. The canonical IER claim that experiential categories can correspond to recurrent regions of structural similarity does not determine:

- what a historical term denotes;
- whether the term identifies a type, token, relation, process, or evaluation;
- which structural features distinguish it;
- whether all its uses share one organization;
- whether adjacent traditional categories are structurally distinct; or
- whether the traditional boundary reflects physical organization, doctrinal purpose, linguistic convention, or several of these together.

Consequently, the question is not:

> What is the IER translation of this Abhidharma term?

It is:

> Does a specified Abhidharma distinction track an independently identifiable IER organization, relation, or trajectory strongly enough to predict something that was not placed into the reconstruction by definition?

## Six Analytical Layers

The reconstruction begins by separating six layers.

| Layer | Traditional analytical object | Tempting shortcut | Legitimate IER question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dhamma type | Treat the name as an experiential signature | Does the type's identity and distribution track a recurring structural organization? |
| 2 | Dhamma token | Treat one occurrence as one IER state, event, collapse, or subject | What physical occurrence, if any, realizes the token, and how are its boundaries identified? |
| 3 | Citta-cetasika assemblage | Treat citta as a UEF and cetasikas as components or control variables | Does one UEF organization predict the assemblage's association and exclusion rules? |
| 4 | Conditional relation | Translate every condition as constraint or causal force | What are the relata, temporal structure, and physical realization of this particular relation? |
| 5 | Consciousness process | Treat a citta sequence as an IER trajectory by definition | Does an independently modeled physical trajectory predict the proposed functional order? |
| 6 | Soteriological classification | Equate wholesome, equanimous, or liberated with flexible or stable geometry | Is there a structural correlate, and can it be separated from the tradition's evaluative purpose? |

These layers interact, but interaction does not erase their differences. A dhamma type can enter an assemblage, occupy positions in several conditional schemes, recur through a process, and receive an evaluative classification. None of those roles is identical merely because one technical term participates in all of them.

## Layer 1: Dhamma Types and Structural Taxonomy

### The traditional object

In Abhidharma usage, *dharma* or *dhamma* can name both a category representing a possible type of occurrence and a particular occurrence of that type. The type is not simply an English label for a familiar feeling. Within a school-specific taxonomy it is individuated through intersecting classifications, characteristic functions, conditions, and relations to other dhammas.[^taxonomy]

The mature Theravāda manual system classifies conditioned reality under consciousness, mental factors, and material phenomena, with nibbāna as unconditioned. Sarvāstivāda uses a different inventory and includes an additional class of factors dissociated from thought as well as multiple unconditioned dharmas. The disagreement is evidence that there is no neutral Abhidharma list waiting to be translated into IER.

### The IER resource

An IER structural signature is a non-ontological compression of local admissible-future geometry. A structural typology groups configurations by similarity of signature. This gives IER a principled reason to expect recurring forms of experiential organization without making ordinary psychological vocabulary fundamental.

But the general typology does not establish a particular correspondence. At least five outcomes remain possible:

1. one dhamma type tracks one recurrent IER signature family;
2. several dhamma types divide one IER organization according to different functions or doctrinal purposes;
3. one dhamma type groups several physically distinct organizations by a shared role;
4. the relevant regularity appears only in a particular explanatory coordinate system; or
5. no stable IER correspondence exists.

### What would count as progress

A nontrivial reconstruction must specify the IER organization without using the traditional label to define it. It must then predict at least one traditional boundary, association, exclusion, or variation across contexts.

Drawing a region around all reported instances of a dhamma merely restates the classification. It does not explain why the region has those boundaries or why nearby cases are excluded.

## Layer 2: Dhamma Tokens and Occurrence Boundaries

A dhamma token is a particular occurrence of a type. It must not be confused with the type itself, but it also must not be assigned automatically to any familiar IER unit.

IER distinguishes among:

- a physical configuration;
- a lawful transition;
- an atomic contraction of reachability;
- an articulated event spanning deformation, collapse, and propagation;
- a stable regime; and
- a continuously operating UEF.

These units answer different questions. An Abhidharma token is therefore not, by definition:

- one IER state;
- one collapse;
- one event;
- one regime;
- one UEF; or
- one experiential subject.

The reconstruction must identify token boundaries independently. A reportable occurrence may be an articulated projection over extended physical change rather than a physically atomic event. Conversely, several traditionally distinguished occurrences may be different classifications of one continuous physical organization.

This is also why momentariness cannot perform the identification for us. The duration of a dharma token, the duration of a citta-cetasika assemblage, the temporal resolution of a scholastic model, the duration of an IER event, and the continuity of a UEF are separate temporal questions.[^time]

## Layer 3: Citta-Cetasika Assemblages

### The Theravāda analysis

In the mature Theravāda presentation, *citta* is the activity of cognizing an object. It never occurs as a bare, isolated consciousness. Mental factors (*cetasikas*) arise and cease with it, share its object and, where applicable, its physical basis, and perform differentiated tasks in the total cognitive occurrence.[^assemblage]

The manual analyzes association in two directions:

- from a mental factor to the types of citta with which it can occur; and
- from a citta type to the combination of mental factors included in it.

This produces exact combinatorial claims. For example, the system identifies seven mental factors as universal to every citta and restricts other factors to specified classes of consciousness. Whether those claims are correct is an empirical and interpretive question, but their form matters: they are not merely observations that experiential qualities overlap or blend.[^association]

### The IER comparison

A UEF is a globally integrated physical regime whose intrinsic constraint is not factorizable into independently resolving subsystem constraints. Participating processes do not become separate or fractional subjects.

That does not make a citta-cetasika assemblage a UEF. Nor does it make a cetasika:

- a physical subsystem;
- a component subject;
- a dimension of a structural signature;
- a control parameter; or
- a force acting on an experiential landscape.

The most promising research target is not verbal correspondence but the association matrix itself.

Suppose an independently specified physical organization were proposed for a class of citta-cetasika occurrences. A successful IER reconstruction would have to predict why some putative factors always co-occur, why others are excluded, and why the permitted combinations vary with citta type. It would also need to determine whether the traditional factors correspond to separable physical regularities, functional descriptions of one regularity, or classifications that cut across IER structure.

If the IER account simply introduces one structural feature for each listed cetasika, it reproduces the taxonomy rather than compressing or explaining it.

## Layer 4: Conditional Relations

Abhidharma analysis is synthetic as well as decompositional. The Theravāda *Paṭṭhāna* system describes twenty-four kinds of conditional relation, including root, object, proximity, contiguity, conascence, mutuality, support, repetition, association, dissociation, presence, absence, and others. In the manual presentation, each relation distinguishes:

1. conditioning states;
2. conditionally arisen states; and
3. the particular way in which the former condition the latter.[^conditions]

These relations do more than assert generic causal dependence. They help locate and individuate dhammas within a structured network.

IER describes lawful continuation using physically admissible configurations and transitions, intrinsic constraint, frontier geometry, collapse, successor realization, welding, propagation, sedimentation, and regime organization. None of these terms is a universal translation for *paccaya*.

Relation-by-relation reconstruction is therefore required.

| Theravāda relation or family | Superficial IER analogy | Identification still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conascence and mutuality | Simultaneous participation or integration | What physical processes co-occur, and is their dependence symmetric? |
| Association | Membership in one globally organized occurrence | Does common object and basis correspond to one UEF organization or to a narrower cognitive relation? |
| Proximity and contiguity | Successor realization | Does the traditional fixed order track physical reachability, explanatory segmentation, or a scholastic process model? |
| Repetition | Attractor stabilization, inertia, or sedimentation | Which mechanism is proposed, and what distinguishes repetition from mere recurrence? |
| Root and decisive support | Persistent bias or viability structure | Are the traditional conditioning roles reducible to frontier geometry, or do they classify a different relation? |
| Absence and disappearance | Availability created by prior cessation | What physical dependency makes non-presence condition the successor? |

The middle column is not a translation key. It records questions that might motivate models. A model must define its physical relata and derive the relevant asymmetry, co-occurrence, or order. Calling all conditioning "constraint" would discard the very distinctions that make the Abhidharma analysis informative.

## Layer 5: Consciousness Processes

Later Theravāda commentaries and manuals organize perception into consciousness processes (*citta-vīthi*) in which successive cittas perform different functions. Five-sense-door and mind-door processes involve specified patterns of adverting, perceiving or receiving, investigating, impulsion, retaining, and return to the inactive mode, with variations according to object and circumstance.[^process]

This is not simply a list of states. It is a functional and temporal architecture built from citta types, association rules, and conditional relations.

An IER trajectory is a lawful sequence of physical configurations. A trajectory can be described through changes in structural signatures, regimes, transitions, and articulated events. But the existence of a sequence in both frameworks does not establish that a consciousness process is an IER trajectory of the same grain.

A reconstruction would need to ask:

- What physical organization realizes each proposed function?
- Are the functions sequential, overlapping, or artifacts of analytical resolution?
- What fixes their order?
- Do traditional process boundaries correspond to physical transitions, event articulations, or explanatory projections?
- Can the model predict omissions, altered sequences, or intervention effects?
- How does the reconstruction compare with contemporary perceptual and neurodynamic evidence?

IER should not inherit the process theory merely because it is detailed. Nor should contemporary neuroscience be used to erase the historical theory before it has been stated accurately. The process model is a source of precise hypotheses and possible failures of correspondence.

## Layer 6: Soteriological Classification

Abhidharma taxonomies are organized partly by soteriological purpose. Theravāda matrices distinguish wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate dhammas; classify defilements and factors of awakening; organize meditative attainments; and culminate in an account of liberation. Analysis is intended not only to describe experience but to participate in abandoning some organizations and developing others.[^soteriology]

IER ontology is non-normative. Structural signatures describe geometry; they do not rank it. Stability, breadth, flexibility, persistence, low switching cost, or recoverability are not synonyms for wholesomeness or liberation.

The following equations are therefore blocked:

```text
wholesome = wide future cone
unwholesome = narrow attractor
equanimity = metastability
nonattachment = flexibility
suffering = threatened continuation
nibbāna = UEF regime
```

IER may eventually describe physical changes associated with contemplative training or the cessation of a particular pattern. That would reconstruct a transformation. It would not by itself recover the traditional evaluation, path structure, or claim about liberation.

This layer may therefore exhibit structural correlation without explanatory or normative equivalence.

## Cross-Layer Constraints

Several rules govern the reconstruction at every layer.

### Preserve school and stratum

"Abhidharma" is not one system. A useful target names a school, text, and historical level. Claims from the *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha* tradition should not be attributed without qualification to canonical Theravāda Abhidhamma, Sarvāstivāda, Sautrāntika, early Buddhism, or Buddhism generally.

### Preserve the type-token distinction

A model of what makes occurrences similar is not yet a model of any particular occurrence. A model of token realization is not yet an explanation of the traditional type boundary.

### Preserve identity rather than generation

Under IER, physical organization does not generate a separate phenomenal product. A successful reconstruction would identify an experiential organization with a physical organization described in another coordinate system.

### Preserve UEF unity

Local processes, factors, events, and projections do not automatically constitute subjects. No mapping may multiply UEFs merely because a traditional analysis distinguishes several simultaneous or successive items.

### Preserve temporal grain

"Moment," "event," "transition," "collapse," "process," "regime," and "subject continuity" must not be used interchangeably. IER collapse is an atomic change in reachability, not a Buddhist consciousness moment.

### Preserve normativity as a separate question

A physical reconstruction may explain organization without vindicating its traditional ethical or soteriological classification.

### Permit failure

The comparison must allow one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-one, cross-cutting, and null correspondences. If every traditional category is guaranteed an IER signature by construction, the procedure cannot discover anything.

## A Worked Research Target: Association Rules

The association rules for Theravāda mental factors provide a better initial target than a broad English category such as *attachment*.

The target can be stated narrowly:

> In the mature Theravāda manual tradition, can an independently specified IER organization predict any allowed or forbidden citta-cetasika associations?

A reconstruction record would contain:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Source target | Exact passage, text, school, and historical stratum |
| Analytical object | Dhamma type, token, assemblage, relation, process, or evaluation |
| Traditional identity conditions | Characteristic, function, manifestation, conditions, associations, and exclusions |
| IER target | Physical boundary, regime, participation structure, signature, relation, or trajectory specified independently |
| Proposed cardinality | One-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-one, cross-cutting, or none |
| Discriminating consequence | A predicted association, exclusion, transition, variation, or intervention effect |
| Rival accounts | Translation, doctrinal inheritance, generic dynamics, cognitive science, or taxonomic error |
| Result status | Supported, underdetermined, contradicted, or non-corresponding |

Three kinds of result would all be informative:

1. *Compression:* one IER organization predicts several traditional factors or rules, showing that the taxonomy separates roles within one structure.
2. *Differentiation:* one traditional factor divides into multiple IER organizations, showing that the taxonomy groups functionally similar but physically distinct cases.
3. *Non-correspondence:* the association pattern fails to track stable IER organization, suggesting that it is doctrinal, evaluative, translation-dependent, or otherwise organized at a different level.

The purpose is not to force convergence. It is to determine which traditional regularities survive reconstruction and what kind of regularity each one is.

## The Bounded Return of the Atlas Metaphor

The atlas metaphor can be retained only as a declared projection.

From one bounded viewpoint, Abhidharma materials do record recurring distinctions, combinations, conditions, and transformations within sentient experience. In that limited sense, they can be used as an atlas of questions.

But the metaphor must not imply that:

- Abhidharma is merely phenomenology;
- its categories are fuzzy regions rather than exact school-specific classifications;
- its texts are raw reports from independent observers;
- one map is shared by all Abhidharma traditions;
- every traditional item has one physical counterpart; or
- IER already knows the geometry that produced the map.

The collection is better understood as layered cartography whose legends, scales, purposes, and ontological commitments vary. IER does not stand outside it with a completed master terrain. It proposes a different physical identity framework under which selected lines on selected maps may become testable reconstruction problems.

## What an IER Explanation Would Have to Show

The generic sequence

```text
intrinsic constraint
-> recurring organization
-> structural signature
-> experiential category
```

is consistent with IER but insufficient as an explanation of Abhidharma.

A substantive explanation would need to show why a specified physical organization yields at least one of the following:

- a particular category boundary;
- a permitted or forbidden factor association;
- a functional distinction within an assemblage;
- a specific conditional asymmetry;
- a transition order in a consciousness process;
- a systematic change under intervention or training; or
- a principled failure of traditional distinctions to align with IER structure.

It must also explain why nearby alternative organizations do not yield the same result and why ordinary dynamical or cognitive language is not sufficient.

Until that burden is met, the appropriate language is:

> proposed reconstruction, structural analogy, or identification problem

rather than:

> IER explanation of the Abhidharma category.

## Explicit Non-Claims

This document does not claim:

- that Abhidharma is one phenomenological taxonomy;
- that dhammas are IER structural signatures;
- that dhamma tokens are IER collapses or events;
- that citta is a UEF;
- that cetasikas are physical components, control variables, or component subjects;
- that conditional relations are all instances of intrinsic constraint;
- that a consciousness process is an IER trajectory at the same temporal grain;
- that momentariness explains UEF continuity;
- that no-self denies every form of experiential subjecthood;
- that meditation provides theory-free observation;
- that wholesome organization is geometrically wider, more stable, or more flexible;
- that nibbāna is a physical regime identified by IER; or
- that a failed correspondence falsifies either IER or an Abhidharma tradition by itself.

## Conclusion

Abhidharma does not present IER with one taxonomy awaiting physical translation. It presents several historically differentiated analytical systems that distinguish types, tokens, assemblages, relations, processes, and soteriological classifications.

IER offers a physical identity claim and a disciplined vocabulary for regimes, participation, admissible continuation, structural signatures, trajectories, and projections. Those resources make reconstruction possible, but they do not make correspondence automatic.

The central task is therefore layered identification:

1. determine what the traditional item is;
2. preserve the structure and purpose by which it is individuated;
3. specify the proposed IER organization independently;
4. derive a consequence that could fail; and
5. classify the result as correspondence, compression, differentiation, or non-correspondence.

The value of Abhidharma for IER lies neither in ancient confirmation nor in a ready-made phenomenological atlas. It lies in the density and precision of the identification problems it makes available.
