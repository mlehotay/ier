# GoL follow-up readings: maintenance, boundaries, and continuation

Date: 2026-11-14\
Status: focused follow-up queue; new works have not received full source reviews\
Authority: non-canonical research\
Basis: the [nineteen-paper reading list](reading-list.md) and its source notes

The first pass sharpened the questions more than it resolved physical
individuation. Further reading is appropriate where it changes a proposed
relation or a discriminating test. It should proceed alongside small
reproductions, rather than postponing all formal work until a larger
bibliography has been exhausted.

The detailed synthesis is in the development repository at
`_work/unity/review/gol-literature-assessment.md`; the nineteen notes and their
page coverage are indexed at `references/references.md`. These paths identify
private development records and are not assumed to exist in the public export.

## Questions the readings leave open

| Question | What changed after reading | Useful next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What makes a participant constitutive? | Mutual influence, necessity for survival, and co-maintenance are different relations; a supplier can be indispensable without belonging to its consumer. | Compare a maintained part, an external supplier, and a perturbing neighbor under one declared policy. F1 and existing Deacon notes. |
| What exactly is maintained? | Beer’s process/component recurrence and biological closure of constraints use different relata and time scales. | Specify the conserved aspect, constrained process, and its maintenance mechanism before translating between frameworks. F1–F2. |
| How do absences participate? | Required OFF states can enable maintenance, while other OFF sites are irrelevant; redundant enabling conditions complicate attribution. | Separate full preconditions, sufficient sets, intervention effects, and membership. F3. |
| Which boundary moves with an organization? | A moving mask can change the described environment without a corresponding absolute-cell change; future boundary analysis needs more context than a selected one-ring intervention. | Preserve coordinates, masks, causal context, and observation horizon. Existing Beer papers, then F4. |
| What continues through transformation? | Continuity can connect both developmental stages and parent/offspring; split and merge cases need additional decisions. | Revisit the identity chapter and test lineage separately from token assignment. No new acquisition required initially. |
| Can statistics select the individual? | Scores depend on distributions and grain; some reward enlarging the candidate, and predictive independence can occur without memory or self-maintenance. | Test alternative ensembles and enlarged scopes; keep detection separate from membership. Existing entries 9 and 16–18. |
| Can a local synergy map be justified? | The 2013 paper leaves localization unresolved for its examined measures; that is a historical limitation, not proof that later approaches cannot help. | Compare later decompositions on shared controls before choosing one. F5–F6. |
| What transfers to physical realization? | A GoL production relation, physical self-maintenance, thermodynamic cost, and IER qualification require distinct arguments. | Analyze the host and modeled organization separately, then state their relation. Existing Pattee; F2 and F7. |
| Does history matter when present boards agree? | Equal complete states have equal subsequent Life evolution under equal rules and conditions, while different production histories may still be relevant to a proposed identity account. | State how past production remains physically effective or why provenance is relevant; do not infer future behavioral differences from the history label alone. Existing entries 5 and 15. |
| Does a statistical boundary ground an agent? | Conditional independence in a model does not by itself choose an organismic boundary; simultaneous and history-conditioned statements differ. | Read opposing commentaries and formal alternatives if a blanket criterion is proposed. F4 and conditional F8. |

These are questions for this IER programme, not claims that the entire research
field lacks answers. Several are older obligations clarified by the sources;
the moving-context, estimator, and size-growth issues make them more precise.

## Reuse material already available

Start with these before acquiring more papers:

- **Di Paolo (2005), Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency.** Local PDF
  and note: `references/refs/di-paolo-2005-autopoiesis-adaptivity-teleology-agency.pdf`
  and the matching Markdown file in `references/notes/`. Revisit pp. 435–439
  and 444–445 for robustness versus regulation, viability tendencies, and
  temporal grain. Ask whether the proposed relation concerns persistence,
  adaptivity, or both. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y).
- **Deacon, Srivastava, and Bacigalupi (2014), The Transition from Constraint
  to Regulation at the Origin of Life.** Already indexed locally. Revisit
  pp. 945–951 for processes that produce one another’s enabling conditions
  and the distinction between containment and regulation.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2741/4259).
- **Pattee (2001), The Physics of Symbols: Bridging the Epistemic Cut.**
  Already indexed locally. Revisit pp. 7–9 and 15–18 for the relationship
  between abstract computation, material constraints, and physical control.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(01)00104-6).
- **Unfinished parts of the original nineteen.** Transcribe Beer’s relevant
  figures; examine the exact definitions/proofs used by any proposed metric;
  obtain Marshall et al.’s separate supporting texts if implementing their
  method. The original notes identify what was and was not read.
- **The Markov-blanket debate already in the acquired PDF.** The first pass
  included Friston (pp. 36–37), Parr (46–48), Virgo–Rosas–Biehl (63–64), and
  the authors’ response (68–75). Compare those positions before extending a
  boundary argument. Other commentaries remain available for targeted reading.

Older Deacon, Di Paolo, and Pattee notes contain historical IER interpretations
that require reassessment. Their non-factorization language is not a finding
established by those authors or an instruction to reinstate a withdrawn test.

## New follow-up queue

F1–F8 are local queue identifiers, separate from the original entries 1–19.
Links and introductory material were checked on 2026-11-14. The files have
not been downloaded into the shared collection during this pass. An accessible
source or abstract is not a completed reading or endorsement.

### F1. Function and constitutive contribution — read first

Mossio, M., Saborido, C., and Moreno, A. (2009). **An Organizational Account
of Biological Functions.** *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*,
60(4), 813–841. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axp036).
[Research-group-hosted PDF](https://www.ias-research.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Mossio_25592036.pdf).

The paper develops functions through contribution to closed, differentiated
self-maintenance. Read for the conditions connecting a part’s contribution
with the organization’s maintenance of the part. Our question is whether
those conditions help separate constituent and supplier without assuming the
whole in advance. Deliver a proposed relation and at least one countercase;
do not equate biological function with experiential membership.

### F2. Physical autonomy beyond formal recurrence — read first

Ruiz-Mirazo, K., and Moreno, Á. (2004). **Basic Autonomy as a Fundamental
Step in the Synthesis of Life.** *Artificial Life*, 10(3), 235–259.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/1064546041255584).
[University-hosted PDF](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~inmanh/adsys10/Readings/Ruiz-Mirazo_and_Moreno_-_Basic_Autonomy_as_a_Fundamental_Step_-_AL_2004.pdf).

The paper examines the physical requirements of basic autonomy in artificial
life. Read for material and energetic conditions left abstract by Life’s
update rule. Produce a comparison between an undriven Life organization,
externally maintained support, and a proposed physical realization. Keep
basic autonomy, life, and experience as separate targets.

### F3. Competing actual-cause definitions — before adopting causal labels

Halpern, J. Y. (2015). **A Modification of the Halpern–Pearl Definition of
Causality.** *IJCAI-15*, 3022–3032.
[Proceedings record](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2015/427).
[Author PDF](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/info/people/halpern/papers/modified-HPdef.pdf)
and [extended preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00162).

This offers a competing structural-model account to compare with original
entry 11. Ask which contingencies are permitted and how overdetermination,
absences, and joint conditions are treated. Apply both accounts to the same
small Boolean/Life-neighborhood examples. Record disagreements rather than
choosing the definition that best matches a preferred membership verdict.
The extended preprint and proceedings have different pagination.

### F4. Boundaries that retain interaction history — targeted comparison

Rosas, F. E., Mediano, P. A. M., Biehl, M., Chandaria, S., and Polani, D.
(2020). **Causal Blankets: Theory and Algorithmic Framework.**
[Chapter DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-64919-7_19), pp. 187–198.
[Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12568).

The proposed perception/action construction uses dynamical sufficient
statistics without requiring steady-state or Markovian dynamics. Ask what
history-dependent separation contributes beyond a preselected partition.
Its abstract’s broad existence claim makes the discrimination question
especially important: why would a blanket identify a maintained individual
rather than merely provide a description for an arbitrary split?

### F5. Pointwise information after the 2013 limitation — conditional method

Finn, C., and Lizier, J. T. (2018). **Pointwise Partial Information
Decomposition Using the Specificity and Ambiguity Lattices.** *Entropy*,
20(4), 297. [DOI and open article](https://doi.org/10.3390/e20040297).

This later proposal decomposes pointwise information through specificity and
ambiguity. Read its axioms, signed terms, examples, and limitations before
claiming it resolves our desired localization problem. Test copy, independent,
XOR, two-bit-copy, and mixed examples. Only then assess whether a collision
map answers a defined question; it would remain a statistical diagnostic.

### F6. Operationally motivated unique information — conditional method

Bertschinger, N., Rauh, J., Olbrich, E., Jost, J., and Ay, N. (2014).
**Quantifying Unique Information.** *Entropy*, 16(4), 2161–2183.
[DOI and open article](https://doi.org/10.3390/e16042161).
[Open preprint, first submitted 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2852).

The authors motivate a decomposition through unique information and pairwise
marginals. Compare it with I_min and F5 using the same distributions. Ask
which assumptions change the diagnosis and whether an averaged measure
meets the intended use. This is a comparison option, not a claim that all
PID definitions agree or that every source count has an easy extension.

### F7. Thermodynamic requirements — for the physical bridge

England, J. L. (2013). **Statistical Physics of Self-Replication.**
*The Journal of Chemical Physics*, 139, 121923.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4818538).
[Open preprint, first submitted 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1179).

The paper derives a heat-production bound for replication coupled to a
thermal bath. Read to discipline physical claims about reproduction and
durability. It concerns replication, so an argument is needed before
applying it to nonreplicating maintenance. A glider count, Shannon entropy,
and host electricity use are different quantities; none is automatically
IER informational tension.

### F8. Test the assumptions of blanket-based physics — only if used

Aguilera, M., Millidge, B., Tschantz, A., and Buckley, C. L. (2022).
**How Particular Is the Physics of the Free Energy Principle?**
*Physics of Life Reviews*, 40, 24–50.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2021.11.001).
[Open preprint, first submitted 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11203).

This technical critique examines particular stochastic-system assumptions.
Read it if the proposal relies on FEP or steady-state blankets; it is not a
prerequisite for a non-blanket GoL account. Pair it with the already acquired
Friston and Parr commentaries and
[Friston’s direct reply](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10149691/1/Friston_PLR%20comment.pdf).
Identify the disputed equation and formulation before generalizing a criticism.
This entry does not declare the wider FEP debate settled.

## Reading and stopping rule

Begin with the existing Di Paolo/Deacon/Pattee material and F1–F2 for the
physical-maintenance question. Use F3 when specifying causal contribution,
F4 for history-dependent boundary comparisons, F5–F6 only if selecting an
information measure, F7 for a thermodynamic claim, and F8 only for an FEP
claim. The list is intentionally selective rather than an exhaustive or
latest-literature survey.

Each additional reading should produce a located claim, its assumptions, a
proposed use or rejection, and a fixture or argument it changes. If it changes
neither a definition nor a discriminating case, defer it. None of these
readings can replace the remaining IER-specific argument connecting general
organization to full experiential qualification and continuity.
