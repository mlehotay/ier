# Game of Life reading list for IER

Status: exploratory reading guide\
Authority: non-canonical\
Additional references checked: 2026-11-14

Nineteen readings on glider boundaries, process membership, joint contribution,
and continuation. Entries 1–5 develop Beer’s Life models. Entries 6–9 cover
mathematical, philosophical, and methodological comparisons. Entries 10–19
extend the list to organizational maintenance, causal contribution, structure
discovery, identity, autonomy, scale, and boundary critiques.

The IER reading prompts below are proposed applications of these sources.
Their methods do not independently establish IER membership, intrinsic
informational tension, experiential unity, or the full `Adj_dia` relation.
Journal/chapter DOIs identify published works; entry 12 is an arXiv preprint
with an arXiv DOI. Open versions are linked where available.

## Glider organization and transition histories

1. **Beer, R. D. (2004). Autopoiesis and cognition in the Game of Life.** *Artificial Life*, 10(3), 309–326. [10.1162/1064546041255539](https://doi.org/10.1162/1064546041255539)\
   Start with Figures 1–3. Beer distinguishes the five live cells, the surrounding OFF boundary, and the interface with the environment. In the blinker collision in Figure 3B, the boundary ruptures while the live shape is still recognizable.

2. **Beer, R. D. (2015). Characterizing autopoiesis in the Game of Life.** *Artificial Life*, 21(1), 1–19. [10.1162/artl_a_00143](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00143)\
   Read for a formal account of recurrent patterns as networks of interdependent processes that construct and maintain their boundaries. Use the network to nominate candidate parts and ask what further evidence would establish IER constitutive membership.

3. **Beer, R. D. (2014). The cognitive domain of a glider in the Game of Life.** *Artificial Life*, 20(2), 183–206. [10.1162/artl_a_00125](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00125)\
   Read §4 for the enumeration of all 2²⁴ configurations of the immediate environment for each of two canonical glider forms. The resulting transition classes make the comparison between survival and destruction explicit.

4. **Beer, R. D. (2020). Bittorio revisited: Structural coupling in the Game of Life.** *Adaptive Behavior*, 28(4). [10.1177/1059712319859907](https://doi.org/10.1177/1059712319859907)\
   Read for interaction graphs that capture both directions of glider–environment coupling and embed possible glider histories in the full grid’s transition graph for small Life universes.

5. **Beer, R. D. (2020). An investigation into the origin of autopoiesis.** *Artificial Life*, 26(1), 5–22. [10.1162/artl_a_00307](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00307)\
   Read for separate treatments of glider creation, persistence, and destruction. This gives the IER comparison a concrete starting point for distinguishing the appearance of a glider from the continuation of an existing one.

## Motion, collisions, and part–whole relations

6. **Eppstein, D. (2002). Searching for spaceships.** In R. Nowakowski (Ed.), *More Games of No Chance* (pp. 433–454). [10.1017/9781316135167.031](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316135167.031)\
   Uses graph search to find patterns that recur with spatial displacement in Conway Life and related automata. The traveling-pattern criterion gives a starting point for identifying the same glider type in different locations. A separate transition history is needed to trace a particular instance.

7. **Dennett, D. C. (1991). Real patterns.** *The Journal of Philosophy*, 88(1), 27–51. [10.2307/2027085](https://doi.org/10.2307/2027085)\
   Uses a Life glider to ask what makes motion real when the underlying sites stay fixed. Read alongside Eppstein for the relation between the moving pattern and the updates that realize it.

8. **Brajon, J., & Makarenko, A. (2019). Game of life with non-regular space with boundaries: Glider case.** *System Research & Information Technologies*, 2019(1). [10.20535/SRIT.2308-8893.2019.1.03](https://doi.org/10.20535/SRIT.2308-8893.2019.1.03)\
   Examines glider collisions and introduces internal boundaries into a modified Life lattice. The lattice boundary provides a comparison with the moving organizational boundary in Beer’s model.

9. **Biehl, M., Ikegami, T., & Polani, D. (2017). Specific and complete local integration of patterns in Bayesian networks.** *Entropy*, 19(5), 230. [10.3390/e19050230](https://doi.org/10.3390/e19050230)\
   Develops a partition-based integration test for spatiotemporal patterns in Bayesian networks. This is a methodological comparison for selecting candidate wholes over time. Applying it to a Life glider would require a specified probabilistic model, and IER ownership would still require a test of where the relevant restriction is borne and resolved.

## Organizational maintenance and causal contribution

10. **Montévil, M., & Mossio, M. (2015). Biological organisation as closure of constraints.** *Journal of Theoretical Biology*, 372, 179–191. [10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029). [Author's full text](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2015-mm-organisation-closure-constraints/).
    Distinguishes processes from constraints conserved at relevant timescales,
    then examines mutual maintenance among constraints and boundaries between
    interacting systems. For IER, ask what a glider regenerates through its
    cycle, what the fixed lattice and rule supply, and whether changing
    continuation conditions amounts to maintaining constitutive organization.
    A mapping from biological constraints to Life needs explicit justification.
    Search terms: `closure of constraints`, `constitutive autonomy`,
    `organizational maintenance`.

11. **Albantakis, L., Marshall, W., Hoel, E., & Tononi, G. (2019). What caused what? A quantitative account of actual causation using dynamical causal networks.** *Entropy*, 21(5), 459. [10.3390/e21050459](https://doi.org/10.3390/e21050459). [Open full text](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/21/5/459).
    Develops an intervention-based account of particular causal occurrences.
    Use it as a comparison for tracing which earlier occurrences produced a
    later participating role. In Life, test how a representation handles three
    neighbors jointly producing a birth and an extra neighbor preventing it.
    Positive pairwise ancestry alone can omit relevant absences and joint
    conditions. Causal contribution remains distinct from constitutive
    membership; the paper's causal criteria are not adopted as IER criteria.
    Search terms: `actual causation`, `joint causes`, `causal contribution absence`.

## Joint information and discovery of candidate organizations

12. **Williams, P. L., & Beer, R. D. (2010). Nonnegative decomposition of multivariate information.** *arXiv preprint*, arXiv:1004.2515. [10.48550/arXiv.1004.2515](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1004.2515). [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2515).
    Introduces partial information decomposition to distinguish redundant,
    unique, and synergistic information supplied by multiple sources about a
    target. For IER, investigate whether a proposed joint contribution can be
    distinguished from overlapping individual contributions. Specify the
    variables, ensemble, target, and redundancy measure before calculating;
    synergy is not an experiential-unity criterion. Search terms:
    `partial information decomposition`, `redundancy`, `synergistic information`.

13. **Lizier, J. T., Flecker, B., & Williams, P. L. (2013). Towards a synergy-based approach to measuring information modification.** In *2013 IEEE Symposium on Artificial Life (ALIFE)* (pp. 43–51). [10.1109/ALIFE.2013.6602430](https://doi.org/10.1109/ALIFE.2013.6602430). [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3440).
    Proposes using partial information decomposition to analyze information
    modification, motivated by interactions such as cellular-automaton particle
    collisions. Read after entry 12 for possible measurements of interaction
    among contributions. A declared perturbation ensemble differs from the
    singleton outgoing successor set of one fixed deterministic board; do not
    substitute one for the other. Search terms: `information modification`,
    `cellular automata collisions synergy`.

14. **Shalizi, C. R., Haslinger, R., Rouquier, J.-B., Klinkner, K. L., & Moore, C. (2006). Automatic filters for the detection of coherent structure in spatiotemporal systems.** *Physical Review E*, 73, 036104. [10.1103/PhysRevE.73.036104](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.036104). [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0508001).
    Uses local sensitivity and local statistical complexity to detect structure
    without prescribing the target shape. Its examples concern elementary and
    cyclic cellular automata; application to Life requires further work. For
    IER, ask whether candidate organizations can be discovered before labeling
    them gliders or toads, reducing dependence on a preselected recognizable
    object. Predictive or dynamical structure nominates candidates rather than
    deciding membership. Search terms: `local causal states`,
    `computational mechanics`, `coherent structure detection`.

## Continuity, autonomy, and spatiotemporal grain

15. **DiFrisco, J., & Mossio, M. (2020). Diachronic identity in complex life cycles: An organizational perspective.** In A. S. Meincke & J. Dupré (Eds.), *Biological Identity: Perspectives from Metaphysics and the Philosophy of Biology*. Routledge. [10.4324/9781351066389-10](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781351066389-10). [Author manuscript, deposited 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Matteo-Mossio/publication/334574029_Diachronic_Identity_in_Complex_Life_Cycles_An_Organizational_Perspective/links/5d31dbd9a6fdcc370a4e250a/Diachronic-Identity-in-Complex-Life-Cycles-An-Organizational-Perspective.pdf).
    Examines organizational continuity across material and functional changes,
    while distinguishing continuity from a sufficient identity condition and
    considering fission and fusion. Use it to extend Life comparisons beyond
    exact recurrence: a collision may transform a continuing organization or
    terminate it and produce another. Compare recruitment, release, splitting,
    merging, and independent checkpoint installation. The biological account
    does not establish IER's full `Adj_dia` relation. Search terms:
    `organizational continuity`, `process identity`, `fission fusion identity`.

16. **Krakauer, D., Bertschinger, N., Olbrich, E., Flack, J. C., & Ay, N. (2020). The information theory of individuality.** *Theory in Biosciences*, 139, 209–223. [10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7). [Open full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7).
    Formalizes temporal information propagation and distinguishes organismal,
    colonial, and driven forms of individuality with different environmental
    dependence. For IER, compare an isolated pattern, interacting patterns,
    and a pattern sustained by supplied conditions. Evaluate how candidate
    boundaries and declared distributions affect the result. Information
    propagation alone does not establish owned resolution or token identity.
    Search terms: `information theory individuality`, `temporal integrity`,
    `environmental dependence`.

17. **Barnett, L., & Seth, A. K. (2023). Dynamical independence: Discovering emergent macroscopic processes in complex dynamical systems.** *Physical Review E*, 108, 014304. [10.1103/PhysRevE.108.014304](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.108.014304). [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06511).
    Develops a Shannon-information-based approach to discovering macroscopic
    processes whose histories suffice for their prediction relative to the
    underlying microscopic process, with an explicit linear operationalization.
    For Life, compare site-level updates with phase-cycle descriptions and ask
    what environmental conditions the macro description needs. This is a
    research comparison, not an existing Life tracker or a test of intrinsic
    closure. Search terms: `dynamical independence`, `macroscopic processes`,
    `multiscale emergence`.

18. **Marshall, W., Albantakis, L., & Tononi, G. (2018). Black-boxing and cause-effect power.** *PLOS Computational Biology*, 14(4), e1006114. [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006114](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006114). [Open full text](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006114).
    Examines causal descriptions at different spatial and temporal grains
    using input-output macro elements in an integrated-information framework.
    Compare the consequences of observing Life by single ticks or complete
    phase cycles, and inspect what each grouping hides. Its integration,
    exclusion, and consciousness commitments require separate assessment;
    neither its score nor a convenient four-step glider description supplies
    an IER scope criterion. Search terms: `causal black-boxing`,
    `spatiotemporal grain`, `causal emergence`.

## Critical checks on proposed boundaries

19. **Bruineberg, J., Dołęga, K., Dewhurst, J., & Baltieri, M. (2022). The emperor's new Markov blankets.** *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 45, e183. [10.1017/S0140525X21002351](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X21002351). [Author-hosted full text with commentaries](https://manuelbaltieri.com/assets/pdf/EmperorMarkovBlanketsPrePublished.pdf).
    Distinguishes a statistical modeling boundary from stronger claims about
    physical agent-environment boundaries and challenges unsupported transfers
    between them. Use as adversarial reading for any proposed Life boundary
    algorithm: what evidence makes a partition constitutive rather than merely
    useful for prediction? Read relevant commentaries alongside the target
    article to examine competing interpretations. Search terms:
    `Markov blanket physical boundary critique`, `statistical individuation`.

## Where to start for IER

Pair Eppstein’s traveling-pattern criterion with Beer’s blinker collision in Figure 3B of paper 1. Eppstein identifies the pattern type. Beer gives a timed perturbation where the boundary changes before the visible shape disappears. Use paper 2 to identify candidate participating processes, then paper 4 to examine how the environment constrains their successors. The remaining IER question is what evidence supports constitutive membership and each clause of `Adj_dia`.

For the extended list, prioritize **10, 11, and 15**: maintained organization,
particular causal contributions, and continuation through transformation.
Then read 12–14 for candidate measurements and structure discovery, 16–18 for
individuation and scale comparisons, and 19 to challenge boundary inferences.

Assess proposed relations against a shared comparison set:

| Comparison | Question for the proposed relation |
| --- | --- |
| Isolated glider and stationary toad | Does it accommodate both migrating and stationary turnover? |
| Two sufficiently separated gliders over a declared horizon | Does it preserve their independence despite a shared grid and clock? |
| Two interacting patterns | Does it distinguish interaction from constitutive membership? |
| Gun and emitted glider | Can it distinguish production from continuing membership? |
| A collision that changes pattern type | Can it assess transformed continuation without assuming identity from shape? |
| An independently installed checkpoint matching a produced board | Does it preserve the difference in production history despite identical later boards? |

For each method, record candidate scope, variables, grain, interval,
interventions, distribution where needed, and the specific relation tested.
These comparisons inform the physical individuation work; they do not by
themselves establish experiential qualification.
