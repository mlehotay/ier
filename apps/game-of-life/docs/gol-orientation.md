# GoL Orientation - Relation to IER

## Status

NON-CANONICAL | EXAMPLE-SCOPED | NOT READER-FACING


## Purpose

This project uses Conway - s Game of Life (GoL) as a controlled example system to explore structural concepts from Informational Experiential Realism (IER).

It is not:

* a model of consciousness
* an implementation of a Unified Experiential Field (UEF)
* evidence for or against IER


## What GoL Provides

GoL is:

* deterministic
* discrete
* fully specified by a local update rule

This makes it useful for isolating structural questions such as:

* how dependence propagates
* how regions form under constraint
* how projected structure behaves under restriction


## Key Limitation

In standard GoL:

[
\(A(s) = \{ \hat{s} \}\)
]

There is exactly one admissible successor.

Therefore:

* admissible futures are singleton
* admissibility factorizes trivially
* intrinsic closure (in the IER sense) is not present


## Why It Is Still Useful

Despite this limitation, GoL can exhibit:

* predicate non-decomposability
* dependence structures spanning regions
* derived admissibility-like constructions

These allow us to demonstrate:

> many forms of - holistic - or non-compositional structure do not imply non-factorizable admissible futures


## IER Interpretation

Within IER terms:

* GoL fully instantiates:

  * configuration
  * flow
  * constraint

* GoL does not instantiate:

  * coherence (intrinsic closure)


## Role in the IER Project

The GoL examples serve as:

> negative controls and structural counterexamples

They help show:

* what coherence is not
* how non-decomposability can arise without intrinsic closure
* why admissible-future structure - not behavior - is the correct level of analysis


## One-Line Summary

> Game of Life is used to rule out incorrect intuitions about structure, not to model or demonstrate consciousness.
