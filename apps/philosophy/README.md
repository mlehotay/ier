## Current Research Interface

The current project investigates the experiential subject, process identity, and the relationship between subjects of experience and physical systems. It separates four questions that are often run together:

- what a conscious state is;
- what the subject of experience is;
- what makes experience synchronically unified; and
- what makes one subject persist through time.

The completed literature search reached a bounded **Outcome A - explicit precedent** finding. Galen Strawson's 2003 account of the thin subject identifies an experience, its subject, its content, and a short-lived physical synergy of process-stuff as numerically identical. The bare claim that an experiential subject is a physical process therefore already has a clear precedent.

The open contribution is narrower: a continuing process-subject whose numerical identity can survive changes of content, organization, and material participants while ending at genuine cessation. The next recommended scholarly product is a constructive paper developing those identity and persistence conditions.

Principal points of contact include:

- [Galen Strawson, “What Is the Relation Between an Experience, the Subject of the Experience, and the Content of the Experience?”](https://doi.org/10.1111/1533-6077.00015)
- [Matthew Owen, “The Neglected Conscious Subject in Consciousness Science”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2024.06.009)
- [Matthew Owen, Darren Hight, and Anthony G. Hudetz, “Human Brain Organoids and the Mereological Fallacy”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12152-024-09577-y)
- [Henry Taylor, “The Relation Between Subjects and Their Conscious Experiences”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-019-01379-w)
- [Helen Steward, “Substances, Agents and Processes”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031819119000494)

The research materials are non-canonical. They identify a terminological inconsistency in IER’s claim that one UEF *constitutes* one experiential subject. The intended relation is numerical identity: the continuing UEF operation is the experiential subject, rather than something that constitutes, realizes, or causally sustains a separate subject. This relation should be stated consistently in a future canonical revision. No canonical revision is made here.
