## Argument cards

### A1. Galen Strawson (2003)

**Full citation and version:** Galen Strawson, “What Is the Relation Between an
Experience, the Subject of the Experience, and the Content of the Experience?”
*Philosophical Issues* 13 (1), 279–315. The accessible HTML is an unfinished
author draft closely matching the published article; the bibliographic record
and DOI refer to the version of record.  
**Stable identifier:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1533-6077.00015);
[accessible text](https://consc.net/event/neh/papers/strawson1.htm).  
**Access level:** full text plus published metadata.  
**Inclusion level:** A — Exact.  
**Question addressed:** What is the relation among an experience, its subject,
and its content?  
**Candidate subject:** a “thin” subject that exists only during experience.  
**Candidate physical relatum:** a spatiotemporally bounded, synergistic portion
of physical process-stuff, illustrated as a short neural episode.  
**Relation asserted:** numerical identity.  
**Key passage location:** §§2–3 for thick/traditional/thin subjects and
process-stuff; §6 for the identity of experience, subject, content, and their
physical synergies.  
**Author's conclusion:** in the thin conception, the experience, subject, and
content are not three ontological items; under materialism, the same physical
synergy is all three.  
**Argument reconstructed:**

1. Every experience necessarily has experiential content and is for a subject.
2. A thin subject is occurrent subjectivity, not a bearer capable of existing
   without experience.
3. No independent identity-fixing basis is supplied for separating that thin
   subject from its particular experience/content.
4. Under materialism, the concrete occurrence is a bounded physical synergy.
5. Therefore the thin subject, experience, content, and physical synergy can be
   numerically identical.

**Evidence/examples:** a set of lights while it is flashing; a roughly
two-second human experience; dreamless sleep; counterfactual changes of content
and physical parts.  
**Strongest objection considered:** ordinary subjects appear able to survive
changes of content and unconsciousness. Strawson treats this as reliance on a
different, thicker subject concept.  
**What the source establishes:** a direct physical subject–process identity
precedent.  
**What it does not establish:** a subject persisting through changing contents,
participant turnover, or experiential gaps; a physically operational
criterion for locating the relevant synergy.  
**Relation to target:** exact ontology, narrower duration and stricter
mereological identity than the continuing-process target.  
**IER-facing relevance:** decisive precedent; cleanest comparison for IER's
purported subject–regime identity and cessation rule.  
**Confidence:** high.  
**Unresolved:** whether Strawson's later books materially revise the
persistence criterion.

### A2. Helen Steward (2020)

**Full citation:** Helen Steward, “Substances, Agents and Processes,”
*Philosophy* 95 (1), 41–61.  
**Stable identifier:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031819119000494).  
**Access level:** full author manuscript and publisher metadata.  
**Inclusion level:** A — explicit rejection/category challenge.  
**Question addressed:** Should organisms and agents be replaced in ontology by
processes?  
**Candidate subject:** an enduring animal/agent.  
**Candidate physical relatum:** the biological processes occurring in it,
considered collectively.  
**Relation asserted:** rejection of numerical identity; processes are
activities of the animal.  
**Key passage location:** final section, pp. 58–60, on consciousness, unity,
and agency.  
**Author's conclusion:** processual biology gives no sufficient reason to
eliminate enduring things, and consciousness/agency give positive reasons to
retain them.  
**Argument reconstructed:**

1. Consciousness is unified and requires a subject.
2. A mere plurality or set of processes does not obviously possess experience
   or supply the subject's unity.
3. Agents exercise powers to initiate and direct processes; those powers
   cannot simply be reassigned to the internal processes.
4. Therefore processes should not replace the animal/agent as subject.

**Evidence/examples:** metabolism and material turnover, life cycles,
ecological interdependence, unified agency.  
**Strongest objection considered:** processualists can redescribe enduring
organisms as self-maintaining processes. Steward replies that dynamic substances
already accommodate turnover and change.  
**Establishes:** the category move from “processes occur” to “the process is
the subject” requires argument.  
**Does not establish:** that no single dynamically unified process could be a
subject; the stated target is often “a set of processes.”  
**IER-facing relevance:** demands a positive unity/bearer argument rather than
mere process description.  
**Confidence:** high.

### B1. Henry Taylor (2020)

**Citation:** Henry Taylor, “The Relation Between Subjects and Their Conscious
Experiences,” *Philosophical Studies* 177, 3493–3507.  
**Stable identifier:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-019-01379-w).  
**Access level:** full open text.  
**Inclusion level:** B — Direct.  
**Question:** Why is a token experience necessarily had by its actual subject,
while that subject could have had a different experience?  
**Candidate subject:** a person/substance.  
**Physical relatum:** no reductive physical identification is required; an
experience is a property-exemplification event.  
**Relation:** the subject, phenomenal property, and time constitute the event.  
**Key passages:** §§4.1–4.3.  
**Conclusion:** the bearer is an identity-fixing constituent of the
experience, but not identical with or identity-dependent on it.  
**Argument reconstructed:**

1. An experience is a substance instantiating a phenomenal property at a time.
2. The constituting substance is the subject.
3. Event identity depends on the specific subject, property, and time.
4. Hence this experience could not have belonged to another subject.
5. The substance's identity does not depend on this event.
6. Hence the same subject could exist without this experience.

**Objection to Strawson:** thin-subject identity discards the second intimacy
datum without enough theoretical gain.  
**Establishes:** a precise nonidentity alternative and persistence objection.  
**Does not establish:** the ultimate physical nature of the subject.  
**IER relevance:** IER must explain why identity is superior to this
asymmetric constitution model.  
**Confidence:** high.

### B2. Barry Dainton (2012; 2016)

**Citations:** “Selfhood and the Flow of Experience,” *Grazer Philosophische
Studien* 84, 173–211; “I—The Sense of Self,” *Aristotelian Society
Supplementary Volume* 90 (1), 113–143.  
**Identifiers:** [2012 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789401207904_009);
[2016 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/arisup/akw007).  
**Access:** full 2012 text; 2016 abstract.  
**Level:** B — Direct/near.  
**Question:** Can a naturalistic subject persist rather than fragment into
momentary experiential selves?  
**Candidate subject:** a capacity for unified and continuous experience.  
**Physical relatum:** not fixed as a current physical process.  
**Relation:** the capacity-system produces experiences.  
**Passages:** 2012 abstract and opening sections; 2016 abstract.  
**Conclusion:** continuity of ordinary experience supports a persisting
subject-capacity; subjects need not be episode-identical.  
**Argument reconstructed:**

1. Ordinary experience exhibits diachronic experiential continuity.
2. Momentary subject/experience identities underweight that datum.
3. A persisting capacity for unified experience explains both continuity and
   survival of intervals without current experience.
4. Therefore a subject is better understood as that capacity-system.

**Strongest objection:** capacities may look like abstractions or require an
underlying bearer. Dainton argues the subject can consist of nothing more than
the capacities.  
**Establishes:** a non-substantial, naturalistic subject with persistence
resources.  
**Does not establish:** identity with the occurrent physical process.  
**IER relevance:** principal rival for sleep/anesthesia: capacity continuity
versus no-gap regime identity.  
**Confidence:** high for the core view.

### B3. Owen (2024) and Owen, Hight & Hudetz (2025)

**Citations:** Matthew Owen, “The Neglected Conscious Subject in Consciousness
Science,” *Physics of Life Reviews* 50, 61–62; Matthew Owen, Darren Hight, and
Anthony G. Hudetz, “Human Brain Organoids and the Mereological Fallacy,”
*Neuroethics* 18, article 8.  
**Identifiers:** [2024 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2024.06.009);
[2025 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12152-024-09577-y).  
**Access:** 2024 metadata/abstract only; 2025 full open text.  
**Level:** B.  
**Question:** Which entity, rather than which state or correlate, is conscious?  
**Candidate subject:** the whole human/organism under a hylomorphic account.  
**Physical relatum:** neural states and proper parts.  
**Relation:** the whole has the mental states; parts contribute/constitute but
are not thereby the subject.  
**Key passages:** 2025 §§2–4 and conclusion.  
**Argument reconstructed from the accessible 2025 source:**

1. Mental states are states of some bearer; a state is not itself a conscious
   subject merely by being conscious in the state sense.
2. Attributing creature consciousness to a proper part risks the mereological
   fallacy.
3. Classical identity theorists identify states with brain states, not
   automatically the bearer with the brain part.
4. A hylomorphic whole provides the bearer of the relevant powers/states.

**Establishes:** the neglected-subject problem and state/creature distinction.  
**Does not establish:** the full positive view of the inaccessible 2024
commentary; a refutation of every process-subject ontology.  
**IER relevance:** motivates an explicit answer but is better an application
than the organizing target of the constructive paper.  
**Confidence:** high for 2025 claims; medium for 2024.

### C1. Nicholson (2018)

**Citation:** Daniel J. Nicholson, “Reconceptualizing the Organism: From
Complex Machine to Flowing Stream,” in *Everything Flows*, 139–166.  
**Identifier:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0007).  
**Access:** full publisher text.  
**Level:** C — Enabling.  
**Question:** What is an organism and how does it persist through material
turnover?  
**Candidate:** organism as dynamically self-maintaining process.  
**Physical relatum:** temporally extended causal/organizational trajectory.  
**Relation:** numerical identity at the organism-process level.  
**Key passages:** sections on streams, trajectories, causal continuity, and
genidentity.  
**Conclusion:** living beings persist by continuous maintenance of form and
organization, not sameness of matter.  
**Argument:** metabolism and turnover are constitutive of living existence;
therefore a static machine model is inadequate; causal-organizational
continuity better captures persistence.  
**Does not establish:** organism = experiential subject, or process unity =
phenomenal unity.  
**IER relevance:** permits a process-subject to survive participant turnover.  
**Confidence:** high.

### C2. Seibt (2018)

**Citation:** Johanna Seibt, “Ontological Tools for the Process Turn in
Biology,” in *Everything Flows*, 113–136.  
**Identifier:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0006).  
**Access:** full publisher text.  
**Level:** C.  
**Question:** How can processes be categorized, individuated, composed, and
tracked?  
**Candidate:** general processes/dynamic individuals.  
**Physical relatum:** process profiles described across multiple dimensions.  
**Relation:** process identity/individuation framework.  
**Key passages:** discussion of spatiotemporal signature, participant
structure, dynamic composition, dynamic shape, and dynamic context.  
**Conclusion:** a monocategorial process ontology can distinguish familiar
individuals without reducing identity to ultimate material parts.  
**Establishes:** a vocabulary for non-arbitrary process profiles.  
**Does not establish:** which profile is conscious or why one profile is a
subject.  
**IER relevance:** best public framework for articulating UEF boundaries and
change tolerance.  
**Confidence:** high.

### C3. DiFrisco (2018) and Pradeu (2018)

**Citations:** James DiFrisco, “Biological Processes: Criteria of Identity and
Persistence,” 76–95; Thomas Pradeu, “Genidentity and Biological Processes,”
96–112; both in *Everything Flows*.  
**Identifiers:** [DiFrisco DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0004);
[Pradeu DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198779636.003.0005).  
**Access:** chapter records/abstract for DiFrisco; author manuscript/record for
Pradeu.  
**Level:** C.  
**Question:** Which conditions individuate and track biological processes?  
**Candidate:** biological process/individual.  
**Physical relatum:** causally and spatiotemporally continuous transformation.  
**Relation:** numerical identity framework.  
**Conclusion:** causal continuity, spatiotemporal continuity, and genidentity
are complementary resources for tracking processes through change.  
**Establishes:** persistence need not mean static qualitative or material
sameness.  
**Does not establish:** subjecthood or a no-gap criterion for consciousness.  
**IER relevance:** foundation for a formal account of regime continuity and
termination.  
**Confidence:** medium-high.

### B4. Bayne and Chalmers (2003)

**Citation:** Tim Bayne and David J. Chalmers, “What Is the Unity of
Consciousness?” in *The Unity of Consciousness*.  
**Stable URL:** [author text](https://consc.net/papers/unity.html).  
**Access:** full text.  
**Level:** B.  
**Question:** In what senses can conscious states be unified?  
**Candidate subject:** presupposed bearer of states.  
**Physical relatum:** none assigned.  
**Relation:** subject unity is same-subject possession; phenomenal unity is
subsumption in a total phenomenal state.  
**Conclusion:** subject unity is trivial by definition, while phenomenal unity
is substantive and may constrain theories of consciousness.  
**Establishes:** physical integration cannot silently substitute for subject or
phenomenal unity.  
**Does not establish:** the subject's ontology.  
**IER relevance:** identifies the bridge principle IER still owes.  
**Confidence:** high.

### B5. Counting and overlap: Schwitzgebel–Nelson and Roelofs

**Citations:** Eric Schwitzgebel and Sophie R. Nelson, “When Counting Conscious
Subjects, the Result Needn't Always Be a Determinate Whole Number,”
*Philosophical Psychology* 39 (2026), 847–867; Luke Roelofs, “Overlapping Minds
and the Hedonic Calculus,” *Philosophical Studies* (2024).  
**Identifiers:** [counting DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/09515089.2025.2520364);
[overlap DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-024-02167-x).  
**Access:** full author/open text.  
**Level:** B.  
**Question:** Must material conscious subjects be discrete nonoverlapping
wholes?  
**Candidate:** naturalistic/material subjects at overlapping scales.  
**Physical relatum:** complex, overlapping, incompletely integrated systems.  
**Relation:** grounding or candidate identity, not a settled ontology.  
**Conclusion:** common naturalistic premises do not guarantee determinate
whole-number subjects; many physical candidates can overlap and share states.  
**Establishes:** maximality, discreteness, and categorical counting need
argument.  
**Does not establish:** that subjects actually are fractional or that every
process theory fails.  
**IER relevance:** principal stress test for the one-UEF/one-subject rule.  
**Confidence:** high.

## Exclusions and recurrent false friends

| Position/search result | Reason excluded or downgraded |
| --- | --- |
| Place/Smart identity theory | Identifies experiences or sensations with brain processes, not the diachronically continuing subject |
| Integrated Information Theory | Its central identity claim concerns experience and cause-effect structure; subject ontology and diachronic identity require further premises |
| Global workspace theories | A workspace broadcasts or makes content accessible; realization/function is not subject identity |
| Generic dynamical consciousness theories | Ongoing dynamics can cause, support, correlate with, or realize consciousness without being the subject |
| Self-model theories | A represented owner or model is not automatically the experiencer, and representation is not identity |
| Thompson-style self-process view | The self is enacted/experiential and processual, but self, subject, and a particular physical process are not explicitly equated |
| Organism-as-process accounts | Establish processual organisms, not that the organism-process is the experiential subject |
| “Process Identity Theory” web taxonomies | Unverified popular taxonomy/search lead; no qualifying peer-reviewed primary source located |
| Panpsychist combination literature | Often discusses how subjects combine or experience relates to process-stuff, but usually does not provide the target continuing physical-process identity |
| The project's own IER paper returned in search | Circular evidence; excluded from the precedent corpus |

- Owen 2024: full text unavailable; do not attribute a detailed positive
  ontology beyond the bearer problem confirmed in the accessible 2025 paper.
- Strawson's *Selves* (2009) and *The Subject of Experience* (2017): citation
  chains confirm continuity with the 2003 thin-subject view, but a full
  book-length comparison remains advisable before publication.

  later primary summaries; full book comparison would improve the treatment of
