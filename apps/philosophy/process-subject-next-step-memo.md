# Process-Subject Next-Step Memo

## Decision

**Recommended primary next product: write a constructive process-subject
paper.**

Do not lead with a novelty claim that no one has identified the experiential
subject with a physical process. Galen Strawson did so explicitly in 2003 for
the thin, occurrent subject. Do not make Matthew Owen the paper's principal
target either. Owen identifies the neglected-bearer problem, but the strongest
philosophical contribution is a constructive account of the identity and
persistence of a physical process-subject.

## Exact contribution

The defensible contribution is narrower and stronger:

> Develop a physicalist identity theory on which one experiential subject is
> one temporally continuous physical process, and provide identity conditions
> that allow changes of content, organization, and material participants while
> treating genuine cessation as the end of that subject.

This would occupy a position not covered by the strongest located alternatives:

- Unlike Place and Smart, it identifies the subject, not only conscious states,
  with a physical process.
- Unlike Strawson's 2003 thin-subject account, it does not make every relevant
  content or micro-part change the death of one subject and birth of another.
- Unlike Dainton's capacity account, it identifies the subject with current
  process operation and denies numerical survival across a genuinely
  experience-null interval.
- Unlike Taylor and Steward, it does not posit an enduring bearer distinct
  from the experience/process.

The core paper claim should therefore be called a **continuing
process-subject identity theory**, with “continuing” carrying the main burden.

## Paper-decision gate

Draft the paper only after a two- to four-page technical note can satisfy all
four conditions below:

1. State the identity unambiguously as
   `experiential subject = the concrete continuing physical process P`, not
   “P constitutes a subject.”
2. Give nonexperiential conditions for individuating P against wider, narrower,
   nested, and overlapping candidates.
3. Give prospective persistence rules for content change, participant turnover,
   severe disruption, fission, fusion, and genuine cessation.
4. Explain why numerical identity is preferable to constitution, realization,
   or causal dependence rather than merely compatible with the data.

If that note cannot be written without circularity or stipulated maximality,
the next product should revert to targeted research on process boundaries
rather than a full paper.

## Provisional outline

### 1. The neglected question

- Separate state identity, subject ontology, synchronic unity, and diachronic
  identity.
- Use Place and Smart to introduce identity theory.
- Use Owen to show that a state theory can leave the bearer unidentified.

### 2. The exact precedent

- Reconstruct Strawson's thin subject and
  subject–experience–content–process identity.
- Credit it as a genuine physical process-subject theory.
- Isolate its episode-scale, strict-part, and content-dependent identity
  conditions.

### 3. Three rival bearer models

- Taylor: the subject as the substance constituting an experience-event.
- Steward/Owen: the enduring animal or hylomorphic bearer.
- Dainton: the subject as a persisting capacity for unified experience.

### 4. The continuing-process proposal

- Define the physical process independently of consciousness vocabulary.
- Specify spatiotemporal profile, participant structure, dynamic composition,
  dynamic shape, and context using Seibt.
- Add causal-organizational continuity and genidentity using
  Nicholson/Pradeu.
- State the identity, unity, and boundary theses separately.

### 5. Persistence and cessation

- Content change and gradual participant turnover.
- Dreaming and severe but continuous reconfiguration.
- Dreamless sleep, anesthesia, coma, and genuine null intervals.
- Explain why capacity and embodied-system continuity do not equal
  experiential-subject identity.

### 6. Fission, fusion, and overlap

- Work one split/fission case and one overlap case.
- Address too-many-minds and indeterminate-counting objections.
- State when one process terminates, two begin, or a proposed fusion fails.

### 7. Why identity?

- Adapt Place's same-process versus correlated-process strategy.
- Compare explanatory consequences of identity, constitution, realization,
  and grounding.
- Admit that structural fit alone does not prove identity.

### 8. Consequences and limits

- Ordinary self/person continuity may diverge from experiential-subject
  continuity.
- No diagnostic claim about actual systems follows.
- State the bounded originality: not process-subject identity simpliciter, but
  its continuing, turnover-tolerant, no-gap form.

## Research required before drafting

1. Read Strawson's *Selves* (2009) and *The Subject of Experience* (2017) in
   full to verify whether later formulations alter the 2003 persistence
   criterion.
2. Read Dainton's *The Phenomenal Self* (2008) and the complete 2016 exchange
   to sharpen the capacity rival and unconsciousness case.
3. Verify O'Conaill's 2020 treatment of subject and experience identity; the
   present session located it as a relevant criticism but did not obtain a full
   primary text.
4. Develop one formal or semi-formal process-individuation schema drawing on
   Seibt, DiFrisco, and Pradeu.
5. Test that schema against overlapping-material-object and indeterminate-count
   cases from Roelofs and Schwitzgebel–Nelson.

These are publication-quality refinements, not blockers to the present bounded
finding.

## IER terms or claims needing later review

No canonical changes are made here. A later authorized review should examine:

- **“Constitutes one experiential subject.”** This is weaker than numerical
  identity in the literature's standard relation vocabulary. If IER intends
  the UEF operation to be the subject, say so directly and consistently.
- **“Exactly one subject per UEF.”** Clarify whether this is definitional,
  derived from physical non-factorization, or an independent axiom.
- **“Maximal set.”** State the comparison class and show that maximality does
  not depend on an observer-selected candidate boundary.
- **Categorical subjecthood.** Address fractional or indeterminate subject
  counts rather than assuming whole-number discreteness.
- **Continuity.** Convert “successive states remain dynamically dependent” into
  identity conditions precise enough to distinguish persistence, failure,
  fission, and cessation.
- **Ownership.** Explain whether “the regime bears constraint” is purely a
  physical relation or covertly imports the bearer conclusion.
- **Identity argument.** Keep the physical specification of a candidate regime
  separate from the argument that the regime is experience and subject.

## Bottom line

The planned research has passed the paper-decision gate in a provisional sense:
there is a real contribution to formulate, but its originality is diachronic
and criterial, not the bare idea of a physical process-subject. The first
writing task should be the technical identity-and-persistence note described
above; the full paper should follow only if that note survives the overlap and
fission tests.

No canonical IER source has been modified.
